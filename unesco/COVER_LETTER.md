## The Urgent Need

As UNESCO has long recognized, braille is not merely a convenience—it is a fundamental human right. The UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities explicitly cites braille as essential for:
- Freedom of expression and opinion (Article 21)
- Access to education (Article 24)
- Full participation in society

Yet today, a profound inequality exists:

- **7,000+ languages** spoken by humanity
- **Only ~130 languages** have braille systems
- **98% of languages** have no braille
- **30 million blind people** cannot read in their native language

In Africa alone, **31 of 54 countries have NO braille access**—affecting over 230 million people across just five countries (Tanzania, Sudan, Iraq, Afghanistan, Angola).

This is not a technical problem. **This is a human rights crisis that demands an urgent solution.**

---

## Why Traditional Approaches Have Failed

UNESCO pioneered efforts to standardize braille as early as 1949, and has published multiple editions of "World Braille Usage." Yet despite 75 years of effort, we have braille for only 130 languages.

**Why so few?** Because traditional braille development:
- Requires **5-10 years** per language
- Costs **$50,000-$500,000** per language
- Needs specialized linguistic expertise
- Demands sustained institutional support

At the current pace (0.74 languages per year), covering all 7,000 languages would take **9,460 years**.

**We cannot wait 9,460 years.**

Indigenous languages, minority languages, and languages spoken in economically disadvantaged regions are systematically excluded—not because they are less important, but because they are not commercially viable.

**This inequality is morally unacceptable.**

## The Solution: WIA Braille

WIA Braille is the world's first **IPA-based universal braille system** that provides automatic support for all documented human languages.

### How It Works

The insight is simple:
1. Every human language can be represented in IPA (International Phonetic Alphabet)
2. We map all IPA phonemes (135+) to 8-dot braille patterns
3. Therefore, any language automatically has braille support

**No custom development needed. No waiting. No cost.**

### What Makes It Different

| Traditional Approach | WIA Braille |
|---------------------|-------------|
| 130 languages (2%) | 7,000+ languages (100%) |
| 5-10 years per language | Immediate support |
| $50K-$500K per language | $0 (Free, MIT License) |
| Excludes minority languages | All languages equal |

### Technical Foundation

- **8-dot braille** (256 patterns) - sufficient for all IPA phonemes
- **Compatible with liblouis** - integrates with existing infrastructure
- **Screen reader support** - works with NVDA, JAWS
- **Validated**: Tested with 50+ languages across all major language families
- **Production ready**: Already integrated into WIA PubScript 1.0

### Philosophical Foundation

We **deliberately chose NOT to patent** this technology, despite its commercial potential.

**Why?** Because:
> "Technology should not be owned. Language, even more so."

This system is South Korea's gift to the world, offered freely in the spirit of **홍익인간** - the same philosophy that inspired King Sejong the Great to create Hangul for the common people 600 years ago.
