## The Ancient Promise

Four thousand years ago, in the land that would become Korea, a philosophy was born:

**弘益人間** *(Hongik Ingan)*

- 弘 (弘) - *To spread widely, to expand*
- 益 (益) - *To benefit, to help*
- 人 (人) - *Human beings*
- 間 (間) - *The world, among people*

**"To widely benefit all humanity"**

This was not merely an ideal. It was a sacred duty—a covenant between those with ability and those in need.

Six centuries ago, this philosophy found new expression when King Sejong the Great created Hangul, the Korean alphabet. He wrote:

> *"The people are ignorant and cannot express their thoughts. I feel sorry for them, and I have created these 28 letters so that every person may easily learn and use them in their daily lives."*

Today, we honor this legacy.

## The Injustice We Cannot Accept

### The Numbers

- **7,000+ languages** spoken by humanity
- **Only ~130** have braille systems
- **6,870+ languages** without braille
- **31 African countries** with no braille access
- **Millions of indigenous peoples** excluded

### The Reality

A blind child born in England learns braille.
A blind child born in a village in Africa, speaking their native language, cannot.

**This is not acceptable.**

Not because it's inefficient.
Not because it's wasteful.
But because **it is morally wrong**.

### The Root Cause

Why don't most languages have braille?

**Because traditional braille development is:**
1. **Expensive** - Requires years of expert work
2. **Complex** - Each language needs custom development
3. **Political** - Requires institutional support
4. **Commercial** - No profit in serving small languages

So indigenous languages are ignored.
Minority languages are forgotten.
"Uncommercial" languages are abandoned.

**This is the opposite of 홍익인간.**

## The Technical Solution: Universal Design

WIA Braille is not magic. It's simply good engineering guided by moral clarity.

### The Insight

**Every human language can be represented in IPA** (International Phonetic Alphabet)

Therefore:
- If we map IPA phonemes to braille patterns
- Then we automatically support all languages
- No custom development needed
- No language left behind

### The Implementation

1. **8-dot braille** (256 combinations) instead of 6-dot (64)
2. **Systematic IPA mapping** for all phonemes
3. **Open source** for universal access
4. **liblouis integration** for existing tools

Simple. Elegant. Universal.

## The Korean Heritage

### From Sejong to WIA Braille

King Sejong faced a similar problem in 1443:
- Chinese characters were too complex for common people
- Only the educated elite could read and write
- The people were voiceless

His solution:
- Created Hangul - scientifically designed, easy to learn
- Made it free for all people
- Enabled universal literacy

**We follow in his footsteps.**

### From Korea to the World

South Korea knows what it means to rise from poverty to prosperity through education. We know the transformative power of literacy. We know what it means to be underestimated and to prove the world wrong.

Now we have the ability to help.

**홍익인간** demands that we do so.

### The Revolutionary Concept: "Learn Once, Read All"

King Sejong's greatest insight was not just creating a writing system—it was creating a system so simple that:

> *"A wise person can learn it in one morning,*
> *Even a foolish person can learn it in ten days."*

**The revolution was accessibility**: Learn Hangul once, and you can READ Korean (even if you don't understand it yet).

**WIA Braille follows the same principle**:
- ✅ Learn WIA Braille once → Read all 7,000+ languages in braille
- ✅ Can pronounce them (via IPA)
- ✅ Reading ability comes first, opening the door to learning

**Understanding meaning?** That requires learning the language itself—but the door is now open.

**Comparison**:

| Aspect | Traditional Braille | WIA Braille |
|--------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Learning** | Must learn each language separately | **Learn once, read all** |
| After learning English Braille | Can read English only | - |
| After learning WIA Braille | - | **Can read 7,000+ languages** |

This is not just a technical improvement—it's a **paradigm shift** in how we think about braille.
