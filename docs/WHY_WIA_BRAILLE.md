## 2. The Braille Literacy Crisis

### 2.1 Shocking Reality

**Question**: What percentage of blind people can read braille?

**Common Answer**: 10% (frequently cited in literature)

**Truth**: **This statistic is from the 1970s and is outdated.**

More recent data shows:
- **58%** of surveyed blind adults read braille (2011 survey, US)
- But **less than 20%** of blind schoolchildren learn braille today (down from 50% in 1980s)

**Sources**:
- [Braille Literacy Statistics](https://brailleworks.com/braille-literacy-statistics/)
- [How Many Braille Readers?](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0145482X211071125)

### 2.2 The Employment Connection

**Critical Statistic**:

> **90% of employed blind people are braille literate.**

This means:
- Braille literacy ≈ Employment opportunity
- No braille = Economic disadvantage
- Language access = Economic justice

**Source**: [National Federation of the Blind](https://nfb.org/images/nfb/documents/pdf/braille_literacy_report_web.pdf)

### 2.3 The Decline

**Braille Literacy Rates Over Time** (US, school-age):
- 1960s: **50%+** of blind students learned braille
- 1980s: **40-50%**
- 2000s: **30%**
- 2024: **<20%**

**Why the decline?**
1. Over-reliance on audio (screen readers, audiobooks)
2. Mainstreaming without braille instruction
3. **Lack of braille materials in minority languages**

## 3. The Language Inequality

### 3.1 The Numbers

- **7,000+ languages** spoken by humanity
- **~130 languages** have braille systems
- **6,870+ languages** have NO braille

**Percentage**: Less than **2%** of languages have braille.

**Sources**:
- [Ethnologue](https://www.ethnologue.com/) (7,168 living languages)
- [World Braille Usage](https://www.perkins.org/resource/world-braille-usage/)

### 3.2 Regional Inequality

#### Africa: The Most Underserved Continent

- **54 countries** in Africa
- **31 countries** (57%) have **NO braille access**
- **Largest affected populations**:
  - Tanzania: 65 million people
  - Sudan: 46 million
  - Iraq: 44 million
  - Afghanistan: 41 million
  - Angola: 35 million

**Total**: Over **230 million people** in just these 5 countries alone.

**Source**: [World Braille Usage - Africa](https://provisiodigital.com/seeing-and-reading-without-borders-braille-alphabets-embraced-by-nations-globally/)

#### Indigenous Languages: Systematically Excluded

**Problem**:
- Thousands of indigenous languages worldwide
- Almost NONE have braille codes
- Examples:
  - **North America**: Most Native American languages
  - **South America**: Hundreds of Amazonian languages
  - **Asia**: Minority languages in China, India, SE Asia
  - **Pacific**: Hundreds of Austronesian languages
  - **Africa**: Thousands of Niger-Congo languages

**Why excluded?**
1. **Not Commercially Viable**: Small speaker populations
2. **Complex Development**: Traditional braille needs custom codes
3. **Lack of Resources**: No funding for minority languages
4. **Political Marginalization**: Governments prioritize official languages

**Source**: [MIUSA Braille Overview](https://miusa.org/resource/tip-sheets/braille/)

### 3.3 Real-World Impact

**Example: A blind Hmong child**

- **Language**: Hmong (Southeast Asia)
- **Speakers**: 4 million people
- **Braille system**: None (until recently, limited)
- **Result**: Cannot read in their native language
- **Impact**: Educational disadvantage, cultural disconnection

**Multiply this by thousands of languages.**

## 5. Why Traditional Approaches Failed

### 5.1 The Traditional Braille Development Process

**Steps**:
1. Linguistic analysis (6-12 months)
2. Phoneme inventory (3-6 months)
3. Braille code design (6-12 months)
4. Expert review and testing (6-12 months)
5. Standardization (1-2 years)
6. Implementation and training (2-5 years)

**Total**: **5-10 years per language**
**Cost**: $50,000 - $500,000 per language
**Result**: Only profitable for major languages

### 5.2 The Math Doesn't Work

At current pace:
- **130 languages** have braille after **175 years** (since Louis Braille)
- Average: **0.74 languages per year**
- To cover all 7,000 languages: **9,460 years**

**We don't have 9,460 years.**

### 5.3 The Commercial Barrier

**Harsh Truth**: Braille development is market-driven.

Languages get braille if:
- Large speaker population (= market size)
- Economic resources (= ability to pay)
- Political influence (= government support)

Languages DON'T get braille if:
- Small population
- Poor communities
- Politically marginalized

**This is morally unacceptable.**
