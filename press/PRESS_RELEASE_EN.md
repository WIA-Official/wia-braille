## Korean Company Releases World's First Universal Braille System for 7,000+ Languages

### Free, Open Source, Patent-Free: "A Gift to Humanity" — Submitted to UNESCO

**SEOUL, South Korea (December 8, 2025)** - SmileStory Inc. (CEO: Dr. Sam Heum Yeon) today announced the release of WIA Braille, the world's first universal braille system supporting all 7,000+ human languages, made freely available to the world without patents.

Currently, only 130 of the world's 7,000 languages (less than 2%) have braille systems, leaving over 30 million blind people unable to read in their native language. WIA Braille is an innovative system based on the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) that enables reading braille in any language after learning it once.

### Solving a 9,460-Year Problem Today

Over the past 175 years (1849-2024), braille has been developed for an average of 0.74 languages per year. At this pace, it would take **9,460 years** to support all 7,000 languages.

"We cannot wait 9,460 years," said Dr. Yeon. "Technology should not be owned. Language, even more so. This is South Korea's gift to the world."

### Core Technical Innovation

**How WIA Braille Works:**
1. Any language → IPA (International Phonetic Alphabet) conversion
2. IPA phonemes → Automatic 8-dot braille mapping
3. Instant braille generation (0 development time)

**Traditional Approach:**
- 5-10 years development per language
- $50K-$500K cost per language
- Minority languages permanently excluded

**WIA Braille:**
- All languages supported immediately
- Cost: $0 (completely free)
- All languages equal
