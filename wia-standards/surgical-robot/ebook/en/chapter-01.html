[MED-006] Chapter 1: Introduction to Surgical Robotics

弘益人間 (Hongik Ingan)

"Benefit All Humanity"

The WIA Surgical Robot Standard is based on the philosophy that robotic technology should enhance surgeon capabilities, improve patient outcomes, and expand access to healthcare.

1.1 What is a Surgical Robot?

A Surgical Robot is a highly precise robotic system designed to assist or perform surgical procedures. These systems translate a surgeon's hand movements into smaller, more precise movements of robotic arms, enabling minimally invasive surgery.

1.2 History of Surgical Robots

Key milestones in surgical robotics development include PUMA 560 (1985), AESOP (1994), ZEUS (1997), and the da Vinci system (1999) which became the gold standard for robotic surgery.

1.3 Clinical Benefits

For Patients: Smaller incisions, reduced bleeding, less pain, faster recovery, minimal scarring

For Surgeons: Enhanced 3D visualization (10-15x magnification), precise control with motion scaling (1:3 ratio), tremor filtering (>90% reduction), 7-DOF instruments exceeding human wrist capabilities

1.4 Technical Challenges

Current limitations include lack of haptic feedback, high costs ($1.5-2.5M initial, $150-200k annual maintenance), significant setup time (20-30 minutes), and steep learning curve (20-40 cases for proficiency).

1.5 WIA-MED-006 Standard Solution

The WIA-MED-006 standard addresses market monopolization through:

  • Open architecture enabling component interoperability
  • Standardized interfaces (control, vision, tools)
  • Rigorous safety requirements
  • Comprehensive certification programs

Based on the Hongik Ingan philosophy, this standard is永free, open, and accessible to all without patents or licensing fees, sustained through certification services.
