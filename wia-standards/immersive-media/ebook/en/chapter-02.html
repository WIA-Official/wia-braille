Chapter 2: 360° Video Production

Understanding 360° Video

360° video, also called spherical or immersive video, captures every direction simultaneously from a single point. Unlike traditional video that shows a rectangular frame, 360° video records the entire environment, allowing viewers to look in any direction during playback.

For education, this technology enables virtual field trips, immersive documentaries, and recorded experiences that students can explore from multiple perspectives. A teacher can record a museum tour, science experiment, or cultural event once, and students can view it repeatedly from any angle.

Camera Systems and Capture

360° Camera Types

  • Consumer 360° Cameras: All-in-one devices like Insta360 X3, GoPro MAX ($300-600) - Great for classroom use and simple field trips
  • Professional 360° Cameras: Insta360 Pro 2, Kandao Obsidian ($3,000-15,000) - High-resolution captures for institutional content
  • Custom Rigs: Multiple action cameras arranged in spherical arrays - Maximum flexibility and quality for specialized productions

Resolution Considerations

Resolution Quality Use Case
4K (3840×2160) Basic Simple classroom tours, low-motion scenes
5.7K (5760×2880) Good Most educational content, virtual field trips
8K (7680×4320) Excellent Professional educational content, archival
11K+ (11520×5760) Exceptional High-detail subject matter, close inspection
Why Higher Resolution Matters: In 360° video, the viewer typically sees only a portion of the total sphere at once (their field of view). A 4K 360° video effectively shows about 1K quality when viewing a normal field of view, while an 8K video shows approximately 2K. Higher resolution is essential for clarity.

Shooting Best Practices

Camera Placement

  • Height: Position cameras at average human eye height (5-6 feet) for natural perspective
  • Stability: Use sturdy tripods or specialized mounts - any movement is magnified in VR
  • Distance: Keep subjects 6+ feet from camera to avoid distortion
  • Blind Spots: Be aware that the tripod and nearby objects will appear in the final video

Lighting Considerations

360° cameras capture everything, including light sources. Avoid harsh contrasts, direct sunlight pointing at lenses, and uneven lighting across the sphere. Natural, diffused lighting works best. When using artificial lights, place them outside the camera's immediate vicinity.

Audio Capture

Most 360° cameras include built-in microphones, but external spatial audio recorders provide superior results. Ambisonic microphones like the Zoom H3-VR capture 3D audio that matches the 360° video, creating fully immersive soundscapes.

Stitching and Post-Production

Stitching Process

360° cameras use multiple lenses to capture overlapping views that must be "stitched" into a seamless sphere. Modern cameras often auto-stitch, but manual refinement improves quality:

  • Remove tripod and equipment using masking tools
  • Adjust stitch lines to minimize visible seams
  • Correct exposure differences between lenses
  • Stabilize footage to reduce motion sickness

Editing Software

  • Adobe Premiere Pro: Industry standard with VR/360° workflow support
  • Final Cut Pro X: Apple's editor with 360° plugins
  • Insta360 Studio: Free software optimized for Insta360 cameras
  • Mistika VR: Specialized 360° editing and stitching

Adding Interactive Elements

Enhance educational 360° videos with interactive hotspots, quizzes, and information overlays. Tools like ThingLink and H5P allow educators to add clickable elements that provide context, questions, or navigation to specific moments.

Projection Formats

Equirectangular Projection

The most common format for 360° video. The spherical capture is unwrapped into a rectangular image with 2:1 aspect ratio (width:height). The poles are stretched, similar to how world maps distort Antarctica and the Arctic.

Cubemap Projection

The sphere is projected onto six square faces (front, back, left, right, top, bottom). This format reduces distortion but requires more complex playback handling. Often used for real-time rendering in game engines.

Stereoscopic 3D

Recording separate views for each eye creates depth perception. Stereoscopic 360° video requires twice the file size but provides more immersive experiences. Label format as "360 3D" or "180 3D stereoscopic."

Delivery and Playback

File Formats and Codecs

  • H.264: Universal compatibility, good compression, widely supported
  • H.265 (HEVC): Better compression, smaller files, requires newer devices
  • VP9: Open-source, used by YouTube, good quality-to-size ratio

Platform Requirements

  • YouTube: Supports up to 8K 360°, automatic VR detection, easy embedding
  • Facebook: 360° video in feed and dedicated VR app
  • LMS Integration: Embed players in learning management systems
  • VR Headsets: Native apps for Oculus, Vive, PlayStation VR

Bandwidth Considerations

High-resolution 360° video requires significant bandwidth. Provide multiple quality options:

  • 4K: ~25 Mbps - Suitable for standard internet connections
  • 5.7K: ~50 Mbps - Good for institutional networks
  • 8K: ~100 Mbps - Requires high-speed connections

Educational Use Cases

Virtual Field Trips

Record tours of museums, historical sites, natural environments, and facilities students cannot easily visit. Include narration explaining what students should observe, with interactive elements highlighting key features.

Laboratory Demonstrations

Capture science experiments and procedures from optimal viewing positions. Students can review dangerous or resource-intensive experiments safely and repeatedly.

Cultural Immersion

Document cultural events, ceremonies, and daily life in different communities. Provide context through spatial audio (ambient sounds, conversations, music) and visual annotations.

Skills Training

Record expert demonstrations of techniques - surgical procedures, manufacturing processes, athletic performances - allowing students to observe from the practitioner's perspective.

Quality Checklist

  • ✓ Stable camera placement with minimal movement
  • ✓ Consistent, well-balanced lighting across the sphere
  • ✓ High-quality spatial or ambisonic audio
  • ✓ Smooth stitching with no visible seam lines
  • ✓ Tripod and equipment removed or minimized
  • ✓ Proper metadata for VR detection
  • ✓ Stabilization applied to reduce motion
  • ✓ Color grading for consistent tone
  • ✓ Interactive elements tested on target platforms
  • ✓ Multiple resolution versions for different bandwidths
