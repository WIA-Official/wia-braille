Chapter 4

Dimensional Modeling (Kimball & Inmon)

4.1 Introduction to Dimensional Modeling

Dimensional modeling is a design technique for structuring data warehouses to support business intelligence and reporting. Two major methodologies dominate: Kimball's bottom-up approach and Inmon's top-down approach.

4.2 Kimball Methodology (Bottom-Up)

Ralph Kimball's approach emphasizes building dimensional models incrementally, starting with individual data marts that are later integrated.

Key Principles

  • Business process focus: Build around key business events
  • Dimensional modeling: Use star schemas throughout
  • Conformed dimensions: Shared dimensions across data marts
  • Incremental development: Deliver value quickly

Kimball's Four-Step Design Process

  1. Select the business process: Identify what to measure
  2. Declare the grain: Define the level of detail
  3. Identify dimensions: Determine context and attributes
  4. Identify facts: Determine measurable metrics

4.3 Inmon Methodology (Top-Down)

Bill Inmon advocates building a centralized, normalized enterprise data warehouse first, then creating dimensional data marts from it.

Key Principles

  • Enterprise-wide view: Single source of truth
  • Normalized data: 3NF in the EDW
  • Subject-oriented: Organized by business subjects
  • Top-down approach: Build foundation first

4.4 Kimball vs Inmon Comparison

Aspect Kimball Inmon
Approach Bottom-up Top-down
Schema Dimensional (Star) Normalized (3NF)
Development Incremental Big bang
Time to value Faster Slower
Flexibility Limited High

4.5 Hybrid Approaches

Many organizations adopt hybrid approaches, combining the best of both methodologies. Common patterns include starting with Kimball's quick wins while building toward Inmon's enterprise vision.

🎯 Choosing the Right Methodology

  • Choose Kimball if: You need quick wins, have limited resources, focus on BI/reporting
  • Choose Inmon if: You need enterprise-wide consistency, have complex data relationships, plan long-term scalability
  • Choose Hybrid if: You want balanced approach, need both agility and consistency
