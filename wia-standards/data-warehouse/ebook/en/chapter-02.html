Chapter 2

OLAP vs OLTP Systems

2.1 Understanding OLTP (Online Transaction Processing)

OLTP systems are designed to handle high volumes of short, atomic transactions. They power operational systems like e-commerce platforms, banking applications, and inventory management systems.

OLTP Characteristics

  • Fast response times: Millisecond-level queries
  • High concurrency: Thousands of simultaneous users
  • Normalized schemas: Third normal form (3NF) to reduce redundancy
  • ACID compliance: Atomicity, Consistency, Isolation, Durability
  • Write-heavy: Frequent INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE operations

2.2 Understanding OLAP (Online Analytical Processing)

OLAP systems are optimized for complex analytical queries that aggregate and analyze large datasets. They support business intelligence, reporting, and data mining applications.

OLAP Characteristics

  • Complex queries: Aggregations, joins, calculations
  • Historical data: Years of data for trend analysis
  • Denormalized schemas: Star and snowflake schemas
  • Read-heavy: Mostly SELECT queries
  • Batch updates: Periodic ETL loads

2.3 Key Differences

Aspect OLTP OLAP
Purpose Daily operations Analysis and reporting
Data Volume GB to TB TB to PB
Query Type Simple, fast Complex, analytical
Schema Normalized (3NF) Denormalized (Star/Snowflake)
Updates Real-time Batch (ETL)

2.4 When to Use Each

🎯 Use OLTP When

  • Processing customer orders and payments
  • Managing inventory in real-time
  • Handling banking transactions
  • Recording sensor data from IoT devices

📊 Use OLAP When

  • Analyzing sales trends over time
  • Creating executive dashboards
  • Performing predictive analytics
  • Generating financial reports

2.5 Hybrid Transactional/Analytical Processing (HTAP)

Modern systems increasingly blur the line between OLTP and OLAP. HTAP systems like SAP HANA and MemSQL enable real-time analytics on transactional data without ETL delays.
