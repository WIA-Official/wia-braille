Chapter 1: Introduction to Sleep Monitoring

"Sleep is not merely rest—it's when the brain cleans itself, memories consolidate, the immune system recharges, and hormones rebalance. Good sleep is the foundation of health."

1.1 Why Sleep Matters

Sleep is essential for health, comprising ~1/3 of our lives. During sleep, critical processes occur: brain cleaning, memory consolidation, immune regulation, and hormone balance.

Sleep Deprivation Consequences: Cognitive decline, metabolic dysfunction (diabetes, obesity), cardiovascular disease, weakened immunity, mental health issues (depression, anxiety), and increased accident risk.

Global Sleep Crisis: ~700 million people suffer from sleep disorders worldwide, 80-90% undiagnosed. Economic cost: $411 billion annually in the US alone from lost productivity.

1.2 Sleep Monitoring Technology Evolution

  • 1920s: EEG invented (Hans Berger)
  • 1950s: REM sleep discovered
  • 1960s: Polysomnography (PSG) developed
  • 1970s: R&K sleep staging
  • 1980s: Actigraphy introduced
  • 2010s: Consumer wearables (Fitbit, Apple Watch)
  • 2020s: AI-powered sleep analysis
