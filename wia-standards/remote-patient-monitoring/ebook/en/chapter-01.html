Chapter 1

Introduction to Remote Patient Monitoring

Remote Patient Monitoring (RPM) is transforming healthcare delivery by enabling continuous care outside traditional clinical settings. This chapter explores the fundamental concepts, historical evolution, and critical role of RPM in modern healthcare systems.

What is Remote Patient Monitoring?

RPM uses digital technologies to collect health data from patients in one location and electronically transmit that information to healthcare providers in a different location for assessment and recommendations. It represents a shift from episodic, reactive care to continuous, proactive health management.

Key Benefits

  • Improved Patient Outcomes: 38% reduction in hospital readmissions for heart failure patients
  • Cost Reduction: Average savings of $2,650 per patient per year
  • Enhanced Access: Care delivery regardless of geographic location
  • Patient Empowerment: Real-time health data promotes engagement

Core Components

RPM systems consist of six essential elements: data collection devices (sensors, wearables), data transmission systems (Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, cellular), secure data storage (HIPAA-compliant cloud), analytics engines (AI/ML algorithms), user interfaces (patient and clinician dashboards), and alert systems (real-time notifications).

Global Trends

The global RPM market is projected to grow from $23 billion in 2020 to $117.5 billion by 2028. COVID-19 accelerated adoption, with regulatory relaxations and expanded insurance coverage driving growth.

弘益人間 Perspective

RPM embodies the principle of 弘益人間 (Benefit All Humanity) by democratizing access to quality healthcare. It removes geographical, economic, and physical barriers, ensuring that everyone can receive appropriate medical care when needed.
