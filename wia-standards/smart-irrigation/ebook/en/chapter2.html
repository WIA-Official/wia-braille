💧 Chapter 2: Evapotranspiration Fundamentals

Understanding Water Loss and Crop Water Requirements

2.1 What is Evapotranspiration?

Evapotranspiration (ET) is the combined process of water evaporation from soil and plant surfaces, plus transpiration through plant leaves. It represents the water consumed by the crop-soil system and is the foundation of irrigation scheduling.

Components of ET:

  • Evaporation: Direct water loss from soil, mulch, and wet plant surfaces
  • Transpiration: Water absorbed by roots and released through leaf stomata
  • Interception: Water captured by plant canopy and re-evaporated

2.2 Reference Evapotranspiration (ETo)

Reference ET (ETo) represents the water loss from a hypothetical reference crop (grass or alfalfa) with uniform height, complete ground cover, and adequate water. It's calculated from weather data and serves as a baseline for determining crop-specific water needs.

Factors Affecting ETo:

  1. Solar Radiation: Primary energy source for evaporation
  2. Air Temperature: Affects saturation vapor pressure
  3. Humidity: Lower humidity increases vapor pressure deficit
  4. Wind Speed: Removes saturated air from leaf surfaces

2.3 Penman-Monteith Equation

The FAO-56 Penman-Monteith equation is the international standard for calculating reference ET:

ETo = (0.408·Δ·(Rn - G) + γ·(900/(T+273))·u₂·(es - ea)) / (Δ + γ·(1 + 0.34·u₂)) Where: ETo = reference evapotranspiration (mm/day) Rn = net radiation at crop surface (MJ/m²/day) G = soil heat flux density (MJ/m²/day) T = mean daily air temperature at 2m height (°C) u₂ = wind speed at 2m height (m/s) es = saturation vapor pressure (kPa) ea = actual vapor pressure (kPa) Δ = slope of vapor pressure curve (kPa/°C) γ = psychrometric constant (kPa/°C)

Simplified Calculation Steps:

  1. Calculate mean temperature: T = (Tmax + Tmin) / 2
  2. Calculate vapor pressure deficit: VPD = es - ea
  3. Estimate net radiation from solar radiation and temperature
  4. Apply Penman-Monteith formula
  5. Result: Daily ETo in millimeters

2.4 Crop Coefficients (Kc)

Different crops have different water requirements. The crop coefficient (Kc) adjusts reference ET to specific crops:

ETc = ETo × Kc Where: ETc = crop evapotranspiration (mm/day) ETo = reference evapotranspiration (mm/day) Kc = crop coefficient (dimensionless)

Kc Values by Growth Stage:

Example: Tomato Crop Coefficients
  • Initial stage (planting to 10% cover): Kc = 0.60
  • Development stage (10% to 70% cover): Kc = 0.60 to 1.15
  • Mid-season (70% cover to maturity): Kc = 1.15
  • Late season (maturity to harvest): Kc = 0.70 to 0.90

Common Crop Coefficients (Mid-Season):

  • Lettuce: 0.95 - 1.00
  • Tomato: 1.10 - 1.15
  • Corn: 1.15 - 1.20
  • Wheat: 1.05 - 1.10
  • Cotton: 0.95 - 1.00
  • Citrus: 0.65 - 0.75
  • Grapes: 0.70 - 0.85

2.5 Alternative ET Calculation Methods

Hargreaves Equation (Simplified)

When full weather data is unavailable, the Hargreaves equation uses only temperature:

ETo = 0.0023 × (Tmean + 17.8) × (Tmax - Tmin)^0.5 × Ra Where: Tmean = mean temperature (°C) Tmax, Tmin = max and min temperature (°C) Ra = extraterrestrial radiation (MJ/m²/day)

Pan Evaporation Method

Uses Class A evaporation pan measurements:

ETo = Kpan × Epan Where: Kpan = pan coefficient (typically 0.70-0.85) Epan = measured pan evaporation (mm/day)

2.6 Practical ET Measurement

Weather Station Requirements:

  • Temperature sensors: Accuracy ±0.5°C
  • Humidity sensor: Accuracy ±5%
  • Anemometer: Wind speed ±0.5 m/s
  • Pyranometer: Solar radiation ±5%
  • Rain gauge: Accuracy ±2%

Station Siting Guidelines:

  • Over irrigated grass or reference crop
  • Minimum 2 meters height for sensors
  • Away from buildings and trees (distance > 2× object height)
  • Level ground with good air circulation

2.7 Using ET for Irrigation Scheduling

Water Balance Approach:

Irrigation Needed = ETc - Effective Rainfall - Soil Moisture Change Daily water balance: SMt = SMt-1 + Rain + Irrigation - ETc - Drainage - Runoff Where: SMt = soil moisture at time t

Irrigation Trigger:

Irrigate when accumulated ET deficit exceeds threshold:

If (Cumulative ETc - Cumulative Water Applied) > Threshold: Trigger Irrigation Threshold = Available Water × MAD Where MAD = Management Allowed Depletion (typically 0.5 for most crops)

2.8 ET-Based Smart Irrigation

Modern smart irrigation controllers automate this process:

  1. Retrieve daily weather data (temperature, humidity, wind, solar radiation)
  2. Calculate reference ET (ETo) using Penman-Monteith
  3. Determine crop ET (ETc) using current crop coefficient
  4. Account for effective rainfall from weather forecast or rain sensor
  5. Calculate net irrigation requirement
  6. Schedule irrigation to replace ET deficit
  7. Adjust for irrigation efficiency and system uniformity
Example Calculation:

ETo = 6.0 mm/day (from weather data)
Kc = 1.10 (tomatoes, mid-season)
ETc = 6.0 × 1.10 = 6.6 mm/day
Effective Rain = 0 mm
Net Irrigation Need = 6.6 mm/day
With 85% efficiency: Applied = 6.6 / 0.85 = 7.8 mm/day
