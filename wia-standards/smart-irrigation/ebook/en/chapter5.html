Irrigation Scheduling

5.1 Scheduling Methods

1. Calendar-Based (Fixed Schedule)

  • Simplest approach
  • Irrigate every X days for Y minutes
  • Does not adapt to weather or soil conditions
  • Inefficient: 40-60% water waste typical

2. ET-Based Scheduling

  • Calculate daily crop water use from weather data
  • Irrigate to replace accumulated ET deficit
  • Adapts to weather conditions
  • Typical water savings: 20-30%

3. Soil Moisture-Based

  • Trigger irrigation when soil moisture reaches threshold
  • Real-time feedback from sensors
  • Most precise method
  • Water savings: 30-50%

4. Hybrid Approach (ET + Soil Moisture)

  • Use ET for baseline scheduling
  • Verify and adjust with soil sensors
  • Best of both methods
  • Recommended for WIA-AGRI-007

5.2 Water Balance Method

Daily Water Balance Equation:

SM(t) = SM(t-1) + Rainfall + Irrigation - ETc - Deep Drainage - Runoff

Where:
SM = soil moisture storage (mm)
ETc = crop evapotranspiration (mm/day)
Deep Drainage = water below root zone
Runoff = surface water loss

5.3 Management Allowed Depletion (MAD)

MAD represents the fraction of available water that can be depleted before irrigation:

  • Sensitive crops (lettuce, strawberries): MAD = 0.3-0.4 (30-40%)
  • Moderate crops (tomatoes, peppers): MAD = 0.5 (50%)
  • Tolerant crops (cotton, grapes): MAD = 0.6-0.7 (60-70%)

5.4 Irrigation Frequency

Frequency Calculation:

Days Between Irrigations = (Available Water × MAD) / Daily ETc

Example:
Available Water = 40 mm
MAD = 0.5
Daily ETc = 6 mm/day
Frequency = (40 × 0.5) / 6 = 3.3 days → Irrigate every 3 days

5.5 Deficit Irrigation Strategies

Deliberately under-irrigating to save water:

Regulated Deficit Irrigation (RDI)

  • Apply less than full ETc during non-critical growth stages
  • Common in tree crops (grapes, olives)
  • Can improve fruit quality while reducing water use

Partial Root Zone Drying (PRD)

  • Alternate wetting of root zones
  • Maintains yield with 20-50% less water
  • Requires careful monitoring

5.6 Scheduling Software & Apps

Modern irrigation scheduling tools provide:

  • Automated ET calculations from weather data
  • Crop coefficient databases
  • Multi-zone schedule optimization
  • Mobile alerts and remote control
  • Historical data analysis
  • Integration with soil sensors and controllers
