Soil Moisture Monitoring

4.1 Why Monitor Soil Moisture?

Soil moisture sensors provide real-time feedback on plant water availability, enabling precision irrigation that responds to actual soil conditions rather than estimates.

4.2 Sensor Technologies

Capacitance Sensors

  • Measure dielectric permittivity of soil
  • Output: volumetric water content (VWC)
  • Range: 0-100% VWC
  • Accuracy: ±2-3%
  • Minimal soil disturbance

Tensiometers

  • Measure soil water tension (matric potential)
  • Output: kPa or centibars (0-80 kPa typical)
  • Directly indicates plant water stress
  • Requires maintenance (refilling)

Time Domain Reflectometry (TDR)

  • High accuracy (±1% VWC)
  • Expensive but research-grade
  • Minimal calibration needed

Gypsum Blocks

  • Low cost, disposable
  • Electrical resistance method
  • Degrades over time (1-2 seasons)

4.3 Sensor Placement

Depth Placement:

  • Shallow (15-30cm): Active root zone, irrigation triggering
  • Mid-depth (30-60cm): Root zone monitoring, drainage detection
  • Deep (60-90cm): Leaching detection, groundwater influence

Horizontal Placement:

  • Under emitters or between emitters
  • Representative of irrigation zone
  • Multiple sensors per zone for large areas
  • Avoid atypical spots (rocks, compaction)

4.4 Interpreting Sensor Data

Soil Moisture Thresholds:
  • Field Capacity: Maximum water retention after drainage (~33 kPa)
  • Refill Point: Trigger irrigation (50-70% depletion of available water)
  • Stress Point: Crop stress begins (~80% depletion)
  • Wilting Point: Permanent wilting (~1500 kPa)

Irrigation Trigger Logic:

  • If soil moisture < refill point → Start irrigation
  • If soil moisture > field capacity → Stop irrigation
  • If soil moisture declining rapidly → Increase frequency
  • If soil moisture stable → Maintain schedule

4.5 Sensor Integration

Communication Protocols:

  • SDI-12 (standard for environmental sensors)
  • Modbus RTU (industrial standard)
  • LoRaWAN (long-range wireless)
  • Zigbee (low-power mesh)

4.6 Data Logging & Visualization

Modern systems provide:

  • Real-time moisture graphs
  • Historical trends
  • Depth profile visualization
  • Alerts for dry or saturated conditions
  • Integration with irrigation controllers
