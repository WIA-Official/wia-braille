Weather Integration

6.1 Weather Data Sources

On-Farm Weather Stations

  • Most accurate for site-specific conditions
  • Measures temperature, humidity, wind, solar radiation, rainfall
  • Cost: $500-$3000
  • Requires maintenance and calibration

CIMIS (California Irrigation Management Information System)

  • Network of 145+ weather stations in California
  • Provides daily ET data
  • Free access via API

NOAA/National Weather Service

  • Free weather forecasts and historical data
  • 7-day forecasts available
  • API: api.weather.gov

OpenWeatherMap

  • Global weather data and forecasts
  • Free tier: 1000 calls/day
  • Current weather, 5-day forecast

6.2 Weather-Based Irrigation Adjustments

Rain Delay

  • If rainfall > 5mm in past 24 hours → Skip next irrigation
  • If rainfall > 10mm → Skip next 2 irrigations
  • Use rain sensors or weather data

Forecast-Based Skip

  • If 70%+ rain probability in next 48 hours → Delay irrigation
  • If forecasted rainfall > 10mm → Skip irrigation

Temperature Adjustments

  • High temperature (>35°C) → Increase irrigation by 10-20%
  • Low temperature (<10°C) → Reduce irrigation by 20-30%
  • Frost conditions (<2°C) → Disable irrigation

Wind Adjustments

  • High wind (>8 m/s) → Delay sprinkler irrigation (drip unaffected)
  • Reduces uniformity and increases evaporation losses

6.3 Seasonal ET Variation

Reference ET varies significantly by season:

Typical ETo Values (California Central Valley):
  • January: 1-2 mm/day
  • April: 4-5 mm/day
  • July: 7-8 mm/day
  • October: 3-4 mm/day

6.4 Climate Adaptation

Smart irrigation helps farmers adapt to climate change:

  • Automatically adjusts to shifting weather patterns
  • Responds to heat waves and droughts
  • Optimizes water use during water restrictions
  • Provides data for climate resilience planning
