Introduction & Vision

The vision for unified telecommunications across the Korean peninsula

📡 The Dream of Connection

For over seven decades, the Korean peninsula has been divided. While the 38th parallel created a physical barrier, an even more profound divide emerged in telecommunications. Families separated by the Korean War couldn't make a simple phone call. Students in Seoul couldn't video chat with peers in Pyongyang. Businesses couldn't coordinate across the DMZ. The absence of basic connectivity has amplified the human cost of division.

"Technology cannot reunite Korea. But technology can reunite Korean families. And family reunion is the first step toward national reunion."

🌏 Current State: Two Isolated Networks

Today, North and South Korea operate completely separate telecommunications networks. South Korea boasts one of the world's most advanced 5G infrastructures, with near-universal coverage and gigabit speeds. North Korea has built its own network, including 4G LTE in urban areas, but the two systems have no interoperability.

🎯 WIA-UNI-006 Vision

WIA-UNI-006 proposes a bold yet achievable vision: unified telecommunications infrastructure that connects every Korean, regardless of which side of the DMZ they call home. This isn't about political reunification overnight. It's about practical connectivity that improves lives today while building bridges for tomorrow.

Core Vision Statement

One Network · One Korea · Connected in Peace

By 2030, any Korean should be able to call any other Korean, free of charge, with the same quality they'd expect from a domestic call. A family in Busan should video chat with relatives in Pyongyang as easily as calling across town. This is not a dream - it's an engineering challenge with a humanitarian purpose.

🌟 Key Principles

1. Humanitarian First

Separated families are the top priority. The system must enable free voice and video calls between North and South Korea, with real-time dialect translation to bridge linguistic divergence.

2. Technical Neutrality

The standard respects both existing systems. South Korea's advanced 5G network doesn't need to be downgraded. North Korea's infrastructure can integrate at its own pace. Network slicing enables coexistence of different service levels.

3. Security & Privacy

End-to-end encryption protects all communications. Regional content filtering options respect local policies while maintaining user privacy. No government can eavesdrop on family conversations.

4. Economic Sustainability

The unified network must be economically viable. Joint infrastructure reduces costs. International roaming revenues can fund humanitarian services. Public-private partnerships ensure long-term success.

5. Phased Implementation

Rome wasn't built in a day. WIA-UNI-006 defines a realistic 10-year roadmap: basic connectivity by 2027, nationwide coverage by 2029, and full integration by 2030. Early wins build momentum for later phases.

💡 Benefits

For Families

  • Real-time dialect translation
  • Photo and video sharing
  • Virtual family reunions via VR

For Business

  • Inter-Korean business communications
  • Supply chain coordination
  • Joint economic zones with full connectivity
  • E-commerce platforms spanning both regions

For Society

  • Cultural exchange and education
  • Emergency services coordination
  • Disaster response cooperation
  • Shared internet and digital resources

🗺️ Roadmap Overview

Phase 1: Foundation (2025-2027)

Build core infrastructure at the DMZ. Establish initial cross-border links. Enable basic voice calling for separated families. Goal: 10,000 calls/month.

Phase 2: Expansion (2027-2029)

Expand 5G coverage to 80% of the peninsula. Launch video calling and messaging services. Open business communications. Goal: 100,000 users connected.

Phase 3: Integration (2029-2030)

Achieve 99% nationwide coverage. Full roaming integration. Unified internet gateway. Zero roaming charges for all inter-Korean communications.

Phase 4: Innovation (2030+)

Deploy 6G network. Satellite connectivity for 100% coverage. AI-powered translation. Virtual reality experiences bringing families together across distance.

弘益人間 - Benefit All Humanity

The ancient Korean principle of Hongik Ingan - "widely benefit humanity" - guides WIA-UNI-006. When technology serves human connection rather than division, when networks unite rather than separate, when a grandmother in Seoul can see her grandson's first steps in Pyongyang via video call - that is when technology achieves its highest purpose.

"The most advanced network in the world is worthless if it can't connect the people who need it most."
