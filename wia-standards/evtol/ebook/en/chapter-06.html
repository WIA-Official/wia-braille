Chapter 6

Airworthiness Certification

EASA SC-VTOL, FAA Part 23, Global Standards

📜 What is Airworthiness Certification?

Airworthiness Certification is the process where national aviation authorities officially recognize that aircraft can fly safely. Similar to automotive safety certification but far more stringent and comprehensive. Certifying a single aircraft takes years and costs hundreds of millions to billions of dollars.

eVTOL is entirely new aircraft category, so existing certification regulations cannot be directly applied. Therefore major aviation authorities like FAA (US Federal Aviation Administration), EASA (European Aviation Safety Agency), and CAAC (China Civil Aviation Administration) are developing eVTOL-specific certification standards.

🌍 Major Certification Authorities and Standards

1. FAA (US Federal Aviation Administration)

FAA classifies eVTOL as "Powered Lift" category. Neither conventional helicopter nor fixed-wing - entirely new category. FAA issues Special Conditions for eVTOL based on existing Part 23 (small aircraft) standards.

✈️ FAA eVTOL Certification Path

  • Part 23: Small aircraft airworthiness standards (basic framework)
  • Special Conditions: Additional requirements for eVTOL-specific characteristics
  • Type Certification: Aircraft design certification (TC)
  • Production Certification: Manufacturing process certification (PC)
  • Airworthiness Certificate: Individual aircraft airworthiness certificate

2. EASA (European Aviation Safety Agency)

EASA issued "Special Condition for VTOL" (SC-VTOL) in 2019. First comprehensive certification standard written specifically for eVTOL. SC-VTOL divides into Enhanced Category (EC) or Basic Category (BC).

  • Basic Category: 2 passengers or less, simple eVTOL (e.g., personal recreational)
  • Enhanced Category: Commercial operation eVTOL, stricter standards

3. CAAC (China Civil Aviation Administration)

China is moving fast on eVTOL certification. EHang EH216 obtained worlds first Type Certificate from CAAC in 2021. China is streamlining certification procedures to foster domestic UAM industry.

4. Korean Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport

Korea is developing own eVTOL certification standards as part of K-UAM initiative. Referencing FAA and EASA standards but including additional requirements for Koreas high-density urban environment. Collaborating with domestic manufacturers like Hyundai Supernal to establish certification system targeting 2025 commercialization.

📋 Type Certification Process

5-Stage Certification Process

FAA and EASA use similar 5-stage certification processes. Explained using Joby Aviations case.

Stage 1: Certification Basis Definition

Manufacturer and authority cooperate to define specific certification standards for the aircraft. Agree on which regulations (Part 23, Special Conditions, etc.) to apply and how.

Duration: 6-12 months

Stage 2: Means of Compliance

Manufacturer presents how to meet each certification requirement. Documents specific verification methods like testing, analysis, simulation.

Duration: 12-18 months

Stage 3: Compliance Verification

Actually perform tests and analyses to prove aircraft meets requirements. Includes ground testing, flight testing, system safety analysis.

Duration: 24-36 months

Stage 4: Certification Plan Approval

Authority reviews and approves manufacturers certification plan. Reviews all documents, test results, analysis reports.

Duration: 6-12 months

Stage 5: Type Certificate Issuance

When all requirements met, issues Type Certificate (TC). Can now produce and sell aircraft of that design.

Duration: 3-6 months

Total Duration: Average 5-7 years. Cost: Small eVTOL $100M-500M, Large $500M-2B

🧪 Certification Test Items

1. Ground Testing

  • Static Structural Test: Apply extreme loads to wings, fuselage for destructive testing
  • Fatigue Test: Verify long-term durability with tens of thousands of repeated loads
  • Crash Test: Landing gear and shock absorption performance testing
  • Battery Safety Test: Overcharge, nail penetration, fire, crash testing
  • Electromagnetic Compatibility (EMC): Resistance to lightning, radio interference
  • Noise Measurement: Measure noise levels during take-off/landing and cruise

2. Flight Testing

Hundreds of hours of flight testing to verify aircraft flight characteristics:

  • Performance Testing: Top speed, climb rate, range, ceiling
  • Handling and Stability: Control response at various speeds and weights
  • Transition Testing: Vertical to horizontal flight transition (tilt-rotor)
  • Failure Mode Testing: Flight capability with 1-2 motor failures
  • Autonomous Flight Testing: Autonomous flight system accuracy and safety
  • Extreme Environment Testing: High/low temperature, strong wind, rain, icing conditions

3. System Safety Analysis

  • FHA (Functional Hazard Assessment): Assess functional failure hazards
  • FTA (Fault Tree Analysis): Analyze failure combinations leading to accidents
  • FMEA (Failure Modes and Effects Analysis): Analyze each component failure effects
  • PSSA (Preliminary System Safety Assessment): Design stage safety assessment
  • SSA (System Safety Assessment): Final system safety verification

🔊 Noise Certification

For eVTOL to operate in cities, noise must be low. Must comply with ICAO Annex 16 and each countrys noise regulations.

🔉 eVTOL Noise Standards

  • Take-Off/Landing: Maximum 65 dB @ 100m distance
  • Cruise: Maximum 60 dB @ 100m distance
  • Ground Operations: Maximum 70 dB @ vertiport boundary
  • Night Operations: Additional 5-10 dB restriction (varies by region)

For reference, normal conversation is 60 dB, helicopters 90-105 dB. eVTOL is 30-45 dB quieter than helicopters at "acceptable in cities" level.

🏭 Production Certification

Type Certification is approval of "design". Actually producing aircraft requires separate Production Certificate (PC). This proves manufacturers quality management system can consistently produce aircraft per certified design.

Production Certification Requirements

  • Quality Management System: ISO 9001 or AS9100 certification
  • Process Documentation: Detailed procedures for all manufacturing processes
  • Inspection and Testing: Component and system inspection for each aircraft
  • Supply Chain Management: Subcontractor quality assurance
  • Traceability: Track origin and history of each part
  • Nonconformance Management: Procedures for isolating and handling defective parts

📄 Continuing Airworthiness

Even after certification, must maintain aircraft safety. Called Continuing Airworthiness.

1. Maintenance Program

  • Daily Check: Pre-flight visual inspection, system self-diagnosis
  • Weekly Check: Battery status, motor vibration, hydraulic/cooling systems
  • Monthly Check: Comprehensive system check, software updates
  • Annual Maintenance: Major component replacement, structural inspection, performance testing

2. Airworthiness Directives (AD)

When safety issues discovered during operations, aviation authorities issue Airworthiness Directives. All operators must immediately comply. For example, if fire risk found in specific battery model, AD issued to immediately replace those batteries.

3. Service Bulletins

Improvements voluntarily issued by manufacturers. Not mandatory but recommended. Examples include new software updates or improved components.

🌐 International Mutual Recognition

eVTOL manufacturers want to operate in multiple countries. Certifying separately in each country takes too much time and money. Therefore mutual recognition agreements between major aviation authorities are important.

  • BASA (Bilateral Aviation Safety Agreement): Bilateral safety agreements
  • FAA-EASA Agreement: US-Europe certification mutual recognition
  • ICAO Standards: International Civil Aviation Organizations global standards

Currently eVTOL lacks complete mutual recognition. Each authority conducts own review but references other authorities test data to reduce duplication.

📊 Major eVTOL Certification Status (as of 2024)

  • EHang EH216: CAAC Type Certificate obtained (2021), commercial operations in China
  • Joby Aviation S4: Passed FAA Stage 4 of 5, targeting 2025 certification
  • Volocopter VoloCity: EASA SC-VTOL certification in progress, expecting 2024-2025
  • Lilium Jet: Early EASA certification stage, targeting 2026
  • Archer Maker: Early FAA stage, targeting 2025-2026
  • Wisk Aero 6th Gen: Negotiating FAA autonomous flight certification path
