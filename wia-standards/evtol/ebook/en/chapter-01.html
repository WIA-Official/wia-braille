Chapter 1

eVTOL Overview

Electric Vertical Take-Off and Landing Overview

🚁 What is eVTOL?

Electric Vertical Take-Off and Landing (eVTOL) aircraft represent the future of urban aviation. These revolutionary vehicles use electric motors and distributed propulsion to take off and land vertically like helicopters, but with significantly lower noise, zero emissions during operation, and enhanced safety through redundancy.

eVTOL is not simply an "electric helicopter." It is a convergence of 21st-century cutting-edge aviation technologies: Distributed Electric Propulsion (DEP), advanced battery systems, Fly-By-Wire (FBW) controls, autonomous flight systems, and AI-based navigation. These technologies combine to create safe, quiet, efficient, and environmentally friendly air transportation.

💡 Core Features of eVTOL

  • Vertical Take-Off and Landing: No runway required - can operate from rooftops, parking lots
  • Electric Propulsion: Battery and electric motor driven - zero emissions, low noise
  • Distributed Propulsion: Multiple motors and propellers for enhanced safety and efficiency
  • Autonomous Flight Capable: AI and sensor-based automatic flight control systems
  • High Safety: Redundant and triplicated systems with emergency parachutes
  • Low Operating Cost: Simple structure and electric power reduce maintenance costs

📜 History and Development of eVTOL

1. Early VTOL Aircraft

The history of VTOL aircraft dates back to the 1940s. During World War II, Germany's Focke-Achgelis Fa 223 was an early practical VTOL helicopter. In the 1950s, Igor Sikorsky perfected the modern single-main-rotor helicopter, ushering in the era of vertical take-off aircraft.

The 1960s saw development of various military VTOL aircraft. The British Harrier jump jet achieved VTOL by redirecting jet engine thrust, and the Bell XV-15 pioneered tilt-rotor technology. These technologies later evolved into practical tilt-rotor aircraft like the V-22 Osprey.

2. The Emergence of Electric Propulsion

Until the early 2000s, most VTOL aircraft used jet or piston engines. However, these were noisy, fuel-hungry, and mechanically complex with high maintenance costs. Noise was especially problematic for urban operations.

The turning point came with the drone revolution of the 2010s. As drone manufacturers like DJI popularized small multicopters, technologies such as brushless DC motors, lithium-ion batteries, and flight control computers rapidly advanced. Engineers soon realized: "What if we scale this up to carry people?"

2011

e-volo VC1 First Manned Flight - Germany's e-volo (later Volocopter) achieved the world's first manned flight of an electric multicopter. The VC1 with 18 rotors stayed airborne for 90 seconds.

2013

Volocopter VC200 Certification Flight - Volocopter received manned flight approval from German aviation authorities (ULM), becoming the first certified electric multicopter.

2016

Uber Elevate Project Launch - Uber announced its urban air mobility vision and accelerated eVTOL development. This triggered massive Silicon Valley capital influx into the eVTOL industry.

2017

Lilium Jet First Flight Success - Germany's Lilium successfully flew a 5-seater eVTOL prototype with 36 electric ducted fans, demonstrating innovative tilting jet engine technology.

2019

Joby Aviation Major Investment - Joby Aviation raised $590 million, setting a record for eVTOL startup funding.

2020

FAA eVTOL Certification Path Announced - The US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) officially announced airworthiness certification criteria and procedures for electric VTOL aircraft.

2021

EHang EH216 China's First Type Certificate - China's EHang obtained the world's first Type Certificate for autonomous eVTOL from CAAC (China Aviation Authority).

2023

Joby Aviation FAA Stage 4 Certification - Joby Aviation passed Stage 4 of FAA's 5-stage certification process, becoming the eVTOL manufacturer closest to commercialization.

2024

Volocopter EASA Certification Progress - Volocopter is pursuing EASA certification under SC-VTOL standards and preparing for Paris Olympics demonstration operations.

🌍 Why eVTOL is Needed

1. Urban Traffic Congestion

Major cities worldwide suffer from severe traffic congestion. According to the Texas Transportation Institute's 2022 Urban Congestion Report, average American metropolitan commuters spend 54 hours annually in traffic jams. Seoul faces worse conditions at 95 hours, and Bangkok at 158 hours.

eVTOL utilizes three-dimensional space beyond ground traffic limitations. From Seoul Gangnam to Incheon Airport takes over 2 hours during peak traffic by ground, but eVTOL can complete the trip in under 20 minutes via direct flight. This goes beyond time savings to significantly improve economic productivity.

2. Environmental Solutions

The transport sector accounts for approximately 24% of global greenhouse gas emissions, with passenger cars and trucks comprising 75%. Urban traffic congestion especially increases emissions as vehicles idle or drive at low speeds.

eVTOL produces zero direct emissions during operation. When charged with renewable energy, it can minimize carbon footprint across the entire lifecycle (Well-to-Wake). NASA research shows eVTOL has higher energy efficiency than electric vehicles for distances over 100km.

🌱 Environmental Benefits of eVTOL

  • Zero Emissions: No emissions during operation, carbon neutral with renewable charging
  • Low Noise: 60-65dB like normal conversation (vs. helicopter 90-105dB)
  • High Efficiency: More energy efficient than ground transport for straight-line flight (100km+)
  • Compact Infrastructure: Vertiports require much smaller footprint than airports
  • Urban Green Preservation: Increases transport capacity without subway/road expansion

3. Overcoming Conventional Aviation Limitations

Helicopters have been used for urban transport for decades but have fatal drawbacks:

  • Noise: Main and tail rotors generate 90-105dB of loud noise
  • Safety: Tail rotor failure is catastrophic, single engine dependency
  • Complexity: Mechanically complex with very high maintenance costs
  • Pilot Difficulty: Requires hundreds of hours of training for skilled pilots
  • Operating Cost: Hourly operating cost of $500-2,000 is prohibitive
  • Emissions: Environmental pollution from jet fuel usage

eVTOL solves all these helicopter disadvantages. Distributed electric propulsion allows continued flight even if one or two motors fail. Electric motors are quiet. Mechanically simple for easy maintenance. Automated systems make piloting easier. Operating costs are much lower.

4. New Accessibility

eVTOL can overcome geographic constraints. It can provide fast, efficient transportation to island regions, mountainous terrain, river-divided cities, and developing nations with inadequate infrastructure.

It can also revolutionize emergency medical services. Golden time for cardiac arrest, stroke, and severe trauma patients is very short. eVTOL air ambulances can transport patients to hospitals or deploy specialized medical teams to scenes without any traffic impact.

🎯 Major eVTOL Applications

1. Urban Air Taxi

The most representative eVTOL application. Passengers summon air taxis via smartphone app, board at the nearest vertiport, and land at a vertiport near their destination. Uber, Joby, and Volocopter are pursuing this model.

2. Airport Shuttle

Urban-to-airport travel is eVTOL's killer application. Routes like Seoul-Incheon Airport, London-Heathrow Airport, and New York-JFK Airport can avoid congested traffic for fast travel. High demand is expected from business and premium travelers.

3. Cargo and Logistics

Transport of medical supplies, blood, organs, urgent parts, and high-value cargo requiring fast delivery. Cargo-only eVTOLs have lower certification requirements and can commercialize faster than passenger models.

4. Emergency Medical Services (EMS)

eVTOL air ambulances will play crucial lifesaving roles. Germany's ADAC Luftrettung is already preparing eVTOL-based emergency medical services in collaboration with Volocopter.

5. Tourism and Leisure

Provides new tourism experiences like city skyline tours, natural landscape viewing, and island tourism. Tourist cities like Dubai, Las Vegas, and New York show great interest.

🚀 Future Vision of eVTOL

eVTOL is not just a new vehicle - it's an innovation that will change the urban paradigm itself. By the 2030s, eVTOL will become routine transport with regular routes in the sky like subways and buses on the ground. By the 2040s, fully autonomous flight will commercialize for safe operation without pilots.

Battery technology advances will extend range for intercity long-distance travel, and hydrogen fuel cells plus hybrid propulsion systems will enable 500km+ flight. Urban design will also change with vertiports on all major building rooftops, making 3D air traffic a core element of city planning.

✨ The 2050 eVTOL World

  • Over 100,000 vertiports operating in major cities worldwide
  • 24/7 unmanned operation through fully autonomous flight
  • Carbon-neutral air transport based on renewable energy
  • Fully integrated MaaS ecosystem with ground transport
  • Public transport accessible to everyone at affordable prices
  • 1,000km+ range to replace domestic flights
