Chapter 5

Safety Systems

Redundancy, Emergency Landing, and Ballistic Parachutes

🛡️ Safety is Paramount

Aviation safety golden rule: "Less than 1 fatal accident per 10 million flight hours." This means 10^-7 level safety. For eVTOL to fly over countless people in cities, this safety level must be achieved - 10x safer than cars (10^-6) and 100x safer than general aviation (10^-5).

eVTOL achieves this through multiple layers of safety systems. Redundant/triplicated components, automatic fault detection and recovery, emergency landing systems, and ballistic parachutes as last resort - using Defense in Depth strategy.

🔁 Redundancy

1. Propulsion System Redundancy

eVTOLs biggest safety feature is distributed electric propulsion. Conventional helicopters have single main rotor and engine - engine failure is very dangerous (must autorotate to land). In contrast, eVTOL has multiple independent motors. Volocopter VoloCity has 18 motors - can safely land even if 9 fail. This is "50%+ failure tolerance" capability.

✅ Propulsion System Redundancy Levels

  • N+1 Configuration: Tolerates 1 motor failure. Minimum requirement
  • N+2 Configuration: Tolerates 2 simultaneous motor failures. Recommended level
  • N/2 Configuration: Tolerates half of all motors failing. Highest level (Volocopter)

2. Battery Redundancy

Battery packs also divided into multiple independent modules. Joby Aviation S4 has 12 independent battery modules, each managed by separate BMS. If one module fails or overheats, its automatically isolated and flight continues with remaining modules.

  • Module Isolation: Electrically disconnect failed module
  • Thermal Isolation: Prevent one modules fire from spreading to others
  • Power Redistribution: Prioritize critical systems with remaining modules

3. Flight Control Computer Redundancy

As explained in previous chapter, eVTOL have 2 or 3 independent flight control computers. Sometimes using processors from different manufacturers to prevent Common Mode Failure.

4. Sensor Redundancy

All critical sensors installed in at least duplicate. IMU, GPS, barometer, magnetic compass all redundant, sometimes combining sensors of different principles (e.g., GPS + vision-based navigation).

⚠️ Fault Detection and Management

1. Health Monitoring System

eVTOL monitors all component status in real-time. Built-In Test (BIT) systems automatically check systems before, during, and after flight.

  • Motors: Monitor temperature, current, vibration, RPM deviation
  • Batteries: Monitor cell-level voltage, temperature, internal resistance, swelling
  • Control Surfaces: Compare commanded vs. actual position with position sensors
  • Structure: Monitor structural fatigue with stress sensors

2. Predictive Maintenance

Machine learning predicts component failures in advance. For example, detecting motor bearing vibration pattern changes allows replacement before failure.

3. Automatic Fault Response

When fault detected, system automatically responds: (1) Detect: Identify fault via sensors or self-diagnosis (2) Isolate: Isolate failed component from system (3) Reconfigure: Reconfigure with remaining systems (4) Degrade: Limit performance if necessary (speed reduction) (5) Land: Land at nearest safe location

🪂 Emergency Landing Systems

1. Controlled Landing

Even if some motors or batteries fail, attempt controlled landing with remaining systems. Flight control computer optimally distributes remaining thrust for safe touchdown.

🎯 Emergency Landing Priority

  1. 1st Priority: Designated vertiports
  2. 2nd Priority: Nearby airports or helipads
  3. 3rd Priority: Large parking lots, parks, sports fields
  4. 4th Priority: Roads (request traffic control)
  5. Last Resort: Water (passenger safety priority)

2. Autorotation (Tilt-Rotor eVTOL)

Some tilt-rotor eVTOL designed to land via autorotation even with complete power loss. Propellers rotate from descending airflow to slow descent rate. However, this requires very skilled technique.

3. Automatic Landing Site Selection

AI system finds safe landing sites in real-time: Scan surrounding terrain with cameras and LiDAR. Identify flat surfaces, obstacle-free spaces. Prioritize areas without people or vehicles. Calculate optimal approach path considering wind direction and speed. Notify pilot or ground control of plan.

🪂 Ballistic Recovery System (BRS)

1. Need for Parachute

When all safety devices fail and aircraft becomes uncontrollable, last resort is lowering entire aircraft with parachute. Called Ballistic Recovery Systems (BRS) or Whole Aircraft Parachute System (WAPS). Already proven technology in small general aviation aircraft (Cirrus SR22, etc.), saving over 500 lives. Mandatory or strongly recommended for eVTOL.

2. Operating Principle

  1. Deployment Decision: Pilot manually or system automatically decides parachute deployment
  2. Rocket Launch: Small solid rocket launches parachute above aircraft (within 1-2 seconds)
  3. Parachute Unfolds: Parachute fully unfolds in air (3-5 seconds)
  4. Descent: Entire aircraft suspended by parachute descends at 6-8 m/s
  5. Landing: Landing gear and shock absorption structure mitigate impact

3. Design Considerations

  • Minimum Deployment Altitude: 100-150m AGL (Above Ground Level)
  • Parachute Size: 2-3x aircraft weight area
  • Descent Rate: 6-8 m/s (minimize passenger injury)
  • Non-Reusable: Replace entire system after single use
  • Weight: 3-5% of total aircraft weight

Parachute is not panacea - insufficient deployment time below 100m, increased landing impact in strong winds (>25 kt), still dangerous over water or dense buildings, risk of entanglement with rotors/wings during deployment.

🔥 Fire Prevention and Suppression

1. Battery Fire Risk

Lithium-ion batteries have Thermal Runaway risk. If one cell overheats, internal chemical reactions can runaway causing fire and explosion.

2. Fire Prevention Design

  • Thermal Barriers Between Cells: Ceramic or aerogel insulation
  • Pressure Relief: Safely vent gases when overheated
  • Temperature Monitoring: Real-time cell-level temperature surveillance
  • Current Limiting: Immediately cut overcurrent
  • Mechanical Protection: Protect battery pack during collision

3. Fire Suppression System

When fire occurs, suppression system automatically activates: Inject inert gas (nitrogen or CO2) to cut oxygen. Spray liquid coolant on hot cells. Isolate fire module electrically and thermally. Exhaust toxic gases outside aircraft.

💺 Passenger Safety Equipment

1. Seat Belts and Crash Protection

  • 4-Point or 5-Point Harness: Secure shoulders and waist
  • Airbags: Some eVTOL equipped with airbags (like cars)
  • Energy-Absorbing Seats: Absorb downward impact energy
  • Headrests: Prevent neck injury

2. Passenger Briefing

Like commercial aircraft, eVTOL must provide passenger safety briefings: Seat belt usage. Emergency exit locations. Oxygen mask usage (if needed). Ditching procedures. Electronic device restrictions.

🌊 Ditching Safety

For routes over water, emergency ditching capability is important. Design considerations: Buoyancy - aircraft floats on water for period. Attitude stability - designed not to capsize. Escape routes - doors that open easily even on water. Life vests - at each seat. Emergency Position Indicating Radio Beacon - automatically activates on water contact to transmit position.

📊 Achieving Safety Goals

Through these multi-layer safety systems, eVTOL can achieve 10^-7 level safety goals. System safety analysis techniques (FTA, FMEA, FHA, etc.) identify all possible failure modes and prepare countermeasures.

✅ Safety Design Principles

  • Fail-Safe: Transition to safe state when failed
  • Redundancy: Duplicate/triplicate critical systems
  • Diversity: Combine different technologies to prevent common mode failures
  • Defense in Depth: Multi-layer defense prevents single failure causing accident
  • Safety by Design: Prioritize safety from design stage
