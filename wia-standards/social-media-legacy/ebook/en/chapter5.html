CHAPTER 5

Data Export and Preservation

"Data is memory made digital. When we export and preserve social media archives, we're not just backing up files—we're safeguarding stories, relationships, and life histories for generations who will never meet the person but can know them through their digital trail."

The Importance of Data Preservation

Social media platforms are ephemeral. MySpace lost 50 million songs and 12 years of user content in a 2019 server migration failure. Google+ shut down, taking millions of posts with it. Vine disappeared, leaving creators scrambling to save videos. The message is clear: platform-hosted content isn't truly yours until you've exported and preserved it independently.

For digital legacy management, data export serves multiple critical functions. It creates offline backups immune to platform shutdowns or account hacks, enables migration to new platforms when services change, provides legal documentation for estate settlement, preserves memories in accessible formats for non-technical family members, and creates historical records for future researchers and descendants.

The WIA-LEG-007 standard mandates comprehensive data export capabilities as a core requirement for any platform claiming legacy management support.

Data Export Formats

The standard defines three primary export formats, each optimized for different use cases:

JSON (JavaScript Object Notation)

Purpose: Complete, structured data with full metadata preservation
Best for: Developers, archivists, migration to other platforms, long-term digital preservation

JSON exports contain everything: post content, timestamps, geolocation data, engagement metrics (likes, comments, shares), relationship graphs (friends, followers, connections), privacy settings for each piece of content, edit history showing content evolution, and embedded media metadata (resolution, camera type, creation date).

{ "userId": "user_123456", "exportDate": "2025-12-25T10:30:00Z", "accountCreated": "2010-03-15T08:20:00Z", "profileData": { "displayName": "Jane Smith", "bio": "Teacher, traveler, cat mom", "location": "Portland, OR", "profilePhoto": "media/photos/profile_001.jpg" }, "posts": [ { "postId": "post_abc123", "timestamp": "2025-06-15T14:22:00Z", "content": "Just finished my first marathon! 26.2 miles...", "visibility": "public", "location": { "name": "Waterfront Park", "coordinates": [45.5231, -122.6765] }, "media": [ { "type": "photo", "filename": "media/photos/2025-06-15_001.jpg", "resolution": "3024x4032", "timestamp": "2025-06-15T14:18:00Z" } ], "engagement": { "likes": 147, "comments": 23, "shares": 5 } } ], "messages": [...], "friends": [...], "photos": [...], "videos": [...] }

CSV (Comma-Separated Values)

Purpose: Spreadsheet-friendly format for easy browsing and analysis
Best for: Non-technical users, data analysis, creating timelines and summaries

CSV exports flatten data into tables that open in Excel, Google Sheets, or any spreadsheet program. While less comprehensive than JSON, they're far more accessible to average users who want to browse through a loved one's posts chronologically or search for specific keywords.

Typical CSV structure includes separate files for posts, comments, photos, friends, and messages, with columns for date, content, visibility, engagement counts, and media references.

XML (eXtensible Markup Language)

Purpose: Archival standard format for long-term preservation
Best for: Libraries, academic archives, legal compliance, multi-decade preservation

XML is verbose but universally supported and designed for longevity. It follows established archival standards like METS (Metadata Encoding and Transmission Standard) and PREMIS (Preservation Metadata), ensuring data remains accessible even as technology evolves over decades.

What Gets Exported

Comprehensive data exports should include all user-generated and user-relevant content:

Core Content

  • Posts and Updates: All status updates, shared links, notes, and text content
  • Comments: Both comments you made and comments on your content
  • Reactions: Likes, loves, laughs, and other engagement you gave or received
  • Photos: All uploaded images at original resolution
  • Videos: All uploaded videos at original quality
  • Stories/Ephemeral Content: Even if expired, archived copies

Relationship Data

  • Friends/Connections: Complete list with connection dates
  • Followers/Following: Asymmetric relationship tracking
  • Groups and Communities: Memberships, roles, and group content
  • Events: Created events, RSVPs, and attendance history

Communication

  • Private Messages: Direct messages, group chats (with privacy controls)
  • Video Calls: Call logs and recordings if saved
  • Voice Messages: Audio recordings in messages

Metadata

  • Timestamps: Creation, modification, and deletion times
  • Locations: Geotags and check-ins
  • Device Information: What device/app was used
  • IP Addresses: For security and verification (optional)
  • Edit History: Previous versions of edited content

Activity Logs

  • Search History: What you searched for (if retention enabled)
  • Viewing History: Content you viewed or engaged with
  • Ad Interactions: Ads you clicked or engaged with
  • App Connections: Third-party apps with access to your account

Privacy-Sensitive Exports

Some data types raise special privacy concerns. Private messages involve third parties whose consent wasn't obtained. Location data could reveal home addresses or sensitive locations. Search history might contain embarrassing or private queries. The standard requires explicit opt-in during Phase 1 (Preparation) for exporting these sensitive categories, with clear explanations of what legacy contacts will access.

The Export Process

Platforms should implement user-friendly export workflows that balance thoroughness with usability:

Initiation

  1. User or verified legacy contact requests export
  2. System confirms identity through multi-factor authentication
  3. User selects export scope (all data, date ranges, specific content types)
  4. User chooses export formats (JSON, CSV, XML, or all)
  5. User opts in/out of privacy-sensitive data inclusion

Processing

  1. System queues export job (may take hours or days for large accounts)
  2. Data is compiled from distributed storage systems
  3. Media files are gathered from CDNs and archived
  4. Metadata is attached and cross-referenced
  5. Export is encrypted for secure delivery

Delivery

  1. User receives notification when export is ready
  2. Secure download link with expiration date (typically 7-30 days)
  3. Large exports may be split into multiple ZIP files
  4. Checksum provided for integrity verification
  5. Download instructions and README documentation included

Post-Export

  1. User downloads and verifies export completeness
  2. Export is stored in multiple locations (3-2-1 backup strategy)
  3. Platform retains export for re-download window (30-90 days)
  4. After retention period, export is deleted from platform servers

Long-Term Preservation Strategies

Downloading data is only the first step. Preservation across years and decades requires thoughtful strategy:

The 3-2-1 Backup Rule

Industry best practice for data preservation: 3 copies, 2 different media types, 1 offsite location.

3 Copies: Original export plus two backups. If one fails, you have redundancy.
2 Media Types: Don't store all copies on the same type of device. Combine external hard drives, cloud storage, USB drives, optical media (DVD/Blu-ray), or NAS (network-attached storage).
1 Offsite: At least one copy physically separate from others. If your house burns down, offsite backup survives.

Storage Media Considerations

  • Cloud Storage (Google Drive, Dropbox, iCloud): Pros: Accessible anywhere, automatic redundancy, no physical degradation. Cons: Requires ongoing subscription, privacy concerns, platform dependency.
  • External Hard Drives: Pros: Large capacity, one-time cost, offline security. Cons: Mechanical failure risk, typically 3-5 year lifespan, requires active maintenance.
  • Solid State Drives (SSDs): Pros: No moving parts, faster than HDDs, more durable. Cons: Higher cost per gigabyte, data degradation if unpowered for years.
  • Optical Media (M-DISC DVD/Blu-ray): Pros: Longevity (claimed 1000+ years), immune to electromagnetic damage, no power required. Cons: Limited capacity, slower access, requires special burners.
  • Network Attached Storage (NAS): Pros: Centralized home backup, RAID redundancy, accessible to whole family. Cons: Upfront cost, technical complexity, still local (needs offsite backup too).

Format Migration

File formats evolve. JSON is standard today but may be obsolete in 2075. Long-term preservation requires periodic format migration:

  1. Every 5-10 years: Review storage media health and data accessibility
  2. Every 10-20 years: Migrate to current standard formats
  3. Maintain multiple formats: Keep both legacy and updated versions
  4. Document everything: Include README files explaining structure and context

Organizational Structure

Organize exported data for easy access by future generations who won't have your context:

Using Exported Data

Raw export files aren't user-friendly for most people. The standard encourages development of viewing tools:

Data Viewers

Simple HTML/JavaScript applications that render JSON exports in readable timeline format. These viewers can run entirely offline, protecting privacy while making data accessible to non-technical family members.

Search and Filter Tools

Scripts or programs that search across all exported content for keywords, dates, people, or locations. Essential for large archives with thousands of posts.

Photo Gallery Generators

Tools that create beautiful photo albums from exported images, sorted by date or event, with original captions and metadata preserved.

Story Recreation

Applications that recreate the original social media experience—scrolling timeline, comment threads, likes and reactions—using only exported data. Allows family members to browse exactly as the deceased's profile appeared.

Open Source Viewer Project

The WIA Foundation maintains open-source viewer tools at github.com/WIA-Official/legacy-viewers. These tools are free, privacy-preserving (run entirely on your computer), and support all WIA-LEG-007 export formats. Platform developers are encouraged to bundle these viewers with exports or link to them in export README files.

Legal and Compliance Considerations

Data Protection Regulations

GDPR grants deceased's heirs certain access rights but also imposes restrictions. Platforms must balance legitimate interest in data access with third-party privacy rights. The standard provides guidance for GDPR-compliant exports that protect both deceased's legacy and others' privacy.

Copyright and Intellectual Property

Exported content includes copyrighted material—photos the deceased took (they own), photos others took of them (photographer owns), and shared content from third parties (original creator owns). Exports should include copyright metadata and usage guidelines to prevent infringement by well-meaning families.

Platform Terms of Service

Some platforms prohibit bulk data scraping but must allow legitimate personal data exports under GDPR and similar laws. The standard clarifies that exports for legacy preservation constitute lawful personal data portability, not prohibited scraping.

Automated Export Scheduling

Waiting until death to export data is risky—accounts might be hacked, platforms could shut down, or passwords might be lost. The standard encourages automated periodic exports:

  • Quarterly Exports: Automatic data backup every 3 months
  • Cloud Storage Integration: Exports auto-upload to user's preferred cloud service
  • Incremental Backups: Only new content since last export (reduces size and time)
  • Verification Checks: Automated integrity verification of stored exports

This proactive approach ensures data is preserved throughout life, not just after death, protecting against account loss from hacking, platform shutdowns, or accidental deletion.

"The most precious things we create often exist only in digital form. Export and preservation transforms ephemeral platform data into permanent family treasures that outlive any company, service, or technology."

Chapter Summary

Data export and preservation are foundational to digital legacy management. The WIA-LEG-007 standard mandates comprehensive exports in JSON (complete structured data), CSV (spreadsheet-friendly), and XML (archival standard) formats. Exports should include all posts, photos, videos, messages, relationships, and metadata. Long-term preservation requires 3-2-1 backup strategy (3 copies, 2 media types, 1 offsite), periodic format migration, and organized storage accessible to future generations. Viewing tools make raw data accessible to non-technical family members, while automated export scheduling protects data throughout life, not just after death.
