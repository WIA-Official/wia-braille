CHAPTER 2

The Four-Phase Protocol

"Life transitions require structure, respect, and time. The four-phase protocol ensures that digital legacy management honors both the deceased and the living, balancing efficiency with compassion."

Overview: A Structured Approach to Digital Death

The WIA-LEG-007 protocol divides digital legacy management into four distinct phases, each with specific objectives, timelines, and technical requirements. This structure provides predictability during unpredictable times, ensuring nothing falls through the cracks while preventing hasty decisions made in grief's immediate aftermath.

Unlike ad-hoc approaches where families scramble to access accounts after death, the four-phase protocol creates a deliberate, respectful journey from preparation through long-term preservation. Each phase builds upon the previous one, creating a comprehensive system that serves the deceased's wishes, family needs, and legal requirements.

Phase 1: Preparation (Lifetime Activity)

Objective: Establish Clear Digital Legacy Intentions

Timeline: Ongoing throughout life, with annual reviews recommended

Key Actors: Account holder, legacy contacts (optional)

The Preparation Phase occurs while the account holder is alive and actively planning their digital afterlife. This proactive approach removes burden from grieving families and ensures personal wishes are honored precisely as intended.

Essential Preparation Steps

  1. Designate Legacy Contacts
    Choose 1-3 trusted individuals who will manage your digital presence after death. These contacts should be:
    • Tech-savvy enough to navigate digital platforms
    • Emotionally stable to handle the responsibility during grief
    • Younger than you (to ensure they outlive you)
    • Aligned with your values and wishes
    Legacy contacts receive specific permissions defined by you, ranging from view-only access to full account management capabilities.
  2. Define Memorialization Preferences
    Specify exactly what should happen to your accounts:
    • Memorialize: Convert to permanent tribute pages
    • Delete: Remove completely after verification period
    • Archive: Lock account but preserve data for family
    • Hybrid: Memorialize some platforms, delete others
  3. Configure Data Export Settings
    Determine what data should be exported and in what formats:
    • All posts, comments, and reactions
    • Photos and videos with metadata
    • Private messages (or exclude for privacy)
    • Friend lists and connection data
    • Export formats: JSON for completeness, CSV for spreadsheets, XML for archival
  4. Set Tribute Page Parameters
    If memorializing, configure tribute page settings:
    • Public visibility vs. friends-only
    • Allow comments and tributes (yes/no/moderated)
    • Featured photos and cover image
    • Memorial statement or obituary text
    • Charitable donation links
    • Anniversary notifications (birthdays, death anniversary)
  5. AI Memorial Bot Preferences
    Decide if you want an AI chatbot trained on your digital history:
    • Opt-in/opt-out for bot creation
    • Training data sources (posts, messages, comments)
    • Interaction limits (family only, friends, public)
    • Personality parameters and tone settings
    • Sunset date (when bot should be deactivated)
  6. Account Deletion Triggers
    Define conditions that should trigger account deletion:
    • After specific time period (e.g., 10 years post-death)
    • When all legacy contacts pass away
    • Immediately upon death verification
    • Never (preserve indefinitely)

Best Practice: Annual Legacy Checkup

Life changes—relationships evolve, preferences shift, new platforms emerge. Schedule an annual "digital legacy checkup" where you review and update your settings. Consider this part of routine maintenance like updating your will or insurance policies. Many users set a reminder on their birthday or New Year's Day.

Phase 2: Verification (30-90 Days Post-Death)

Objective: Confirm Death and Validate Legal Authority

Timeline: 30-90 days (cooling-off period)

Key Actors: Legacy contacts, legal representatives, platform verification teams

The Verification Phase is the most security-critical stage. It prevents fraudulent account takeovers while enabling legitimate legacy management. The cooling-off period serves dual purposes: allowing time for thorough verification and preventing impulsive decisions during grief's acute phase.

Verification Requirements

  1. Death Certificate Submission
    Official government-issued death certificate with:
    • Full legal name matching account registration
    • Date of death
    • Government seal and certification
    • Unique certificate number for verification
    Certificates are verified against government databases where possible. Digital signatures and blockchain timestamps ensure authenticity.
  2. Legal Representative Identification
    Person requesting access must prove they are:
    • Designated legacy contact (if configured during Phase 1)
    • Court-appointed executor of estate
    • Next of kin with legal authority
    • Legal guardian or power of attorney
    Documentation required: government ID, proof of relationship, court orders if applicable.
  3. Multi-Factor Authentication
    Legacy contacts verify their identity through:
    • Email verification link
    • SMS code to registered phone
    • Video verification call (for high-value accounts)
    • Biometric verification where available
  4. Fraud Detection Algorithms
    Automated systems check for suspicious patterns:
    • Recent account activity (is user truly deceased?)
    • Multiple simultaneous legacy requests (red flag)
    • Mismatched personal information
    • Requests from high-fraud regions
    • Doctored or forged documents
    Machine learning models trained on thousands of legitimate and fraudulent requests identify anomalies for human review.
  5. Cross-Platform Verification
    WIA-LEG-007 enables verification sharing across platforms. If Facebook verifies a death, Instagram can reference that verification rather than requiring duplicate documentation. This reduces family burden while maintaining security through cryptographic proof chains.
Day 0: Death occurs
Days 1-7: Legacy contact submits initial request with death certificate
Days 7-14: Platform verifies documents and legal authority
Days 14-21: Fraud detection review and cross-platform checks
Days 21-30: Cooling-off period for final verification and family deliberation
Day 30-90: Extended cooling period if requested or if verification issues arise
Post-Cooling: Phase 3 execution begins

Security Protocols

The verification phase implements defense-in-depth security:

  • Document Hashing: All submitted documents are cryptographically hashed and timestamped on immutable ledgers, preventing tampering.
  • Government API Integration: Direct queries to vital statistics databases confirm death records without relying solely on submitted certificates.
  • Anomaly Detection: Machine learning identifies unusual patterns like sudden preference changes before death or legacy contacts added immediately prior to death.
  • Human Review: High-risk cases (large follower counts, verified accounts, financial influencers) receive mandatory human oversight.
  • Appeal Process: Rejected requests can be appealed with additional documentation, preventing legitimate families from being locked out.

Privacy Consideration: Balancing Access and Protection

Death doesn't eliminate privacy rights. Even verified legacy contacts don't automatically get access to all data—especially private messages, which may contain confidential information from third parties. The standard requires explicit opt-in during Phase 1 for message access, respecting both the deceased's wishes and correspondents' privacy.

Phase 3: Execution (Immediate Post-Verification)

Objective: Implement Legacy Preferences Automatically

Timeline: 24-72 hours post-verification

Key Actors: Platform systems, legacy contacts, AI training systems

Once verification completes, Phase 3 executes the deceased's preferences rapidly and accurately. Automation ensures consistency while reducing the emotional burden on families who shouldn't have to manually implement complex settings during grief.

Execution Workflow

  1. Account Status Transition
    The account changes from "active" to the specified status:
    • Memorialized: Profile marked with memorial indicator, login disabled, content preserved
    • Archived: Hidden from public view but data preserved for legacy contacts
    • Scheduled Deletion: Marked for removal after specified period
    Visual indicators inform viewers they're viewing a memorial, preventing confusion and inappropriate interactions (like tagging deceased in photos).
  2. Legacy Contact Notification
    All designated legacy contacts receive comprehensive notifications:
    • Email summary of their permissions and responsibilities
    • Link to legacy dashboard for account management
    • Instructions for downloading data exports
    • Tribute page URL if memorialization chosen
    • Resources for grief support and digital legacy guidance
  3. Data Export Generation
    Complete archives are compiled in specified formats:
    • JSON files with full metadata, timestamps, and relationships
    • CSV spreadsheets for easy viewing in Excel/Sheets
    • XML for long-term archival standards compliance
    • Media files (photos, videos) organized by date
    • README documentation explaining file structure
    Exports are encrypted and accessible only to verified legacy contacts via secure download links with expiration dates.
  4. Tribute Page Creation
    If memorialization chosen, a dedicated tribute page launches:
    • Profile photo with memorial frame overlay
    • Cover photo and memorial statement
    • Timeline showing life highlights
    • Tribute wall for friends and family messages
    • Photo galleries and memory collections
    • Charitable donation integration if specified
    • Event listings (memorial services, celebrations of life)
  5. AI Memorial Bot Training
    If opted-in during Phase 1, bot training begins:
    • Data collection from specified sources (posts, comments, etc.)
    • Natural language processing to extract personality traits
    • Large language model fine-tuning on writing style
    • Memory graph construction linking events, people, and places
    • Safety filters to prevent inappropriate responses
    • Testing phase with legacy contacts before public activation
    Training typically takes 24-72 hours depending on data volume.
  6. Privacy Settings Update
    Privacy controls adjust to memorial status:
    • No new friend requests accepted
    • Tagging disabled to prevent inappropriate uses
    • Messaging redirected to legacy contacts
    • Content visibility frozen at death-time settings
    • Search engine indexing updated per family preferences
  7. Platform-Specific Actions
    Different platforms have unique features:
    • Facebook: Birthday notifications suppressed, tribute section added
    • Instagram: Stories archived, memorial badge on profile
    • Twitter: Account locked from new tweets, bio updated
    • LinkedIn: Profile marked "In Memoriam," job seeking disabled
    • TikTok: Video uploads stopped, viewing statistics frozen

Phase 4: Maintenance (Long-Term Preservation)

Objective: Sustain Digital Legacy Over Years and Decades

Timeline: Indefinite (10+ years typical)

Key Actors: Platform preservation systems, legacy contacts, community members

The final phase isn't an ending but a beginning—maintaining digital legacies for years or decades. This requires robust infrastructure, ongoing management, and adaptation to changing technologies.

Long-Term Maintenance Activities

  1. Legacy Contact Management
    As time passes, legacy contact situations change:
    • Original contacts may pass away, requiring designation of successor contacts
    • Contact information updates (new emails, phone numbers)
    • Permission transfers if contacts become unable to serve
    • Addition of new contacts as family structures evolve
    The standard includes protocols for contact succession planning.
  2. Tribute Page Moderation
    Memorial pages require ongoing oversight:
    • Removing spam and inappropriate content
    • Approving genuine tributes if moderation enabled
    • Updating memorial information (obituary links, service details)
    • Curating photo galleries and memory collections
    • Managing anniversary posts and notifications
  3. AI Bot Interactions
    Memorial bots continue engaging with users:
    • Answering questions about the deceased
    • Sharing memories and stories
    • Providing comfort during anniversaries
    • Updating responses based on new information from legacy contacts
    • Monitoring for misuse or inappropriate queries
    Bots evolve through continued learning while maintaining core personality.
  4. Data Archive Preservation
    Ensuring exported data remains accessible:
    • Format migration as standards evolve (JSON today, ??? in 2045)
    • Storage redundancy across geographic regions
    • Integrity verification through periodic checksums
    • Backup restoration testing
    • Legacy contact download access maintenance
    The standard recommends 3-2-1 backup strategy: 3 copies, 2 different media types, 1 offsite.
  5. Platform Migration
    When platforms shut down or merge:
    • Advance notification to legacy contacts (minimum 90 days)
    • Data export in standard WIA formats
    • Migration assistance to surviving platforms
    • Preservation of URLs through redirects where possible
    MySpace's collapse taught painful lessons about digital impermanence; this standard ensures legacies outlive platforms.
  6. Legal Compliance Updates
    As laws evolve:
    • GDPR and privacy regulation compliance
    • Copyright law updates affecting posthumous content
    • Right-to-be-forgotten requests from third parties
    • Jurisdictional changes as users move or laws shift
  7. Community Healing
    Supporting the ongoing grieving process:
    • Anniversary notifications to close friends and family
    • Memory reminder features ("5 years ago today...")
    • Grief resource links and support group connections
    • Celebration of life event coordination
    • Charitable giving campaigns in deceased's honor

Sunset Planning: When Legacies End

Not all legacies should last forever. The standard supports "sunset dates"—predetermined times when memorial accounts transition to full deletion or archive-only status. This respects the natural flow of memory: intense grief fades, future generations move forward, and digital space has limits. Sunset dates might be 10, 25, or 50 years post-death, giving families closure while ensuring reasonable resource allocation.

Integration: The Four Phases Working Together

The power of this protocol lies in how the phases interconnect. Preparation (Phase 1) choices directly drive Execution (Phase 3) automation. Verification (Phase 2) rigor protects Maintenance (Phase 4) integrity. Each phase informs and enables the others, creating a comprehensive lifecycle approach to digital legacy.

Users who invest time in thorough Phase 1 preparation gift their families seamless Phase 3 execution. Platforms that implement robust Phase 2 verification prevent catastrophic Phase 4 fraud. And thoughtful Phase 4 maintenance honors the intentions set during Phase 1, closing the circle of respect, security, and remembrance.

"The four-phase protocol transforms death from a digital disaster into a managed transition—respecting the past, serving the present, and preserving for the future."

Chapter Summary

The WIA-LEG-007 four-phase protocol provides structure for digital legacy management: Preparation (lifetime planning), Verification (30-90 day cooling period with fraud prevention), Execution (automated implementation of preferences), and Maintenance (long-term preservation). Each phase serves distinct purposes while integrating into a comprehensive system that honors the deceased, protects security, and serves families across decades.
