Chapter 01

Space Debris Overview

1.1 Definition

Space debris comprises all non-functional human-made objects in Earth orbit. This includes defunct satellites (3,000+), rocket upper stages (2,000+), fragmentation debris, and mission-related objects.

1.2 Classification

By size: Large (10cm+, ~34,000 tracked), Medium (1-10cm, ~900,000), Small (1mm-1cm, ~128M). 1cm objects at 7-8 km/s carry grenade-level energy.

1.3 Sources

Satellite launches, collisions (2009 Iridium-Kosmos), intentional destruction (2007 Chinese ASAT test created 3,500+ trackable fragments), explosions.

1.4 Distribution

Concentrated in LEO (800-1000km 'garbage belt'), MEO (GPS orbits), and GEO ring. LEO has self-cleaning via atmospheric drag but takes decades-centuries.

1.5 Risks

Collision energy devastating: 1cm = grenade, 10cm = satellite destruction. ISS performs multiple avoidance maneuvers annually. $billions in satellite assets at risk.

💡 Key Takeaways

This chapter covered essential aspects of space debris overview. Understanding these concepts is crucial for effective space debris management and ensuring long-term sustainability of space activities.
