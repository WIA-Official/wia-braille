CHAPTER 7

🌐 Satellite Internet Services

7.1 Evolution

Satellite internet emerged in late 1990s but remained expensive with slow speeds and high latency. Late 2010s HTS technology and LEO mega constellations transformed the industry. Starlink leading 2020s revolution with fiber-like performance globally.

7.2 Starlink (SpaceX)

World's largest commercial satellite constellation providing global high-speed internet from LEO.

Technical Specs:

  • Satellites: 5,000+ operational (2025), targeting 42,000
  • Altitude: 550km (Shell 1), 340km (Shell 2)
  • Frequency: Ku-band (user), Ka-band (gateway)
  • Speed: 50-200 Mbps down, 10-20 Mbps up
  • Latency: 20-40 ms (fiber-class)
  • ISL: Gen2 satellites have 100 Gbps laser links

Dishy Terminal:

  • Form: 50cm flat phased array
  • Auto-tracking: Electronic beam steering, plug-and-play
  • Price: $499 terminal, $99-120/month service
  • Power: 50-100W average
  • Weather: -30°C to +50°C, snow melt built-in

Service:

  • Coverage: 60+ countries
  • Subscribers: 2M+ (2024)
  • Markets: Rural US/Canada/Europe/Australia
  • Special: Ukraine war communications, disaster response

Mobile Services:

  • RV: Internet while driving
  • Maritime: Dual-Dishy for ships
  • Aviation: In-flight WiFi 100-350 Mbps
  • Pricing: $250-5,000/month (2-5x residential)
Digital Divide: Starlink enables 3 billion unconnected people to access online education, telemedicine, e-commerce, dramatically improving equality.

7.3 OneWeb

UK/India-owned LEO operator targeting enterprise, government, telcos via B2B2C model.

Specs:

  • Satellites: 648 complete
  • Orbit: 1,200km polar
  • Frequency: Ku-band
  • Speed: 150-300 Mbps (enterprise leased lines)
  • Latency: 50-70 ms

Business Model:

  • Telecom partnerships: AT&T, Hughes, Telstra
  • Government contracts: UK MoD, Arctic comms
  • Enterprise: Remote mines, oil platforms, cruises
  • Backhaul: 5G base station connectivity

7.4 Amazon Kuiper

Amazon's satellite internet project integrating with AWS cloud for edge computing and IoT.

Plan:

  • Satellites: 3,236 planned
  • Orbit: 590/610/630km triple-shell
  • Launch: Blue Origin, ULA, Arianespace
  • Service: 2025-2026 target

Differentiation:

  • AWS integration: Direct satellite-to-AWS region access
  • Edge computing: Onboard satellite processing (future)
  • IoT optimization: Low-power protocols for millions of sensors
  • Amazon ecosystem: Prime discounts, Alexa integration

7.5 GEO HTS

Viasat:

  • Viasat-3: 1 Tbps/satellite, world's largest commercial comsat
  • Speed: 100 Mbps unlimited plans
  • Latency: 600-700 ms (GEO)
  • Strength: #1 aviation internet market share
  • Price: $70-150/month

HughesNet:

  • Jupiter 3: 500 Gbps capacity
  • Speed: 25-100 Mbps
  • Subscribers: 1.5M (2023)
  • Price: $50-150/month

7.6 Regional Projects

China Guowang:

  • Plan: 12,992 LEO satellites
  • Purpose: Sovereign Chinese satellite internet
  • Status: Test satellites from 2024

Europe IRIS²:

  • EU-led constellation
  • Purpose: European sovereignty, government secure comms
  • Budget: €6 billion

Canada Telesat Lightspeed:

  • Plan: 298 LEO satellites
  • Orbit: 1,000km MEO
  • Target: Arctic and Canadian remote areas

7.7 Economics

Cost Structure:

  • Satellite: $50-100k/unit (LEO smallsat), $300-500M (GEO HTS)
  • Launch: $10-30k/kg (Falcon 9 reuse)
  • Ground: Gateways, NOC, support
  • Terminal: $500-1,000 manufacturing (Starlink sells $499 at loss)
  • Operations: 5-7 year satellite life requires replacement

Revenue:

  • Consumer: $50-150/month subscriptions
  • Enterprise: $1,000s-10,000s/month leased lines
  • Government: Defense/disaster contracts
  • Telco: Wholesale backhaul

Break-even:

Starlink reportedly cash-flow positive above 1M subscribers. 2025 est. 2M subs = ~$3B annual revenue.

7.8 Social Impact

Digital Inclusion:

40% of global population (3B people) lack internet access, mostly in rural/developing regions. Satellites provide access to education, healthcare, economic opportunity.

Remote Education:

COVID pandemic made online learning standard. Students without internet lost educational opportunities. Satellites close this gap.

Telemedicine:

Rural areas gain video consultations, remote diagnostics, medical record access with specialists.

Disaster Response:

When terrestrial networks fail from earthquakes/hurricanes/floods, satellite terminals restore emergency communications within 24 hours.

Ukraine War Case:

Russia's invasion destroyed Ukrainian infrastructure. Starlink provided thousands of terminals maintaining government/military/civilian communications, proving satellites are national security assets.

7.9 Challenges

Space Debris:

Tens of thousands of satellites increase collision/debris risks. Starlink satellites auto-deorbit at end-of-life, but failures create debris.

Astronomy Interference:

Bright satellites disrupt telescopic observations. SpaceX added VisorSat sunshades as mitigation, but tensions with astronomy community persist.

Spectrum Congestion:

Thousands of satellites sharing frequency bands complicate ITU coordination and increase interference risks.

Geopolitical Tensions:

US corporate dominance of global satellite internet concerns China, Russia, EU, spurring sovereign constellations and new space race.

7.10 Conclusion

Satellite internet enters new era with Starlink, OneWeb, Kuiper LEO mega constellations. 20-40ms latency and 50-200 Mbps speeds approach fiber performance, bridging global digital divides. GEO HTS maintain niches in aviation/maritime despite latency/speed disadvantages. Future will see LEO-GEO coexistence, each leveraging unique strengths.
