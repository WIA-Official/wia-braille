CHAPTER 4

📡 Ground Equipment

4.1 Ground Segment Overview

Ground infrastructure includes gateways, VSAT, antennas, and TT&C systems connecting satellites to end users. Performance and cost of ground equipment critically determine overall system economics and quality.

4.2 Antenna Systems

Parabolic Reflector Antennas

  • Prime Focus: Feed at center front, high efficiency but feed blocks beam
  • Cassegrain: Primary + secondary reflector, feed at rear minimizes blockage
  • Offset: Feed off-center, used in consumer DTH dishes

Phased Array Antennas

Hundreds/thousands of small elements with electronic beam steering. No mechanical movement, essential for LEO satellite tracking (Starlink Dishy).

  • Active: TX/RX modules per element, high-performance, expensive
  • Passive: Phase shifters only, lower cost/performance
  • Advantages: Fast beam steering, multiple simultaneous beams, fault tolerance
  • Disadvantages: High cost, complexity, power consumption

4.3 VSAT (Very Small Aperture Terminal)

Small-antenna (0.6-2.4m) two-way satellite terminals. Standard for enterprise networks since 1980s.

Components:

  • ODU (Outdoor): Antenna, LNB, BUC, feed horn
  • IDU (Indoor): Modem, router, power supply
  • Hub: Central large earth station, internet backbone connection

Network Topologies:

  • Star: All VSAT via central hub. Common, easy management, double-hop latency
  • Mesh: Direct VSAT-to-VSAT. Lower latency, complex, expensive
  • Hybrid: Star + Mesh combination

Applications:

SectorUse
BankingATM, branch VPN
RetailPOS, inventory
EnergyPipeline, SCADA monitoring
TelecomMobile backhaul
GovernmentPolice, military communications

Modern VSAT:

  • Ka-band: 60cm for 50+ Mbps
  • HTS: 1/10 cost per Mbps
  • Flat panel: Vehicle/ship/aircraft mounting
  • DVB-S2X: 30% efficiency gain

4.4 Gateway Stations

Large earth stations (7-13m antennas) connecting satellites to internet backbone. Starlink uses 200+ gateways globally.

Components:

  • Large antenna: High EIRP and G/T
  • HPA: Multi-kW amplifiers
  • Multi-feed: Single antenna, multiple satellites
  • Backhaul: 10-100 Gbps fiber to internet
  • Baseband: Modulation, multiplexing, packet processing

Deployment Strategy:

  • Geographic distribution across continents
  • Site diversity: 50-100km spacing for rain fade
  • Near Internet Exchange Points
  • Reliable power and fiber access

4.5 TT&C (Telemetry, Tracking, Command)

Ground facilities monitoring and controlling satellites 24/7 from launch to end-of-life.

Functions:

  • Telemetry: Receive satellite health data (temperature, voltage, fuel, attitude)
  • Tracking: Precise position/velocity measurement
  • Command: Transmit control commands

Frequency:

  • S-band: 2.0-2.3 GHz (most common)
  • X-band: Military, deep space
  • Ka-band: Next-gen HTS

Orbit Determination:

  • Ranging: Round-trip time for distance (meter accuracy)
  • Doppler: Frequency shift for velocity
  • Angle: Antenna pointing for direction
  • Propagation: Predict future orbits considering gravity, solar wind, drag

Station Keeping (GEO):

  • East-West: ±0.05° drift correction
  • North-South: Inclination correction (most fuel-intensive)
  • Frequency: Every few days/weeks
  • Life limit: Fuel depletion prevents orbit maintenance

4.6 User Terminals

Satellite Phones:

  • Iridium: LEO, global, handheld
  • Inmarsat: GEO, maritime/aviation
  • Speed: Voice + low data (2.4-128 kbps)
  • Cost: $500-1,500 terminal, $1-3/min calls

Satellite Internet:

  • Starlink Dishy: Phased array, auto-tracking, 50-200 Mbps, $99/mo
  • OneWeb: Enterprise, 150-300 Mbps
  • HughesNet/Viasat: GEO, 25-100 Mbps

Satellite TV:

  • 45-60cm offset dish
  • LNB: Ku-band to IF (950-2150 MHz)
  • STB: DVB-S2 demodulation, MPEG-4 decoding

Mobile Terminals:

  • Automotive: Auto-tracking flat panel
  • Aviation: In-flight WiFi, Ka-band
  • Maritime: VSAT, FleetBroadband
  • Portable: BGAN backpack modems

4.7 RF Equipment

LNB (Low Noise Block Converter):

  • Amplifies weak satellite signals with ultra-low noise (0.3-0.8 dB)
  • Downconverts Ku-band (10.7-12.75 GHz) to IF (950-2150 MHz)
  • Types: Single, Dual, Quad outputs

BUC (Block Up Converter):

  • Upconverts indoor modem IF to satellite uplink frequency
  • Input: L-band (950-1750 MHz)
  • Output: C/Ku/Ka-band, 1-20W

HPA (High Power Amplifier):

  • SSPA (Solid State): 100W-1kW, reliable
  • TWTA (Traveling Wave Tube): 1-10kW, high power
  • Requires cooling (forced air/water)

4.8 Conclusion

Ground equipment forms the critical interface between space and terrestrial networks. Recent advances in Ka-band HTS and phased arrays enable dramatic miniaturization and cost reduction.
