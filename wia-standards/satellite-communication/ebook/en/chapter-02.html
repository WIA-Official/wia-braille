🛰️ Communication Satellite Systems

2.1 Orbital Classification

Communication satellites are classified by altitude: GEO (Geostationary Earth Orbit), MEO (Medium Earth Orbit), and LEO (Low Earth Orbit). Each orbit has unique characteristics and optimal applications.

2.2 GEO (Geostationary Orbit)

GEO satellites at 35,786 km altitude orbit with Earth's 24-hour rotation period, appearing stationary from ground. Proposed by Arthur C. Clarke in 1945, also called Clarke Orbit.

Advantages:

  • Fixed Antenna: Ground antennas don't need tracking systems
  • Wide Coverage: Single satellite covers 1/3 of Earth
  • Few Satellites: Three satellites cover entire globe (except poles)
  • Continuous Service: 24/7 connectivity without interruption

Disadvantages:

  • High Latency: 240-280 ms round-trip delay
  • High Power: Long distance requires high transmission power
  • Polar Limitation: Low elevation angles at high latitudes
  • Launch Cost: Expensive to reach high orbit

Major GEO Satellites:

  • Intelsat series (global communications)
  • Viasat-3 (1 Tbps capacity broadband)
  • GOES, Meteosat (weather observation)
  • Korea's Mugunghwa, Chollian satellites

2.3 MEO (Medium Earth Orbit)

MEO satellites operate between 2,000-35,786 km, primarily used for navigation (GPS, Galileo, GLONASS) with some communication applications.

GPS Constellation:

  • Altitude: 20,200 km
  • Period: 11 hours 58 minutes
  • 24+ satellites for global coverage
  • Precise positioning and timing

MEO Communication:

  • SES O3b: 20 satellites at 8,000 km for maritime/aviation
  • O3b mPOWER: Next-gen with 5,000+ beams per satellite
  • Balance between GEO coverage and LEO latency

2.4 LEO (Low Earth Orbit)

LEO satellites at 160-2,000 km altitude complete orbits in 90-120 minutes. Recent mega constellations (Starlink, OneWeb) have revolutionized satellite internet.

LEO Characteristics:

  • Low Latency: 20-40 ms, approaching fiber optic performance
  • Low Power: Shorter distance enables smaller, cheaper terminals
  • Small Satellites: Smartphone-sized terminals possible
  • Many Satellites: Hundreds to thousands needed for global coverage
  • Short Lifespan: 5-7 years due to atmospheric drag

Mega Constellations:

Starlink (SpaceX):

  • 5,000+ satellites operational (2025)
  • Target: 42,000 satellites
  • Altitude: 340 km, 550 km shells
  • Speed: 50-200 Mbps download
  • Service: 60+ countries, 2M+ subscribers

OneWeb:

  • 648 satellites complete
  • Altitude: 1,200 km polar orbit
  • Focus: Enterprise, government, aviation

Amazon Kuiper:

  • Planned: 3,236 satellites
  • Altitude: 590-630 km
  • Integration with AWS cloud

Inter-Satellite Links (ISL):

Next-gen LEO satellites use laser ISL for satellite-to-satellite communication at 100 Gbps, enabling global data relay without ground stations and faster intercontinental routes than submarine cables.

2.5 Hybrid Systems

Future satellite networks will integrate GEO, MEO, and LEO for optimized services:

  • GEO: Broadcasting, weather, backbone traffic
  • MEO: Navigation, medium-latency comms
  • LEO: High-speed internet, mobile, real-time services
5G NTN: 5G standards integrate satellites, enabling smartphones to seamlessly switch between terrestrial and satellite networks for ubiquitous connectivity.

2.6 Conclusion

Satellite systems are classified by orbital altitude as GEO, MEO, and LEO, each with unique characteristics. GEO dominated for 60 years despite high latency and costs. MEO serves primarily navigation with emerging communication roles. LEO mega constellations represent a renaissance in satellite communications, offering fiber-like latency and global coverage.
