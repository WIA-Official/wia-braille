Chapter 3: Avatar Standards & Identity

Your Digital Self

Your avatar is your presence in the metaverse - your visual representation, your identity, your digital body. The WIA standard ensures that your avatar can travel with you across all virtual worlds while maintaining consistency and customization.

Universal Avatar Format

WIA-EDU-018 supports multiple avatar formats with VRM as the primary standard:

VRM (Virtual Reality Model)

The VRM format is designed specifically for avatars with built-in support for:

  • Humanoid rigging (bones, joints, proportions)
  • Facial expressions and lip-sync
  • Eye tracking and gaze
  • Spring bones for hair and clothing physics
  • Material properties and shaders
  • Metadata and licensing information

Alternative Formats

Additional supported formats include:

  • glTF 2.0: General 3D models with humanoid extensions
  • USD (Universal Scene Description): For complex, multi-component avatars
  • FBX: Legacy support for existing content

Avatar Customization

Body Types

Support for diverse representations:

  • Realistic human proportions
  • Stylized and cartoon characters
  • Abstract and artistic forms
  • Non-humanoid creatures
  • Accessibility-focused designs

Persistent Identity

Decentralized Identifiers (DIDs)

Each user has a unique, portable identity:

  • Self-sovereign - you control your identity
  • Privacy-preserving - share only what you choose
  • Verifiable credentials - trusted attributes
  • Cross-platform - works everywhere

Reputation System

Build reputation that travels with you:

  • Achievements and badges
  • Community endorsements
  • Skill certifications
  • Participation history
  • Creator credentials

Accessories and Wearables

NFT Integration

Digital fashion and accessories as verifiable assets:

  • Unique, limited-edition items
  • Blockchain-verified ownership
  • Cross-platform compatibility
  • Creator royalties on resale
  • Authenticated luxury brands

Dynamic Wearables

Items that respond to context:

  • Weather-reactive clothing
  • Animated accessories
  • Context-aware displays (badges, titles)
  • Interactive props and tools

Animation and Expression

Standard Animation Set

Core animations for interoperability:

  • Locomotion: idle, walk, run, jump
  • Gestures: wave, point, thumbs up, clap
  • Emotions: happy, sad, surprised, thinking
  • Social: handshake, hug, high five
  • Actions: sit, dance, celebrate

Facial Expressions

Rich emotional communication:

  • 52 blend shapes for facial animation
  • Automatic lip-sync from speech
  • Eye blinking and gaze direction
  • Eyebrow and mouth expressions
  • VR headset tracking integration

Privacy and Control

Appearance Privacy

Control how you appear to others:

  • Private mode - generic avatar for anonymity
  • Friend-only customization visibility
  • Context-specific appearances
  • Age-appropriate filtering

Personal Boundaries

Safety features built into avatar interactions:

  • Personal space bubbles
  • Touch/interaction permissions
  • Blocking and muting
  • Safe zones and timeout spaces

Technical Specifications

Performance Guidelines

  • Polygon Count: 15,000-30,000 triangles
  • Texture Size: 2K-4K resolution
  • Bone Count: Maximum 100 bones
  • Material Count: 5-10 materials
  • File Size: Target <50MB

The goal: Your avatar is uniquely you, recognizable across all platforms, and expressive enough to convey your personality and emotions in the digital realm.
