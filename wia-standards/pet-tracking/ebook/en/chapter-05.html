5.1 Lost Pet Detection

Early detection of a lost pet situation dramatically improves recovery chances. The WIA-PET-TRACKING standard defines automated detection mechanisms and manual activation protocols.

Automated Lost Pet Detection Triggers

Trigger Condition Confidence Level Automatic Action
Geofence violation + no return for 30+ minutes Medium Send alert to owner
Geofence violation + no return for 2+ hours High Suggest activating lost mode
Distance from home > 5 km unexpectedly High Immediate alert + location
Continuous movement away from home for >1 hour Medium-High Alert with trajectory map
Location in unfamiliar area (>10 km from any known zone) High Alert + recovery resources
Owner manually activates lost pet mode Confirmed Activate full recovery protocol

Sighting Verification Algorithm

SIGHTING PLAUSIBILITY CHECK
============================

Given:
  Last confirmed location: (lat1, lng1, time1)
  Reported sighting: (lat2, lng2, time2)

Calculate:
  distance = haversine(lat1, lng1, lat2, lng2)
  timeElapsed = time2 - time1 (in hours)

  maxWalkingSpeed = 5 km/h (dog walking/running)
  maxDriveSpeed = 60 km/h (if pet in vehicle)

Checks:
  1. Distance Plausibility:
     IF distance <= maxWalkingSpeed × timeElapsed:
       plausibility = "high"
     ELSE IF distance <= maxDriveSpeed × timeElapsed:
       plausibility = "medium" (may have been transported)
     ELSE:
       plausibility = "low" (unlikely unless error)

  2. Direction Consistency:
     Calculate bearing from last location
     IF consistent with known escape route:
       +0.2 to confidence
     IF opposite direction from searches:
       -0.1 to confidence

  3. Photo Matching (if available):
     Run image recognition on submitted photo
     Compare with pet's profile photos
     matchScore = 0.0 to 1.0
     IF matchScore > 0.8:
       High confidence match

  4. Reporter Credibility:
     Check reporter's history:
       - Previous accurate reports: +0.1
       - False positives: -0.2
       - First-time reporter: neutral

Final Confidence Score:
  High: > 0.75 → Alert owner immediately, high priority
  Medium: 0.50-0.75 → Alert owner, moderate priority
  Low: < 0.50 → Log for reference, low priority

Example:
  Last seen: 10:00 AM at (37.7749, -122.4194)
  Sighting: 11:45 AM at (37.7810, -122.4105) [~0.9 km away]
  Time elapsed: 1.75 hours
  Max plausible distance: 5 km/h × 1.75 h = 8.75 km
  Actual distance: 0.9 km ✓ Plausible
  Photo match: 0.92 ✓ High confidence
  → Final score: 0.9 (HIGH confidence)

5.5 Lost Pet Alert Distribution

Multi-channel alert distribution maximizes the chances that someone who sees the pet will recognize it and report the sighting.

Distribution Channels

ALERT DISTRIBUTION MATRIX
=========================

Channel 1: Mobile App Push Notifications
  Target: App users within search radius
  Reach: 500-5,000 users (depending on area density)
  Delivery: Instant (<5 seconds)
  Engagement: 30-40% open rate

Channel 2: Email Alerts
  Target: Registered community members
  Reach: 200-2,000 users
  Delivery: 1-5 minutes
  Engagement: 15-25% open rate

Channel 3: SMS/Text Alerts
  Target: Opted-in emergency contacts
  Reach: 50-500 users
  Delivery: Instant
  Engagement: 80-90% open rate

Channel 4: Social Media Posts
  Platforms: Facebook, Twitter, Instagram, Nextdoor
  Target: Local community groups, pet groups
  Reach: 1,000-100,000+ users (depending on shares)
  Delivery: Minutes to hours (algorithm dependent)
  Engagement: 5-15% engagement rate, high viral potential

Channel 5: Lost Pet Databases
  Examples: PetAmberAlert, Fido Finder, local databases
  Target: Shelters, vets, rescue organizations
  Reach: 100-1,000 organizations
  Delivery: Minutes to hours
  Engagement: High for professionals

Channel 6: Digital Signage
  Target: Pet stores, vet clinics, dog parks
  Reach: 500-5,000 daily views
  Delivery: Hours (requires approval)
  Engagement: Moderate awareness

Channel 7: Physical Flyers
  Target: Neighborhood bulletin boards, poles
  Reach: Local area only
  Delivery: Manual distribution
  Engagement: Traditional but effective

5.7 Success Metrics and Recovery Statistics

Tracking recovery outcomes helps improve the system and provides valuable insights to the pet community.

Recovery Time Analysis

Recovery Timeframe Success Rate Primary Factor
< 2 hours 85-95% Pet still in immediate area, rapid response
2-6 hours 70-80% Community sightings, active tracking
6-24 hours 50-65% Extended search, social media reach
1-3 days 35-50% Shelter pickup, distant sightings
3-7 days 20-35% Persistent search, luck, microchip scans
> 7 days 10-25% Microchip, distant shelter, Good Samaritan

Recovery Success Factors

FACTORS CORRELATING WITH SUCCESSFUL RECOVERY
=============================================

Positive Factors:
  ✓ GPS tracker with active battery (+60% success rate)
  ✓ Immediate lost pet mode activation (+45%)
  ✓ Microchip registration up to date (+40%)
  ✓ Friendly, sociable pet temperament (+35%)
  ✓ Community engagement (sightings, searches) (+50%)
  ✓ Distinctive appearance or markings (+30%)
  ✓ Wearing collar with tags (+25%)
  ✓ Dense urban/suburban area (+20%)
  ✓ Good weather conditions (+15%)
  ✓ Daytime loss vs. nighttime (+15%)

Negative Factors:
  ✗ Timid or fearful pet temperament (-30%)
  ✗ Rural/wilderness area (-25%)
  ✗ Nighttime loss (-20%)
  ✗ Severe weather (storms, extreme heat/cold) (-25%)
  ✗ Multiple days before reporting (-40%)
  ✗ No identification (no chip, tags, tracker) (-50%)
  ✗ Pet transported by vehicle (-35%)

Example Calculation:
  Baseline recovery probability: 65%
  + GPS tracker active: +60% → 125% (cap at 95%)
  + Immediate activation: already in GPS benefit
  + Urban area: +20% → bounded at 95%
  + Daytime: +15% → bounded at 95%
  - Timid temperament: -30% → 65%

  Estimated Recovery Probability: ~65-75%

5.8 Post-Recovery Protocol

After successful recovery, important steps ensure the pet's wellbeing and help prevent future incidents.

Recovery Checklist

POST-RECOVERY ACTIONS
=====================

Immediate (0-2 hours after recovery):
  □ Mark alert as "FOUND" in system
  □ Notify all participants and thank them
  □ Veterinary health check (recommended)
  □ Provide food, water, and rest
  □ Document any injuries or issues
  □ Take photo confirming safe recovery

Within 24 Hours:
  □ Review tracker data to understand escape route
  □ Identify how pet escaped (gate, fence, door)
  □ Make necessary repairs or improvements
  □ Update geofences if needed
  □ Share recovery story (optional, helps community)
  □ Consider reward fulfillment for finder

Within 1 Week:
  □ Analyze escape causes (boredom, fear, chase)
  □ Implement preventive measures
  □ Consider training if behavioral issues
  □ Review and update emergency contacts
  □ Ensure microchip registration current
  □ Backup tracker data for reference

Optional:
  □ Contribute to lost pet statistics (anonymous)
  □ Share learnings with community
  □ Update pet insurance if claimed
  □ Consider additional safety measures

Chapter Summary

Key Takeaways:

  1. Early Detection: Automated triggers based on geofence violations and location anomalies can detect lost pet situations within minutes, dramatically improving recovery chances.
  2. Network Effect: Multi-channel alert distribution to community members, shelters, vets, and services creates a powerful network for sightings and recovery.
  3. Verification: Sighting reports should be algorithmically verified for plausibility based on distance, time, and photo matching to reduce false positives.
  4. Coordination: Grid-based search coordination prevents duplication and ensures systematic coverage of the search area.
  5. Time is Critical: Recovery success drops significantly after the first few hours; systems should prioritize rapid activation and response.
