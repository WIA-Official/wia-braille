🏭 Chapter 7: Applications and Use Cases

"Industrial robots are not just machines - they are enablers of innovation across every manufacturing sector."

Understanding real-world applications helps engineers select the right robot and design effective automation solutions.

7.1 Material Handling and Palletizing

Material handling represents 38% of all industrial robot applications, making it the single largest use case.

7.1.1 Pick and Place Operations

Typical Characteristics:

  • Cycle Time: 0.5-3 seconds per pick
  • Accuracy: ±0.5 mm to ±2 mm
  • Payload: 1 kg to 100 kg
  • Robot Types: SCARA (small parts), delta (high-speed), articulated (versatile)

Common Scenarios:

  • PCB component placement: >200 picks/minute with vision guidance
  • Pharmaceutical packaging: Clean room operation, 100% traceability
  • Food sorting: Hygienic design, washdown capability (IP67)
  • E-commerce order fulfillment: Vision-guided bin picking, variable parts

7.1.2 Palletizing

Requirements:

  • Reach: 2000-3500 mm (to reach top of pallet)
  • Payload: 50-300 kg per box
  • Speed: 10-15 cycles/minute
  • Pattern Generation: Software calculates stable stacking patterns

Economic Benefits:

Metric Manual Palletizing Robot Palletizing Improvement
Boxes/hour 120-150 600-900 5-6x faster
Accuracy ±10 mm ±2 mm 5x better
Injury Risk High (back strain) None Eliminates injuries
Flexibility Low (retraining needed) High (program change) Fast changeover
Labor Cost/year $60,000-$80,000 $15,000 (maintenance) 75% reduction

ROI Calculation Example:

Robot System Cost: $120,000 (robot + gripper + integration)
Annual Savings: $65,000 (labor cost difference)
Payback Period: 1.8 years
5-Year ROI: 270%

7.1.3 Machine Tending

Applications:

  • CNC machine loading/unloading
  • Injection molding part removal
  • Die casting extraction
  • 3D printer bed loading
  • Heat treatment furnace loading

Benefits Over Manual Tending:

  • Utilization: Machines run during breaks and night shifts (70% → 95% utilization)
  • Consistency: Same load/unload cycle every time
  • Quality: Reduced part damage from handling
  • Safety: Operators removed from hot/sharp parts

7.2 Welding and Assembly

7.2.1 Arc Welding

Process Parameters:

  • Speed: 5-15 mm/s (MIG/MAG), 3-8 mm/s (TIG)
  • Path Accuracy: ±0.5 mm
  • Torch Angle Control: ±1 degree
  • Seam Tracking: Laser or vision-based, compensates for part variation

Welding Processes Automated:

  • GMAW (MIG/MAG): Most common, versatile, automotive body shops
  • GTAW (TIG): High quality, aerospace, thin materials
  • Resistance Spot Welding: Automotive, high-speed (60-100 spots/minute)
  • Laser Welding: Precision, minimal heat-affected zone, electronics

Quality Metrics:

Metric Manual Welding Robot Welding
Weld Quality Consistency 70-85% 95-99%
Deposition Rate 60-70% duty cycle 85-95% duty cycle
Material Waste 15-25% 5-10%
Rework Rate 5-10% <2%

7.2.2 Assembly Operations

Precision Assembly:

  • Screw Driving: Vision-guided hole location, torque control
  • Snap-Fit Assembly: Force control to detect proper insertion
  • Adhesive Dispensing: Consistent bead width and placement
  • Press Fitting: Force/position monitoring, quality verification

Electronics Assembly Example:

Product: Smartphone Assembly
Operations:
  1. Component placement on PCB (SCARA robot, 0.05mm accuracy)
  2. Adhesive dispensing for screen bonding
  3. Screen press-fit with force monitoring (<100 N limit)
  4. Camera module insertion (dual-robot coordination)
  5. Screw driving (4 screws, 0.5 Nm torque verification)
  6. Final inspection (vision system, 18 checkpoints)

Cycle Time: 42 seconds
Quality: 99.7% first-pass yield
Production: 2,000 units/day (dual-robot cell)

7.3 Painting and Coating

7.3.1 Spray Painting Robots

Specialized Requirements:

  • Explosion-Proof Design: ATEX/IECEx certification for flammable paint vapors
  • Paint-Resistant Materials: Sealed motors, special coatings
  • Hollow Wrist: Paint hoses routed through robot arm
  • High Speed: Up to 2 m/s TCP speed for large parts

Process Control:

  • Flow Rate: Proportional to TCP speed (constant film thickness)
  • Gun Orientation: Perpendicular to surface (±5 degrees)
  • Standoff Distance: 200-300 mm (constant)
  • Path Planning: Overlapping spray patterns for uniform coverage

Benefits:

Aspect Manual Painting Robot Painting
Paint Transfer Efficiency 35-55% 75-90%
Film Thickness Consistency ±30% ±5%
Material Waste 45-65% 10-25%
Rework Rate 8-15% 1-3%
Worker Health Exposure High VOC exposure Eliminated

7.3.2 Coating and Sealing

Applications:

  • Underbody Sealing: Automotive, corrosion protection
  • Adhesive Application: Windshield bonding, structural bonding
  • Conformal Coating: PCB protection, electronics
  • Powder Coating: Electrostatic spray, metal finishing

7.4 Quality Inspection and Testing

7.4.1 Vision-Based Inspection

Inspection Tasks:

  • Dimensional Measurement: Part dimensions, hole locations, flatness
  • Presence/Absence: Component verification, completeness check
  • Surface Defects: Scratches, dents, discoloration
  • Label Verification: OCR of serial numbers, barcode reading
  • Color Matching: Paint color consistency, sorting by color

Typical Accuracy:

Measurement Type Accuracy Speed
2D Dimension (edge-to-edge) ±0.01 mm (telecentric lens) 100-200 ms
3D Height Measurement ±0.05 mm (laser triangulation) 500 ms-2 s
Color Verification ΔE < 2 50-100 ms
Defect Detection (AI-based) 98-99.9% detection rate 200-500 ms

7.4.2 Coordinate Measuring Machine (CMM) Loading

Process:

  1. Robot retrieves part from production line
  2. Places part in CMM fixture with precise alignment (±0.02 mm)
  3. CMM performs automated measurement program
  4. Robot removes part, sorts by measurement result (pass/fail/rework)
  5. Data transmitted to quality database

Benefits:

  • Throughput: 100% inspection vs. 5-10% sampling
  • Traceability: Every part measured, data linked to serial number
  • Continuous Operation: 24/7 lights-out inspection

7.5 Collaborative Applications

7.5.1 Human-Robot Collaboration Scenarios

Hand-in-Hand Applications:

  • Finishing Operations: Human sands rough areas, robot sands large flat surfaces
  • Flexible Assembly: Robot holds large part, human installs small components
  • Quality Inspection: Robot positions part, human performs visual check
  • Packaging: Robot performs repetitive tasks, human handles irregular items

Case Study: Electronics Assembly

Company: Mid-sized electronics manufacturer
Product: Custom control panels (>500 variants)
Previous: Manual assembly, 12 minutes/panel
Solution: UR10e cobot + human collaboration
Tasks:
  - Robot: PCB placement, screw driving (8 screws)
  - Human: Connector crimping, wire routing, final inspection

Results:
  - Cycle time: 8 minutes/panel (-33%)
  - Quality: 99.2% first-pass yield (was 94%)
  - Ergonomics: Eliminated repetitive screw driving
  - Flexibility: Program change in <5 minutes for variants
  - ROI: 14 months payback

7.5.2 Safety Implementation

Layered Safety Approach:

  1. Inherent Safety: Cobot with force limiting (150 N body, 65 N head)
  2. Speed Monitoring: 250 mm/s maximum in collaborative zone
  3. Workspace Zones:
    • Zone 1 (operator workspace): Cobot speed 100 mm/s
    • Zone 2 (shared workspace): Cobot speed 200 mm/s
    • Zone 3 (robot-only): Full speed (1000 mm/s)
  4. Laser Scanner: Monitors zones, adjusts speed dynamically
  5. Emergency Stop: Accessible within 1 meter of cobot

7.6 Summary

This chapter explored real-world industrial robot applications:

  • Material Handling: Pick-and-place, palletizing, machine tending (38% of market)
  • Welding: Arc welding, spot welding with seam tracking and quality monitoring
  • Assembly: Precision operations with force control and vision guidance
  • Painting: High transfer efficiency, consistent quality, worker safety
  • Inspection: 100% quality verification with vision and CMM integration
  • Collaborative: Human-robot teaming for flexible manufacturing

Key Takeaways:

  • Robot selection must match application requirements (speed, accuracy, payload)
  • ROI typically achieved in 1-3 years with proper implementation
  • Quality and consistency improvements often exceed labor savings
  • Collaborative robots enable automation of previously manual-only tasks
  • Integration with vision and force sensors unlocks adaptive capabilities

7.7 Advanced Manufacturing Applications

7.7.1 Additive Manufacturing Integration

3D Printing with Robots:

  • Large-Format Printing: Robot-mounted extruder for >1 meter parts
  • Multi-Material Printing: Automatic tool changer for different materials
  • Hybrid Manufacturing: Combined additive + subtractive (milling)

Benefits:

Aspect Traditional 3D Printer Robot-Based 3D Printing
Build Volume 300x300x300 mm (typical) 2500x2500x2000 mm+
Multi-Axis Capability 3-axis (X,Y,Z) 5-6 axis (complex geometries)
Material Handling Manual changeover Automated tool changer
Part Complexity Layer-by-layer only Curved layers, overhangs without supports

7.7.2 Cleanroom and Sterile Applications

Pharmaceutical Manufacturing:

  • Vial Filling: Aseptic processing, 100% container closure integrity
  • Syringe Assembly: Pre-filled syringes, automated needle insertion
  • Lyophilization Loading: Freeze-drying tray loading, contamination prevention

Cleanroom Robot Requirements:

  • Stainless steel construction (316L grade)
  • Sealed motors and electronics
  • Low particle generation (<100 particles/ft³)
  • ISO Class 5 compatibility
  • H₂O₂ vaporization resistance (sterilization)
  • Validation per FDA 21 CFR Part 11

7.7.3 Semiconductor and Electronics

Wafer Handling:

  • Wafer Transfer: Between process chambers, <0.01 mm precision
  • Inspection Loading: Automated optical inspection (AOI)
  • Die Bonding: Pick-and-place with vision alignment
  • Wire Bonding: High-speed gold/aluminum wire attachment

Precision Requirements:

Operation Position Accuracy Repeatability Speed
Wafer Handling (300mm) ±0.05 mm ±0.01 mm 2-3 wafers/minute
Die Bonding ±0.005 mm ±0.002 mm 5,000-10,000 UPH
Wire Bonding ±0.001 mm ±0.0005 mm 10-15 bonds/second
Chip Placement (SMT) ±0.02 mm ±0.01 mm 25,000-40,000 CPH

7.8 Industry-Specific Case Studies

7.8.1 Automotive: Tesla Gigafactory

Deployment Scale:

  • ~1,000 robots per factory
  • Mix of ABB, KUKA, FANUC systems
  • Fully automated battery pack assembly
  • Model changeover in <1 minute (flexible tooling)

Innovation Highlights:

  • Adaptive Welding: Real-time seam tracking with laser vision
  • Multi-Robot Coordination: Up to 8 robots work on single vehicle simultaneously
  • Quality Control: Every weld inspected via vision + ultrasound
  • Cycle Time: 45 seconds per vehicle station (industry-leading)

7.8.2 Aerospace: Boeing 777X Wing Assembly

Challenge: Automated drilling and fastening of composite wing structures (85 meters long).

Solution:

  • Robots: KUKA KR 1000 Titan (1,000 kg payload) with AFAG drilling end-effector
  • Accuracy: ±0.125 mm hole position over 30+ meter wing
  • Sensors: Laser tracker for absolute positioning, contact probe for surface verification
  • Cycle Time: 60,000 fasteners per wing, 50% faster than manual
  • Quality: 99.98% first-time quality (vs. 97% manual)

7.8.3 E-Commerce: Amazon Fulfillment

Robotic Systems:

  • Kiva Robots (now Amazon Robotics): 200,000+ mobile robots across facilities
  • Robotic Arms: Cardinal system for package handling up to 50 lbs
  • Palletizing: Automated pallet building for outbound trucks

Performance:

  • 1 million packages per day per facility
  • Order fulfillment time: <15 minutes (was 60-75 minutes)
  • Inventory density: 50% increase (compact storage with robot access)
  • Employee productivity: 2-3x increase (robots bring items to pickers)

7.9.2 Payback Calculation

Example: Palletizing Application

Scenario: Replace 2 manual palletizers (one per shift)

Costs:
  Robot System: $180,000 (robot + gripper + safety + integration)
  Annual Operating: $18,000/year

Savings:
  Labor: 2 workers × $55,000/year = $110,000/year
  Reduced Injuries: $8,000/year (estimated workers comp savings)
  Quality Improvements: $12,000/year (reduced damage from mishandling)
  Increased Throughput: $25,000/year (faster cycle time enables more production)
  Total Annual Savings: $155,000/year

Payback Calculation:
  Net Annual Benefit: $155,000 - $18,000 = $137,000
  Simple Payback: $180,000 / $137,000 = 1.31 years (15.7 months)

5-Year Analysis:
  Total Investment: $180,000 + (5 × $18,000) = $270,000
  Total Savings: 5 × $155,000 = $775,000
  Net Benefit: $505,000
  ROI: ($505,000 / $270,000) × 100% = 187%
