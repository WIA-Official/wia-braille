Data Quality and Governance Integration

Master data management and data governance are deeply intertwined. While MDM provides the technology platform for managing critical business data, data governance establishes the policies, processes, and organizational structures that ensure data quality, compliance, and effective usage. This chapter explores how to integrate data quality management and governance frameworks into your MDM implementation.

Data Quality Dimensions

Data quality is measured across multiple dimensions. Understanding these dimensions helps you establish appropriate quality rules and metrics:

DimensionDefinitionExample Metric
AccuracyData correctly represents real-world entity% of addresses validated against postal database
CompletenessAll required attributes are populated% of customer records with email address
ConsistencyData is uniform across systems% of customers with matching name across CRM and ERP
TimelinessData is up-to-date and available when neededAverage age of customer address data
ValidityData conforms to defined formats and rules% of phone numbers in E.164 format
UniquenessNo unwanted duplicates exist% of duplicate customer records

Data Quality Rules

MDM systems enforce data quality through validation rules applied at various stages:

Format Validation Rules

  • Email format: Must match RFC 5322 email specification
  • Phone numbers: Must follow E.164 international format (+[country][area][number])
  • Dates: Must be valid dates in ISO 8601 format (YYYY-MM-DD)
  • Postal codes: Must match country-specific format (US: 99999 or 99999-9999)

Business Logic Validation

  • Age validation: Customer must be 18+ for certain products
  • Credit limits: Cannot exceed maximum based on customer tier
  • Geographic constraints: City must be in specified state/province
  • Referential integrity: Foreign keys must reference valid parent records

Governance Workflows

MDM platforms provide workflow capabilities to route data issues and changes through appropriate approval processes:

Exception Handling Workflow

  • Detection: Data quality rule violation detected
  • Classification: Issue categorized by severity (critical, high, medium, low)
  • Routing: Assigned to appropriate steward based on domain and issue type
  • Investigation: Steward researches root cause
  • Resolution: Steward corrects data or updates rules
  • Verification: System confirms issue resolved
  • Documentation: Resolution logged for audit trail

Data Quality Scorecards

Scorecards provide visibility into data quality metrics across domains, systems, and time periods. A comprehensive scorecard includes:

  • Overall quality score: Composite metric across all dimensions
  • Dimension scores: Individual scores for accuracy, completeness, etc.
  • Trend analysis: Quality improvement or degradation over time
  • Source system comparison: Which systems provide highest quality data
  • Domain breakdown: Quality scores by business domain
  • Critical field focus: Detailed metrics for most important attributes

Compliance and Regulatory Alignment

MDM plays a critical role in regulatory compliance by ensuring data accuracy, traceability, and appropriate access controls:

GDPR Compliance

  • Right to access: Provide customers with all data held about them
  • Right to rectification: Allow customers to correct inaccurate data
  • Right to erasure: Delete customer data upon request (right to be forgotten)
  • Data portability: Export customer data in machine-readable format
  • Consent management: Track and honor consent preferences

Financial Regulations (SOX, Basel III)

  • Audit trails: Complete history of all changes to financial master data
  • Segregation of duties: Different users for data entry vs. approval
  • Access controls: Role-based permissions for sensitive financial data
  • Data lineage: Track origin and transformation of financial metrics

Data Governance Framework

A comprehensive data governance framework for MDM includes:

Key Framework Components:
  • Policies: High-level principles governing data management
  • Standards: Specific requirements for data formats, quality, security
  • Procedures: Step-by-step processes for common activities
  • Roles & Responsibilities: Clear assignment of data ownership and accountability
  • Metrics & KPIs: Measurable indicators of data quality and governance effectiveness
  • Tools & Technology: MDM platform, data quality tools, workflow systems

Continuous Improvement

Data quality and governance are not one-time initiatives but ongoing processes requiring continuous monitoring and improvement:

  • Regular quality assessments: Periodic data profiling and quality measurements
  • Root cause analysis: Investigate recurring quality issues to fix at the source
  • Process optimization: Streamline stewardship workflows based on feedback
  • Training and enablement: Ensure stewards have skills and tools needed
  • Technology evolution: Adopt new capabilities (AI/ML) as they mature

By integrating robust data quality management and governance practices into your MDM implementation, you create a sustainable foundation for trusted, high-quality master data that drives business value.
