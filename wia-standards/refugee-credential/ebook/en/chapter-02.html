Chapter 2: Current Challenges in Credential Recognition

Hongik Ingan

"Benefit all humanity"

The current system fails millions. Documents are destroyed in war. Universities are bombed. Government offices burn. Yet the skills in people's hands and knowledge in their minds remain intact.

2.1 The Lost Documentation Problem

When people flee conflict or disaster, documents are often the last priority:

How Documents Are Lost

Cause Percentage Example
Left Behind 45% Fleeing at night with only essentials
Destroyed 25% Home bombed, documents burned
Confiscated 15% Taken at checkpoints or borders
Water Damaged 10% Sea crossings, flooding
Stolen 5% By smugglers or thieves

[!] Warning: Even when documents survive, they may be unusable - damaged, in a language unrecognized, or from institutions that no longer exist.

Compounding Factors

  • Institution Destruction - Universities and government offices destroyed
  • Record System Collapse - Digital records lost, backups destroyed
  • Staff Displacement - People who could verify are also refugees
  • Political Barriers - Home country won't cooperate with verification

2.2 Verification Without Documents

Current verification methods assume institutional cooperation:

Traditional Verification Flow

Traditional Process:
1. Applicant provides documents
2. Verifier contacts issuing institution
3. Institution confirms authenticity
4. Verification complete

[!] FAILURE POINTS for Refugees:
- Step 1: No documents to provide
- Step 3: Institution destroyed or uncooperative
- Result: Verification impossible

Current Workarounds (Inadequate)

Method Problems
Sworn Statements Easily falsified, no confidence measure
Portfolio Review Subjective, time-consuming, inconsistent
Complete Re-education Wasteful, demeaning, years of delay
Blanket Recognition Fraud risk, undermines trust

[i] Note: None of these approaches provide a standardized, progressive confidence measure.

2.3 Trust and Authentication

Verification systems rely on trust chains that break for refugees:

Broken Trust Chains

Normal Trust Chain:
[Employer] trusts [Verification Service]
    trusts [Ministry of Education]
        trusts [University]
            issued [Diploma]

Refugee Trust Chain:
[Employer] trusts [Verification Service]
    trusts [Ministry of Education]
        Cannot contact [Syrian University - destroyed]
            Cannot verify [Lost Diploma]

Authentication Challenges

Challenge Description
No Biometric Link Old documents have no biometric verification
Name Transliteration Arabic/Cyrillic names spelled differently
Date Format Confusion Different calendar systems
Fraudulent Documents Black market for fake credentials

2.4 Language and Cultural Barriers

Credential recognition faces significant linguistic challenges:

Translation Problems

  • Technical Terminology - Medical/engineering terms don't translate directly
  • Degree Equivalence - What is a "Kandidat Nauk" or "Diplom"?
  • Grading Systems - Different scales, different meanings
  • Institution Names - Universities renamed, merged, or split

Supported Languages in WIA Standard

Priority Languages Coverage
Tier 1 Arabic, Ukrainian, Farsi, Pashto, Tigrinya 60% of refugees
Tier 2 French, Spanish, Swahili, Dari, Rohingya 25% of refugees
Tier 3 English, Turkish, Russian, Somali Host countries

2.5 Economic Integration Failures

The human cost of credential non-recognition is enormous:

Underemployment Statistics

Profession Underemployment Rate Typical Alternative
Doctors 70% Healthcare assistants, taxi drivers
Engineers 60% Factory workers, delivery drivers
Teachers 55% Retail, childcare
Lawyers 80% Interpreters, office workers
Accountants 50% Bookkeeping, data entry

Economic Waste

Annual Cost of Credential Non-Recognition:
- Lost GDP contribution: $30-50 billion
- Welfare costs (unnecessary): $10-15 billion
- Re-training costs: $5-8 billion
- Total annual waste: ~$50-70 billion globally

[!] Warning: Beyond economics, there is profound human cost - loss of professional identity, depression, wasted years.

2.6 Current System Failures

Case Study: Medical Professionals

Syrian Doctor Attempting to Practice in UK:

Traditional Path (without WIA):
1. Apply for document verification (impossible - records destroyed)
2. Take PLAB exam (1-2 years preparation)
3. Complete clinical attachment (6 months, unpaid)
4. Foundation training (2 years)
5. Specialty training (5-8 years)

Total: 8-12 years to return to previous level
Many never complete the process

Case Study: Academic Credentials

Ukrainian PhD Attempting University Position in EU:

Traditional Path:
1. Get transcripts (university damaged)
2. Get degree verified (ministry overwhelmed)
3. Get credential evaluated (6-12 months)
4. Apply for recognition (varies by country)
5. Often required to repeat courses

Total: 2-4 years delay minimum

2.8 Key Innovations

Innovation 1: Confidence Scoring

Instead of binary (verified/not verified), WIA provides a confidence percentage:

Confidence Score Components:
- Base Level Confidence: 30-95%
- Evidence Bonuses: +5-15% per verified claim
- Consistency Bonus: +10% for matching information
- Recency Penalty: -1% per year since verification
- Final Score: Weighted combination

Innovation 2: Peer Network

Former colleagues become verifiers:

Peer Matching Algorithm:
1. Input claimed institution + time period
2. Search peer network for overlapping attendance
3. Verify peer's own credentials
4. Request blind verification (prevent collusion)
5. Aggregate peer confirmations

Innovation 3: Competency Assessment

Skills testing provides objective evidence:

Assessment Categories:
- Medical: Clinical reasoning, practical skills
- Engineering: Technical problems, design challenges
- Teaching: Lesson planning, pedagogical knowledge
- Language: CEFR-aligned proficiency tests

Innovation 4: Progressive Rights

Each verification level unlocks capabilities:

Confidence Unlocked Rights
30%+ Entry-level employment, volunteer work
50%+ Skilled employment, further education
70%+ Supervised professional practice
85%+ Independent professional practice
95%+ Full recognition equivalent to native credentials
