🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Hydrogen fuel cells convert H₂ and O₂ into electricity through electrochemical reactions with water as the only byproduct
  • Modern FCVs achieve 400-650 km range with 3-5 minute refueling time, comparable to conventional vehicles
  • System efficiency of 45-55% from hydrogen to electricity enables practical zero-emission transportation
  • Type IV tanks store hydrogen at 700 bar with gravimetric density targets of 5.5% for automotive applications
  • Integration of fuel cell, power electronics, electric motor, and battery buffer creates efficient hybrid powertrain
  • WIA-AUTO-007 standard provides comprehensive framework for safety, performance, and interoperability

📝 Review Questions

  1. Calculate the power output of a fuel cell stack with 350 cells operating at 0.65V and drawing 380A current.
  2. Explain why hydrogen fuel cell vehicles maintain better performance in cold weather compared to battery electric vehicles.
  3. Describe the complete electrochemical reactions occurring at the anode and cathode of a PEMFC.
  4. A hydrogen vehicle has 5.6 kg H₂ capacity. Calculate theoretical range assuming 45% powertrain efficiency and 0.95 MJ/km consumption.
  5. Compare the energy density of hydrogen (120 MJ/kg) with gasoline (44 MJ/kg) and explain storage volume challenges.
  6. Discuss the role of the small battery buffer (1-2 kWh) in fuel cell hybrid powertrains and its impact on efficiency.
