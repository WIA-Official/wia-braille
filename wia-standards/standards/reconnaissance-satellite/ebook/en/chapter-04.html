Chapter 4

Signals Intelligence & SIGINT

Electronic intelligence gathering through COMINT, ELINT, and FISINT capabilities.

SIGINT Overview

Signals Intelligence (SIGINT) satellites intercept electronic emissions across the electromagnetic spectrum, providing critical intelligence on communications, radar systems, and electronic warfare activities. Unlike imaging satellites that photograph targets, SIGINT systems eavesdrop on the electronic environment, detecting, identifying, and geolocating RF emitters. This capability proves invaluable for understanding adversary capabilities, intentions, and activities.

The WIA-DEF-011 standard defines three primary SIGINT categories: COMINT (Communications Intelligence) intercepts voice and data communications, ELINT (Electronic Intelligence) characterizes radar and weapon systems, and FISINT (Foreign Instrumentation Signals Intelligence) monitors telemetry from missile tests and space launches. Modern satellites combine multiple SIGINT capabilities on single platforms, achieving comprehensive electronic surveillance.

COMINT: Communications Intelligence

COMINT satellites intercept radio communications across VHF, UHF, and microwave bands. Ground-based military units, ships, aircraft, and mobile command posts constantly emit RF communications that space-based collectors can intercept from overhead. Large antenna arrays with sophisticated signal processing enable simultaneous monitoring of thousands of frequencies.

Frequency Coverage

Band Frequency Range Typical Applications Antenna Type
VHF 30-300 MHz Tactical military comms, air traffic control 2-10 m dipole array
UHF 300-3000 MHz Satellite uplinks, tactical radio 1-3 m phased array
L-band 1-2 GHz Mobile satellite phones, GPS 0.5-2 m reflector
S-band 2-4 GHz Weather radar, surface search radar 0.3-1 m dish
C/X-band 4-12 GHz Satellite communications, radar 0.2-0.5 m parabolic

The enormous antennas required for VHF/UHF reception present engineering challenges. The USA's Mentor/Orion SIGINT satellites deploy mesh antennas exceeding 100 meters in diameter - larger than a football field - to achieve adequate gain at low frequencies. These gossamer structures unfurl in orbit like giant umbrellas, collecting faint signals from thousands of kilometers altitude.

Signal Detection and Processing

Detecting weak signals amidst noise and interference requires high signal-to-noise ratios. The WIA-DEF-011 standard specifies minimum SNR requirements for reliable signal detection and demodulation.

SNR for Detection:

SNR_dB = 10 × log₁₀(P_signal / P_noise)

Minimum requirements:
• Detection: SNR ≥ 10 dB
• Demodulation: SNR ≥ 15 dB
• High-quality copy: SNR ≥ 20 dB

Where:
P_signal = Signal power (watts)
P_noise = Noise power (watts)

Geolocation Accuracy

Determining emitter location provides critical intelligence. SIGINT satellites employ Time Difference of Arrival (TDOA) and Frequency Difference of Arrival (FDOA) techniques, comparing signal arrival times and Doppler shifts at multiple satellites to triangulate emitter position.

Geolocation Error:

σ_geo = c / (2 × B × SNR)

Where:
• σ_geo = Geolocation accuracy (meters)
• c = Speed of light (3×10⁸ m/s)
• B = Bandwidth (Hz)
• SNR = Signal-to-noise ratio

Example:
B = 1 MHz, SNR = 100 (20 dB)
σ_geo = 3×10⁸ / (2 × 10⁶ × 100) = 1.5 m
SNR Level SNR (dB) Typical Accuracy Use Case
High >20 dB 100-500 m Precision targeting
Medium 10-20 dB 500-2000 m General location
Low <10 dB 2-10 km Area of interest

ELINT: Electronic Intelligence

ELINT systems characterize radar and electronic warfare emitters by measuring frequency, pulse repetition interval, pulse width, scan patterns, and modulation characteristics. This electronic fingerprinting enables identification of specific radar types - surface-to-air missile systems, early warning radars, fire control radars - providing critical order of battle intelligence.

Radar Signal Parameters

Modern ELINT systems measure multiple parameters simultaneously to create detailed emitter signatures. The WIA-DEF-011 standard specifies measurement accuracy requirements for intelligence-grade characterization.

Key ELINT Measurements:

  • Frequency: ±0.1 MHz accuracy across 0.5-40 GHz spectrum
  • Pulse Width: ±10 nanoseconds for 0.1-100 μs pulses
  • PRF: ±1 Hz accuracy for pulse repetition frequency
  • Scan Rate: ±0.1 RPM for mechanically scanned radars
  • Antenna Pattern: ±1 dB gain measurement accuracy
  • Modulation: Linear FM chirp, Barker code, phase coding detection

Emitter Database

Collected ELINT data populates comprehensive emitter libraries containing signatures for thousands of radar systems worldwide. When a new signal is detected, its parameters are matched against this database to identify the system type, providing immediate intelligence value. The WIA-DEF-011 standard defines data formats for emitter records enabling multi-national intelligence sharing.

Example Emitter Record:

{
system_id: "S-300PMU-2",

peak_power: 150 kW,

threat_level: "high"
}

FISINT: Foreign Instrumentation Signals

FISINT collects telemetry and tracking signals from missile tests, satellite launches, and weapon system trials. When a nation tests a ballistic missile, extensive telemetry streams from the vehicle to ground stations, reporting performance data. FISINT satellites positioned overhead intercept this telemetry, providing insight into missile capabilities, performance, and development status.

Telemetry signals typically use PCM/FM modulation in S-band or C-band frequencies. Data rates range from kilobits to megabits per second depending on the number of onboard sensors. Sophisticated signal processing decommutates the telemetry stream, extracting individual parameter values and reconstructing flight profiles. The WIA-DEF-011 standard defines telemetry processing requirements for intelligence applications.

Multi-INT Correlation

Maximum intelligence value emerges when SIGINT data correlates with imagery intelligence (IMINT). An optical reconnaissance satellite photographs a SAM battery deployment, while ELINT characterizes its radar emissions, and COMINT intercepts tactical communications - together providing comprehensive situational awareness. The WIA-DEF-011 standard promotes multi-INT fusion through standardized data formats and timing synchronization.

Intelligence Type Information Provided Correlation Value
IMINT (Optical) Visual appearance, location, activity Identifies what
IMINT (SAR) All-weather structure, movements Tracks activity
ELINT Radar type, modes, capabilities Characterizes system
COMINT Communications, command structure Reveals intentions
FISINT Performance data, test results Assesses capability

SIGINT Satellite Architectures

SIGINT satellites operate in multiple orbital regimes depending on mission requirements. Geostationary orbits (36,000 km altitude) provide continuous coverage of specific regions, ideal for COMINT collection against fixed ground stations. Highly Elliptical Orbits (HEO) enable long dwell times over high-latitude targets unreachable from GEO. Low Earth Orbit SIGINT satellites achieve better signal strength but limited dwell time over targets.

SIGINT Orbital Regimes:

  • GEO (36,000 km): Continuous regional coverage, large antennas required
  • HEO (Molniya): 12-hour elliptical orbit for high-latitude coverage
  • LEO (400-1000 km): Stronger signals, global coverage, shorter dwell
  • Constellations: Multiple LEO satellites provide persistent coverage

Key Takeaways

  • SIGINT satellites intercept communications (COMINT), radar emissions (ELINT), and test telemetry (FISINT)
  • VHF/UHF COMINT requires enormous deployable antennas exceeding 100 meters diameter
  • Geolocation accuracy improves with signal bandwidth and SNR, achieving <500m precision
  • ELINT characterizes radar systems through frequency, pulse parameters, and scan patterns
  • Emitter databases containing thousands of signatures enable automatic system identification
  • Multi-INT correlation combining SIGINT with imagery provides comprehensive intelligence
  • Different orbits (GEO, HEO, LEO) optimize for coverage vs signal strength trade-offs

Summary

This chapter explored signals intelligence capabilities that complement imaging reconnaissance. COMINT intercepts communications across VHF through microwave frequencies, ELINT characterizes radar and electronic warfare systems, and FISINT collects test telemetry. Geolocation techniques using TDOA/FDOA enable precise emitter positioning, while comprehensive databases support automatic signal identification.

The synergy between SIGINT and imaging intelligence provides commanders with comprehensive situational awareness - imagery shows what is present, while SIGINT reveals electronic activity and intentions. Different orbital regimes optimize collection for specific targets and regions. The next chapter examines image processing and analysis techniques that extract intelligence from reconnaissance data.
