4.1 Introduction to Microbiome Sequencing

The revolution in microbiome research was made possible by next-generation sequencing (NGS) technologies that can sequence millions to billions of DNA fragments in parallel. This chapter covers the main sequencing approaches used in microbiome studies: 16S rRNA gene sequencing, shotgun metagenomics, and ITS sequencing for fungi.

Each method has distinct advantages and trade-offs in terms of cost, resolution, and information content. Understanding these differences is critical for designing studies that answer specific research questions while staying within budget and timeline constraints.

4.2 16S rRNA Gene Sequencing

4.2.1 The 16S rRNA Gene as a Molecular Barcode

The 16S rRNA gene is approximately 1,500 base pairs long and contains nine hypervariable regions (V1-V9) interspersed with highly conserved regions. This structure makes it ideal for bacterial identification:

  • Conserved regions: Allow universal primer design that amplifies the gene from all bacteria
  • Variable regions: Provide taxonomic information for species/genus identification
  • Appropriate length: Short enough to sequence completely, long enough for phylogenetic analysis
  • High copy number: Multiple copies per cell enable detection of low-abundance species

4.2.2 Variable Region Selection

Region Forward Primer Reverse Primer Amplicon Length Taxonomic Resolution Best For
V1-V2 27F 338R ~311 bp Phylum/Class Long-read sequencing (PacBio)
V3-V4 341F 805R ~464 bp Genus (sometimes species) Recommended - Best overall
V4 515F 806R ~291 bp Genus Human Microbiome Project, Earth Microbiome
V4-V5 515F 926R ~411 bp Genus (high resolution) Complex communities

WIA-BIO-013 Recommendation: The V3-V4 region provides the best balance between taxonomic resolution and compatibility with Illumina MiSeq 2×300 bp sequencing, with nearly complete read overlap for high-quality consensus sequences.

4.2.3 PCR Amplification Protocol

Standard 16S V3-V4 PCR Protocol:

Primers (with Illumina adapters):
Forward: 341F - CCTACGGGNGGCWGCAG
Reverse: 805R - GACTACHVGGGTATCTAATCC

Master Mix Preparation (per reaction):
- 2× KAPA HiFi HotStart ReadyMix: 12.5 μL
- Forward primer (10 μM): 1.0 μL
- Reverse primer (10 μM): 1.0 μL
- Template DNA (5-10 ng): 2.5 μL
- PCR-grade water: 8.0 μL
Total volume: 25 μL

Thermocycling Conditions:
1. Initial denaturation: 95°C for 3 min

2. 25 cycles:
   - Denaturation: 95°C for 30 sec
   - Annealing: 55°C for 30 sec
   - Extension: 72°C for 30 sec

3. Final extension: 72°C for 5 min

4. Hold: 4°C

Important Notes:
- Use high-fidelity polymerase (KAPA, Q5, or Phusion)
- Limit to 25-30 cycles to minimize PCR bias
- Include negative control (no template)
- Include positive control (mock community)

Expected Yield:
- Strong band at ~550 bp (primers + amplicon)
- Concentration: 10-50 ng/μL

4.2.4 Library Preparation and Indexing

After initial PCR amplification, samples must be indexed with unique barcodes to enable multiplexing (sequencing multiple samples together):

Dual-Index PCR (Illumina Nextera Protocol):

Index PCR Setup (per sample):
- PCR product from Step 1 (diluted 1:10): 5 μL
- 2× KAPA HiFi ReadyMix: 25 μL
- Nextera XT Index Primer 1 (i7): 5 μL
- Nextera XT Index Primer 2 (i5): 5 μL
- Water: 10 μL
Total: 50 μL

Cycling Conditions:
1. 95°C for 3 min
2. 8 cycles:
   - 95°C for 30 sec
   - 55°C for 30 sec
   - 72°C for 30 sec
3. 72°C for 5 min
4. Hold at 4°C

Post-PCR Cleanup:
1. AMPure XP bead cleanup (0.8× ratio)
   - Add 40 μL beads to 50 μL PCR product
   - Incubate 5 min at RT
   - Magnetic separation
   - Wash 2× with 200 μL 80% ethanol
   - Air dry 5 min
   - Elute in 25 μL TE buffer

2. Quantify cleaned libraries (Qubit dsDNA HS)

3. Check size distribution (TapeStation or Bioanalyzer)
   - Expected size: ~630 bp (amplicon + adapters + indices)

4. Normalize and pool libraries
   - Target: 4 nM final concentration
   - Pool equal volumes for equimolar pooling

4.3 Shotgun Metagenomic Sequencing

4.3.1 Advantages Over 16S Sequencing

While 16S sequencing provides taxonomic composition, shotgun metagenomics sequences all DNA in a sample, providing:

  • Species and strain-level resolution: Full genomes enable precise identification
  • Functional information: Identifies genes, metabolic pathways, and functional capabilities
  • Viruses, fungi, and eukaryotes: Not limited to bacteria/archaea
  • Horizontal gene transfer: Detects mobile genetic elements and resistance genes
  • No PCR bias: Direct sequencing without amplification artifacts

4.3.2 Library Preparation Protocol

Shotgun Metagenomic Library Preparation:

Requirements:
- Input DNA: 100-500 ng high-quality DNA
- A260/A280: 1.8-2.0
- A260/A230: >1.5
- Concentration: >5 ng/μL

Protocol Steps (Illumina DNA Prep):

1. Tagmentation (Fragmentation + Adapter Addition)
   - DNA input: 50-100 ng in 30 μL
   - Add 10 μL Tagment Buffer
   - Add 10 μL Tagment Enzyme
   - Mix and incubate: 55°C for 15 min
   - Add 10 μL Tagment Stop Buffer
   - Incubate: RT for 5 min

2. Post-Tagmentation Cleanup
   - Add 25 μL AMPure XP beads
   - Magnetic separation and wash
   - Elute in 17 μL TE

3. PCR Amplification (add indices)
   - Add PCR Master Mix and index primers
   - Cycling:
     * 68°C for 3 min
     * 98°C for 3 min
     * 5 cycles: 98°C/45s, 62°C/30s, 68°C/2min
     * 68°C for 1 min

4. Final Cleanup
   - AMPure cleanup (0.8× or 1.0×)
   - Elute in 30 μL

5. Quality Control
   - Qubit quantification: expect 5-20 nM
   - Fragment size: 300-600 bp (peak ~400 bp)
   - TapeStation: smooth distribution

Pooling and Sequencing:
- Normalize to 4 nM
- Pool samples equimolarly
- Sequence on Illumina (NovaSeq, NextSeq, or MiSeq)
- Target depth: 10-50 million reads per sample

4.3.3 Sequencing Depth Recommendations

Sample Type Minimum Reads Recommended Reads Deep Sequencing Data Size
Gut Microbiome 5 million 10-20 million 50-100 million 1-5 Gb per sample
Oral Microbiome 5 million 10-15 million 30-50 million 1-3 Gb per sample
Skin Microbiome 5 million 10 million 20-30 million 0.5-2 Gb per sample
Low Biomass 10 million 20-50 million 100+ million 5-10 Gb per sample
Environmental 10 million 20-40 million 100-500 million 10-50 Gb per sample

4.4 ITS Sequencing for Fungi

4.4.1 The ITS Region

The Internal Transcribed Spacer (ITS) region is the standard marker for fungal identification, analogous to 16S rRNA for bacteria. The ITS region consists of ITS1 and ITS2 separated by the 5.8S rRNA gene.

4.4.2 ITS Amplification Protocol

ITS2 Region Sequencing (Fungi):

Primers:
- Forward: fITS7 (GTGARTCATCGAATCTTTG)
- Reverse: ITS4 (TCCTCCGCTTATTGATATGC)

PCR Protocol:
- Same as 16S but with modified annealing temperature

Cycling Conditions:
1. 95°C for 3 min
2. 30 cycles:
   - 95°C for 30 sec
   - 52°C for 30 sec (lower than bacterial 16S)
   - 72°C for 30 sec
3. 72°C for 5 min

Important Considerations:
- ITS length is highly variable (200-600 bp)
- May require size selection
- Lower annealing temp due to primer Tm
- Some samples may need increased cycle number (35)
- Consider including bacteria removal step (PNA blockers)

4.6 Quality Control for Sequencing

4.6.1 Library QC Metrics

Pre-Sequencing Quality Checks:

1. Concentration (Qubit):
   ✓ Pass: 2-10 nM final library
   ⚠ Warning: 1-2 nM (may still work)
   ✗ Fail: <1 nM (insufficient for sequencing)

2. Fragment Size (TapeStation/Bioanalyzer):
   16S Libraries:
   ✓ Pass: Sharp peak at 550-630 bp
   ⚠ Warning: Broad peak or secondary peaks
   ✗ Fail: No peak or wrong size

   Shotgun Libraries:
   ✓ Pass: Smooth distribution 300-600 bp, peak ~400 bp
   ⚠ Warning: Skewed distribution
   ✗ Fail: Adapter dimers (<200 bp)

3. Pooling Validation:
   - Calculate molarity for each library
   - Pool in equal nanomolar amounts
   - Final pool: 4 nM in 10-20 μL
   - Verify pooled concentration

4. PhiX Control:
   - Add 1-5% PhiX to increase diversity
   - Essential for low-diversity libraries (16S)
   - Helps with base calling and cluster identification

4.7 Sequencing Run Setup

4.7.1 MiSeq Run Configuration (Example)

Illumina MiSeq 2×300 bp Run (16S V3-V4):

Sample Sheet Setup:
- Project name
- Sample IDs and index sequences
- Ensure no index collisions
- Balanced index distribution

Library Loading:
1. Dilute library pool to 4 nM
2. Denature with 0.2 N NaOH
   - 5 μL library + 5 μL 0.2 N NaOH
   - Incubate 5 min at RT
3. Add HT1 buffer to achieve 20 pM
   - For 16S: load at 8-12 pM (lower due to low diversity)
4. Add PhiX control (5%)
   - Denature PhiX separately
   - Mix 95% sample : 5% PhiX

Run Parameters:
- Read 1: 300 cycles
- Index 1: 8 cycles
- Index 2: 8 cycles
- Read 2: 300 cycles
Total: 624 cycles (~56 hours)

Expected Metrics:
- Cluster density: 800-1,000 K/mm²
- Clusters passing filter: >80%
- Q30 score: >70% (Read 1), >60% (Read 2)
- Total reads: 20-25 million
- Reads per sample: Depends on pooling

4.9 Cost Analysis and Sample Planning

4.9.1 Budget Planning Example

Cost Breakdown for 96-Sample Study:

16S rRNA Sequencing (MiSeq):
- DNA extraction kits: $6/sample × 96 = $576
- PCR reagents: $3/sample × 96 = $288
- Library prep kits: $4/sample × 96 = $384
- Sequencing (1 MiSeq run): $1,500
- Consumables/controls: $250
Total: ~$3,000 ($31/sample)

Shotgun Metagenomics (NovaSeq):
- DNA extraction kits: $8/sample × 96 = $768
- Library prep kits: $15/sample × 96 = $1,440
- Sequencing (1/4 NovaSeq S4 lane): $4,000
- Bioinformatics storage: $500
Total: ~$6,700 ($70/sample)

Recommendations:
- 16S for exploratory studies, large cohorts
- Shotgun for functional analysis, strain-level resolution
- Consider hybrid approach: 16S screening + shotgun for selected samples
- Plan for 10-15% failed samples (re-extraction/re-sequencing)

Key Takeaways

  • 16S rRNA sequencing targets a single gene for taxonomic identification, offering cost-effective community profiling at the genus level with well-established protocols and databases.
  • The V3-V4 region is the recommended target for 16S sequencing, providing optimal taxonomic resolution and full read overlap on Illumina MiSeq 2×300 bp sequencing.
  • Shotgun metagenomics sequences all DNA in a sample, providing species/strain-level resolution plus functional information, but requires 3-5× higher sequencing depth and analysis complexity.
  • Library preparation quality control is critical—proper quantification (Qubit), size verification (TapeStation), and equimolar pooling ensure balanced read distribution across samples.
  • Sequencing depth requirements vary by sample type: gut samples need 10-20 million reads, while low-biomass samples may need 50+ million reads for adequate coverage.
  • Cost per sample ranges from $30-50 for 16S sequencing to $70-150 for shotgun metagenomics, making study design and sample prioritization important for budget management.
