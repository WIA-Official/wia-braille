2.1 The Critical First Step

Sample collection represents the foundational step in microbiome analysis, and errors at this stage can compromise all downstream results. The microbiome is highly dynamic, with composition changing in response to diet, medication, stress, and circadian rhythms. Proper collection techniques ensure that the sample accurately represents the microbial community at the time of collection and preserves it until analysis.

The WIA-BIO-013 standard emphasizes standardized collection protocols to ensure reproducibility across studies and enable meaningful comparisons between datasets generated in different laboratories worldwide.

2.2 Gut Microbiome Collection

2.2.1 Fecal Sample Collection Protocol

Fecal samples provide the most accessible window into the gut microbiome. The WIA-BIO-013 protocol requires specific procedures to minimize contamination and preserve DNA integrity:

Standardized Fecal Collection Protocol (WIA-BIO-013):

Materials Required:
├─ Sterile collection tube (50 mL conical)
├─ Sterile spatula or collection spoon
├─ DNA stabilization buffer (optional but recommended)
├─ Gloves (powder-free)
├─ Biohazard bag
└─ Collection date/time label

Step-by-Step Protocol:

1. PREPARATION (Before Collection)
   ├─ Wash hands thoroughly
   ├─ Put on powder-free gloves
   ├─ Label collection tube with ID, date, time
   └─ Prepare collection container

2. COLLECTION (Timing is Critical)
   ├─ Morning sample preferred (6-10 AM)
   ├─ Avoid antibiotics 4 weeks prior
   ├─ Avoid probiotics 2 weeks prior
   ├─ Fast food/alcohol 24 hours prior
   └─ No bowel preparation or laxatives

3. SAMPLE HANDLING
   ├─ Collect 200-500 mg (pea-sized portion)
   ├─ Sample from interior, avoid toilet water
   ├─ Transfer to sterile tube immediately
   ├─ Add 2 mL DNA stabilization buffer (if using)
   ├─ Mix by vortexing 30 seconds
   └─ Seal tube tightly

4. STORAGE (Time-Sensitive)
   ├─ Immediate extraction: 4°C, <24 hours
   ├─ Short-term: -20°C, <7 days
   ├─ Long-term: -80°C, years
   └─ Room temperature with buffer: <2 weeks

5. TRANSPORT
   ├─ Place in biohazard bag
   ├─ Keep cold (ice pack/dry ice)
   ├─ Ship within 24 hours
   └─ Use overnight delivery

2.2.2 Critical Variables Affecting Gut Microbiome

Multiple factors can significantly alter gut microbiome composition. The WIA-BIO-013 standard requires documenting these variables as essential metadata:

Variable Impact Level Recommended Control Period Notes
Antibiotics Very High 4 weeks abstinence Can reduce diversity by 30-50%
Probiotics Moderate-High 2 weeks abstinence Transient colonization, affects composition
Diet Changes Moderate Stable diet 3 days prior High-fiber vs. high-fat shows differences
Time of Day Low-Moderate Morning collection preferred Circadian variations exist
Bowel Consistency Moderate Bristol Stool Scale 3-5 Diarrhea/constipation alters composition
Exercise Low Document regular routine Chronic exercise increases diversity
Alcohol Low-Moderate 24-48 hour abstinence Acute consumption affects composition

2.3 Other Body Site Collection

2.3.1 Skin Microbiome Collection

Skin harbors diverse microbial communities that vary by site (sebaceous, moist, dry). The swab-based collection method is standardized in WIA-BIO-013:

Skin Swab Protocol:

Materials:
├─ Sterile cotton or polyester swabs
├─ Sterile PBS or collection buffer
├─ Template for sampling area (2 × 2 cm)
└─ Storage tubes

Procedure:
1. Select sampling site(s):
   ├─ Forehead (sebaceous)
   ├─ Antecubital fossa (moist)
   ├─ Volar forearm (dry)
   └─ Plantar foot (specialized)

2. Clean area (if needed):
   ├─ No showering 24 hours prior
   ├─ No topical products 48 hours prior
   └─ Gently wipe excess oils/debris

3. Swab technique:
   ├─ Moisten swab with sterile buffer
   ├─ Rub 30 seconds in defined area
   ├─ Rotate swab while rubbing
   ├─ Apply moderate pressure
   └─ Swab same area twice

4. Storage:
   ├─ Break swab handle, place in tube
   ├─ Add 500 μL buffer
   ├─ Store -80°C or -20°C
   └─ Extract within 1 week

2.3.2 Oral Microbiome Collection

The oral microbiome shows high site specificity (tongue, teeth, gingival sulcus, buccal mucosa). Saliva provides a representative overview:

Saliva Collection Protocol:

Pre-collection Requirements:
├─ Fasting state (2 hours minimum)
├─ No oral hygiene 2 hours prior
├─ No eating/drinking 2 hours prior
└─ Rinse mouth with water 10 minutes before

Collection Method:
1. Passive drool method (preferred):
   ├─ Sit upright, head tilted forward
   ├─ Allow saliva to pool in mouth
   ├─ Drool into sterile tube
   ├─ Collect 2-5 mL
   └─ Takes 2-5 minutes

2. Spitting method (alternative):
   ├─ Accumulate saliva in mouth
   ├─ Spit into sterile tube
   ├─ Repeat until 2 mL collected
   └─ Avoid forced salivation

3. Immediate processing:
   ├─ Add equal volume RNA/DNA stabilizer
   ├─ Mix by inversion (10 times)
   ├─ Store -80°C or process immediately
   └─ Can store room temp 1 week with stabilizer

2.4 DNA Stabilization and Storage

2.4.1 Storage Temperature Effects

Temperature during storage dramatically affects DNA quality and microbial composition. The table below shows WIA-BIO-013 recommendations:

Storage Method Temperature Maximum Duration DNA Quality Composition Stability Use Case
Immediate Extraction 4°C 24 hours Excellent Excellent Lab-based studies
Short-term Freezing -20°C 1 week Good-Excellent Good Temporary storage
Long-term Freezing -80°C Years Excellent Excellent Biobanking, large studies
Stabilization Buffer Room temp 2-4 weeks Good Good Field studies, mailing
Ethanol Preservation Room temp Months Fair-Good Moderate Field studies (limited access)
Freeze-Dried Room temp Years Good Fair Long-term archival

2.4.2 Commercial Stabilization Buffers

Several commercial DNA stabilization solutions enable room-temperature storage and shipping. These solutions typically contain:

  • Chaotropic salts (guanidinium chloride, guanidinium thiocyanate): Denature proteins and inactivate nucleases
  • Chelating agents (EDTA): Bind divalent cations required for DNase activity
  • Reducing agents (DTT, β-mercaptoethanol): Prevent oxidative DNA damage
  • pH buffers (Tris-HCl): Maintain optimal pH for DNA stability

Examples include OMNIgene GUT (DNA Genotek), FTA cards (Whatman), and RNAlater (for RNA preservation).

2.5 Metadata Collection Requirements

The WIA-BIO-013 standard adopts and extends the MIxS (Minimum Information about any Sequence) standards developed by the Genomic Standards Consortium. Comprehensive metadata is essential for proper interpretation and enables cross-study comparisons.

2.5.1 Required Metadata Fields

WIA-BIO-013 Metadata Requirements:

SAMPLE IDENTIFIERS (Required)
├─ Sample ID (unique, persistent)
├─ Subject ID (anonymous but linkable)
├─ Study ID
└─ Collection batch ID

COLLECTION INFORMATION (Required)
├─ Collection date (ISO 8601: YYYY-MM-DD)
├─ Collection time (24-hour format: HH:MM)
├─ Collection method (standardized protocol)
├─ Body site (controlled vocabulary)
├─ Body subsite (if applicable)
└─ Collector ID/name

SUBJECT DEMOGRAPHICS (Required)
├─ Age (years) or age range
├─ Sex (male/female/other)
├─ Ethnicity (self-reported)
├─ Geographic location (country, region)
├─ BMI or weight/height
└─ Dietary pattern (omnivore/vegetarian/vegan)

MEDICAL HISTORY (Required if applicable)
├─ Disease status (diagnosed conditions)
├─ Medications (past 3 months)
├─ Antibiotic use (past 6 months)
├─ Probiotic use (past 1 month)
├─ Hospitalization (past year)
└─ Surgery (past year, if GI-related)

LIFESTYLE FACTORS (Recommended)
├─ Diet (24-48 hour recall)
├─ Alcohol consumption (weekly average)
├─ Smoking status
├─ Exercise frequency
├─ Sleep quality/duration
└─ Stress level (validated scale)

STORAGE CONDITIONS (Required)
├─ Storage method (buffer type, temperature)
├─ Storage duration before processing
├─ Freeze-thaw cycles (if any)
└─ Shipment conditions

LAB PROCESSING (Required)
├─ Extraction date
├─ Extraction method/kit
├─ DNA concentration
├─ DNA quality metrics (260/280, 260/230)
└─ Lab ID/technician

2.6 Quality Control for Sample Collection

2.6.1 Contamination Control

Microbiome samples are susceptible to contamination from environmental sources, collection materials, and human handling. WIA-BIO-013 requires specific controls:

  • Negative Controls: Process "blank" samples (unused swabs, empty tubes with buffer) through entire workflow to identify contamination sources. Common contaminants include skin bacteria from handlers and environmental microbes from reagents.
  • Positive Controls: Use mock communities (defined mixtures of bacterial strains) to validate extraction and sequencing protocols. Commercial standards like ZymoBIOMICS Microbial Community Standard provide known composition.
  • Field Blanks: For studies involving sample shipping or field collection, transport unopened collection tubes alongside samples to capture environmental contamination.
  • Sterile Technique: All collection materials must be sterile. Wear powder-free gloves. Avoid talking over open samples. Work in clean environment away from high-traffic areas.

2.6.2 Sample Quality Assessment

Before proceeding with expensive sequencing, assess sample quality:

Quality Control Checkpoints:

VISUAL INSPECTION
├─ Sample volume: Minimum 200 mg (fecal)
├─ Contamination: No visible blood, mucus, toilet water
├─ Integrity: No signs of degradation or mold
└─ Labeling: Correct ID, legible, date recorded

DNA QUANTIFICATION
├─ Method: Fluorometry (Qubit) or spectrophotometry
├─ Concentration: >5 ng/μL (minimum)
├─ Optimal range: 10-50 ng/μL
└─ Total yield: >500 ng for 16S, >1 μg for shotgun

DNA PURITY
├─ A260/A280 ratio: 1.8-2.0 (pure DNA)
│  ├─ <1.8 indicates protein contamination
│  └─ >2.0 indicates RNA contamination
├─ A260/A230 ratio: >1.5 (preferably >2.0)
│  └─ Low values indicate salt/phenol contamination
└─ Color: Clear to slightly cloudy (not brown/dark)

DNA INTEGRITY
├─ Gel electrophoresis: High MW band >10 kb
├─ Bioanalyzer: DNA Integrity Number (DIN) >7
├─ Fragment size: >1 kb for 16S, >10 kb for shotgun
└─ No low MW smearing (indicates degradation)

ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
├─ Pass all QC metrics → Proceed to sequencing
├─ Marginal QC → Consider re-extraction or exclusion
└─ Failed QC → Re-collect sample if possible

2.7 Special Considerations

2.7.1 Low-Biomass Samples

Some sample types (blood, cerebrospinal fluid, tissue biopsies) contain very low microbial biomass, making them particularly vulnerable to contamination. Special precautions required:

  • Use of ultra-clean collection materials and reagents
  • Increased number of negative controls (1 per 4 samples instead of 1 per 10)
  • Larger sample volumes when possible
  • Consideration of 16S copy number variation between species
  • Statistical methods to subtract contaminant sequences (decontam package in R)

2.7.2 Pediatric and Neonatal Samples

Infant microbiomes differ substantially from adults and require modified protocols:

  • Meconium: First stool, collected in delivery room, very low biomass
  • Infant stool: Often liquid, requires careful handling to avoid contamination
  • Sample volume: Reduce to 50-100 mg for small infants
  • Frequency: More frequent sampling (daily to weekly) to capture rapid changes
  • Ethical considerations: Parental consent, minimal invasiveness

Key Takeaways

  • Sample collection is the most critical step in microbiome analysis, and errors here cannot be corrected later in the pipeline.
  • Standardized protocols (WIA-BIO-013) ensure reproducibility across studies and enable meaningful data comparisons and meta-analyses.
  • Different body sites require specific collection methods: fecal samples (200-500 mg interior), skin swabs (30-second rubbing), saliva (2 mL passive drool).
  • Storage temperature dramatically affects results: -80°C for long-term storage, DNA stabilization buffers enable room-temperature shipping for 2-4 weeks.
  • Comprehensive metadata collection (subject demographics, medical history, lifestyle, storage conditions) is essential for proper interpretation of results.
  • Quality control measures including negative controls, positive controls, and DNA quality assessment prevent contamination and ensure data reliability.
