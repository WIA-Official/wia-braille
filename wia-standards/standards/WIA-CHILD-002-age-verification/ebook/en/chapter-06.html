Regulatory Compliance

Comprehensive coverage of COPPA, GDPR-K, FERPA, CCPA, and international regulations, with compliance checklists, audit procedures, and legal best practices for age verification.

Age verification systems operate within a complex web of regulations designed to protect children's privacy, safety, and rights online. From the United States' COPPA to the European Union's GDPR-K, from California's CCPA to the UK's Age Appropriate Design Code, platforms must navigate overlapping and sometimes conflicting requirements. Non-compliance carries severe penalties—up to $50,000 per violation under COPPA, 4% of global revenue under GDPR— making regulatory expertise essential. This chapter provides actionable guidance for achieving and maintaining compliance across major jurisdictions.

6.1 COPPA: Children's Online Privacy Protection Act

COPPA is the foundational U.S. regulation protecting children under 13 online. Enacted in 1998 and updated in 2013, COPPA applies to operators of websites and online services directed to children, as well as operators with actual knowledge they are collecting information from children.

6.1.1 Core COPPA Requirements

Requirement Description Technical Implementation Violation Penalty
Privacy Policy Clear notice of data collection practices Dedicated child privacy policy, prominent placement Up to $50,000 per violation
Parental Notice Direct notice to parents about data collection Email notification, verification required Up to $50,000 per violation
Verifiable Parental Consent Obtain consent before collecting child data Email+ (enhanced consent methods for sensitive data) Up to $50,000 per violation
Parental Access Allow parents to review child's data Parent portal, data export functionality Up to $50,000 per violation
Parental Deletion Allow parents to delete child's data Delete endpoints, account closure process Up to $50,000 per violation
Data Security Protect collected information Encryption, access controls, regular audits Up to $50,000 per violation
Data Minimization Collect only what's necessary Field validation, purpose justification Up to $50,000 per violation

6.2 GDPR-K: GDPR for Children

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) includes special provisions for children's data protection. Article 8 requires parental consent for processing personal data of children under 16 (though member states can lower this to 13). GDPR-K extends strong privacy protections to children.

6.2.1 GDPR-K vs COPPA

Aspect COPPA (USA) GDPR-K (EU) Compliance Strategy
Age Threshold Under 13 Under 16 (13-16 by member state) Use most restrictive (under 13)
Territorial Scope U.S.-directed services EU residents (extraterritorial) Global compliance framework
Penalties Up to $50,000 per violation Up to 4% global revenue or €20M Prioritize GDPR due to higher penalties
Consent Specificity General parental consent Granular, purpose-specific consent Implement granular consent UI
Right to Erasure Parental deletion rights Child's right to be forgotten Automated deletion workflows
Data Portability Not required Machine-readable export required JSON/XML export functionality

Maximum Penalty Alert

Under GDPR, violations involving children's data can result in fines up to €20 million or 4% of annual global turnover, whichever is higher. The UK's Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) fined TikTok £12.7 million ($15.9M) in 2023 for GDPR violations involving children's data. YouTube was fined $170 million by the FTC for COPPA violations. Compliance is not optional.

6.3 Other Major Regulations

6.3.1 California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA/CPRA)

California's privacy law includes special protections for minors under 16. The CCPA prohibits the sale of personal information of consumers aged 13-16 without affirmative opt-in authorization, and consumers under 13 without parental consent.

  • Opt-In Requirement: Must obtain affirmative opt-in before selling minor's data
  • Notice Requirement: "Do Not Sell My Personal Information" link must be prominent
  • Penalties: Up to $7,500 per intentional violation
  • Private Right of Action: Consumers can sue for data breaches ($100-$750 per incident)

6.3.2 UK Age Appropriate Design Code

The UK's groundbreaking regulation mandates age-appropriate design for services likely to be accessed by children under 18. Key requirements include:

  • Privacy by Default: Highest privacy settings by default for children
  • Data Minimization: Collect minimum data necessary
  • Detrimental Use: Do not use children's data in ways harmful to their wellbeing
  • Parental Controls: Provide tools for parents to exercise rights
  • Profiling: Disable profiling by default
  • Nudge Techniques: Do not use nudges to encourage children to weaken privacy settings
  • Transparency: Provide clear, age-appropriate privacy information

Key Takeaways

  • COPPA penalties are $50,000 per violation: Each instance of collecting data from a child without parental consent is a separate violation—fines accumulate rapidly.
  • GDPR-K has extraterritorial reach: Any service processing EU children's data must comply, regardless of company location, with penalties up to 4% of global revenue.
  • Compliance requires technical + policy measures: Automated data minimization validation, consent management systems, parental access portals, and deletion workflows are essential.
  • Age thresholds vary by jurisdiction: COPPA protects under-13, GDPR-K under-16 (with member state variations), UK Code under-18—use most restrictive for global compliance.
  • Parental consent methods must be verifiable: FTC-approved methods include email-plus, credit card verification, government ID, and video chat—simple email alone is insufficient.
  • Regular compliance audits are mandatory: Automated and manual audits should verify privacy policies, consent mechanisms, data minimization, security, and parental access rights.
