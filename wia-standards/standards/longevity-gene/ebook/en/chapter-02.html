Target Genes and Selection

Exploring the catalog of longevity-associated genes, evidence classifications, detailed gene profiles, and systematic selection algorithms for personalized interventions.

2.1 Gene Classification by Function

Not all longevity genes work through the same mechanisms. The WIA-AUG-017 standard classifies longevity-associated genes into eight functional categories based on their primary mechanism of action. Understanding these categories helps clinicians select genes that target specific aging pathways relevant to each patient's biological profile.

Category Primary Mechanism Example Genes Lifespan Extension
Telomere Maintenance Prevent replicative senescence by maintaining telomere length TERT, TERC, RTEL1 Variable (cellular)
Metabolic Regulation Energy sensing, autophagy, caloric restriction mimetics SIRT1, SIRT3, SIRT6, AMPK, MTOR, FOXO3 10-30%
DNA Repair Maintain genome stability, repair DNA damage BRCA1, TP53, ATM, SIRT6 5-15%
Stress Response Cellular stress resistance, antioxidant response FOXO3, NRF2, HSF1 15-30%
Inflammation Reduce chronic inflammation (inflammaging) IL6, TNF, NLRP3 Healthspan focus

2.2 Evidence Classification System

The strength of scientific evidence varies considerably across longevity genes. WIA-AUG-017 employs a rigorous three-tier evidence classification system to guide gene selection. This system integrates data from model organism studies (C. elegans, Drosophila, mice) and human genetic association studies.

Strong Evidence

Genes receive "Strong Evidence" classification when they demonstrate lifespan extension in multiple model organisms AND show associations with human longevity in population studies. These genes should be prioritized for initial clinical applications.

Strong Evidence Criteria:

  • Lifespan extension ≥15% in at least two model organisms (worms, flies, mice)
  • Replication across independent research groups
  • Human genetic association studies showing variants linked to longevity
  • Mechanistic understanding of longevity pathway

Examples: FOXO3, SIRT1, SIRT6, TERT (with caveats), MTOR (inhibition)

Moderate Evidence

"Moderate Evidence" genes show promising results but lack complete validation across all criteria. These genes may be suitable for clinical application with enhanced monitoring protocols.

Moderate Evidence Criteria:

  • Lifespan extension in at least one model organism OR strong human associations
  • Mechanistic rationale supported by research
  • Safety profile acceptable based on existing data

Examples: KLOTHO, GDF11, SIRT3, AMPK (certain variants)

Emerging Evidence

"Emerging Evidence" genes have strong mechanistic rationale and preliminary data but lack definitive lifespan studies. These genes should be reserved for research protocols only.

2.3 Detailed Gene Profiles

The following sections provide comprehensive profiles of the eight most promising longevity genes, covering molecular function, longevity mechanisms, evidence levels, editing strategies, and critical safety considerations.

FOXO3 (Forkhead Box O3)

Function: Transcription factor regulating stress response, DNA repair, autophagy, and metabolism

Longevity Mechanism:

  • Autophagy Promotion: Activates genes encoding autophagy proteins, enabling cellular self-cleaning
  • DNA Repair Enhancement: Upregulates DNA repair machinery genes
  • Stress Resistance: Activates antioxidant enzymes (SOD2, catalase)
  • Metabolic Regulation: Coordinates glucose and lipid metabolism

Evidence Level: STRONG

  • Most robust human genetic association with longevity (Willcox et al., 2008)
  • FOXO3 SNPs (rs2802292, rs2764264) overrepresented in centenarians across multiple populations (Japanese, German, Italian, American)
  • DAF-16 (C. elegans ortholog) extends lifespan 20-30% when activated
  • Protective against cardiovascular disease, diabetes, Alzheimer's disease

Editing Strategy: Upregulation via enhancer activation or introduction of protective alleles

Target: FOXO3 enhancer region (chr6:108,561,000-108,563,000)
Method: dCas9-p300 (epigenetic activation)
Expected fold-change: 1.5-2x baseline expression
Timeline: Measurable effects within 3-6 months

Safety Considerations:

Excellent safety profile - No major safety concerns identified in preclinical studies. FOXO3 acts as a tumor suppressor, reducing cancer risk. Monitor metabolic parameters during treatment.

SIRT1 (Sirtuin 1)

Function: NAD+-dependent deacetylase regulating metabolism, stress response, and chromatin structure

Longevity Mechanism:

  • Caloric Restriction Mimetic: Activates same pathways as dietary restriction without reduced food intake
  • Mitochondrial Enhancement: Promotes mitochondrial biogenesis via PGC-1α deacetylation
  • Insulin Sensitivity: Improves glucose metabolism and insulin signaling
  • Neuroprotection: Protects against neurodegeneration through multiple mechanisms

Evidence Level: STRONG

  • Extends lifespan 10-15% in mice (multiple studies)
  • Activated by resveratrol and NAD+ precursors (NMN, NR)
  • Protects against metabolic syndrome, type 2 diabetes, neurodegeneration
  • Conserved longevity pathway from yeast to mammals

Editing Strategy: Moderate upregulation (1.5-2x) to mimic pharmacological activation

Safety Considerations: Generally safe. Monitor metabolic parameters (glucose, insulin sensitivity). Avoid excessive upregulation (>3x) which may disrupt metabolic homeostasis.

SIRT6 (Sirtuin 6)

Function: NAD+-dependent deacetylase with roles in DNA repair, inflammation suppression, and metabolic regulation

Longevity Mechanism:

  • DNA Repair: Enhances double-strand break repair through PARP1 activation
  • Inflammation Suppression: Deacetylates NF-κB, reducing chronic inflammation
  • Telomere Maintenance: Maintains telomeric chromatin structure
  • Glucose Metabolism: Suppresses glycolysis, promotes oxidative metabolism

Evidence Level: STRONG

  • Extends lifespan 15% in male mice (Kanfi et al., 2012)
  • Protects against age-related inflammation (inflammaging)
  • Tumor suppressor - reduces cancer risk
  • Low expression associated with accelerated aging phenotypes

Editing Strategy: Upregulation (1.5-2x baseline)

Excellent safety profile - Anti-cancer effects make this one of the safest longevity genes. SIRT6 is considered a "guardian of the genome."

TERT (Telomerase Reverse Transcriptase)

Function: Catalytic subunit of telomerase enzyme; adds TTAGGG repeats to chromosome ends

Longevity Mechanism:

  • Replicative Lifespan Extension: Prevents telomere shortening, extends cellular division capacity
  • Stem Cell Maintenance: Maintains stem cell regenerative capacity
  • Senescence Prevention: Cells avoid replicative senescence
  • Tissue Rejuvenation: Restored telomerase can reverse aging phenotypes in aged mice

Evidence Level: STRONG (with significant caveats)

  • TERT reactivation reverses tissue degeneration in aged mice (Jaskelioff et al., 2011)
  • Extends cellular lifespan dramatically in vitro
  • Certain TERT variants associated with human longevity
  • BUT: Constitutive activation strongly increases cancer risk

Editing Strategy: Conditional, tissue-specific upregulation with strict safety monitoring

Approach: Inducible TERT under tissue-specific promoter
Upregulation limit: <3-fold increase
Monitoring: Monthly cancer surveillance
Exclusions: Any cancer history or high-risk variants

Critical Safety Considerations:

  • Cancer Risk: Telomerase activation is a hallmark of 85-95% of cancers
  • Requires continuous cancer biomarker surveillance (tumor markers, ctDNA)
  • Exclude patients with cancer history within 5 years
  • Exclude high-risk genetic variants (BRCA1/2, TP53, Lynch syndrome)
  • Consider senolytic combination therapy to clear pre-malignant cells

KLOTHO (Alpha-Klotho)

Function: Anti-aging hormone regulating mineral metabolism, FGF23 signaling, and oxidative stress

Longevity Mechanism:

  • Oxidative Stress Resistance: Increases expression of antioxidant enzymes
  • Cognitive Enhancement: Improves synaptic function and cognitive performance
  • Kidney Protection: Prevents age-related kidney decline
  • Cardiovascular Protection: Reduces vascular calcification and arterial stiffness

Evidence Level: MODERATE-STRONG

  • KL-VS variant (F352V + C370S) associated with human longevity and enhanced cognition
  • Klotho overexpression extends lifespan 20-30% in mice
  • Klotho levels decline with age (~50% reduction by age 40)
  • Supplementation reverses cognitive decline in aged mice

Editing Strategy: Upregulation or secreted protein delivery

Safety Considerations: Generally safe. Monitor serum phosphate and calcium levels as Klotho regulates mineral metabolism. Risk of hypophosphatemia if overexpressed.

2.4 Gene Selection Algorithm

Selecting optimal longevity genes for each patient requires systematic evaluation across multiple dimensions. The WIA-AUG-017 standard defines a quantitative selection algorithm: 

Gene Selection Score (GSS) = (Evidence × 0.40) + (Safety × 0.25) +
                             (Age-Match × 0.20) + (Goal-Alignment × 0.15)

Where each component is scored 1-10:

Evidence Score:
  - Strong Evidence = 10
  - Moderate Evidence = 6
  - Emerging Evidence = 3

Safety Score:
  - Excellent (FOXO3, SIRT6) = 10
  - Good (SIRT1, KLOTHO) = 8
  - Moderate (TERT with precautions) = 5
  - Uncertain (novel genes) = 3

Age-Match Score:
  - Optimal age group = 10
  - Acceptable age group = 6
  - Non-optimal age group = 3

Goal-Alignment Score:
  - Perfect match to patient goals = 10
  - Partial match = 6
  - Minimal match = 3

Total GSS range: 1-10 (normalized)
Recommended threshold: GSS ≥ 7.0 for treatment

Age-Based Recommendations

Different genes show optimal efficacy and safety profiles at different life stages. The standard provides age-stratified recommendations:

Age Group Primary Target Genes Rationale Expected Outcomes
18-40 SIRT1, FOXO3, SIRT6 Prevention focus; enhance cellular maintenance before damage accumulates Slow biological aging rate; establish healthspan foundation
40-60 SIRT1, FOXO3, AMPK, SIRT3 Metabolic health; maintain function as aging accelerates Prevent metabolic syndrome; preserve mitochondrial function
60-75 TERT, KLOTHO, SIRT6, FOXO3 Cellular repair; address accumulated damage; telomere maintenance becomes critical Reverse biological age 5-10 years; restore tissue function
75+ TERT, KLOTHO, GDF11 Tissue rejuvenation; systemic restoration; prioritize quality of life Extend healthspan; maintain independence; cognitive preservation

2.5 Multi-Gene Combinations

While single-gene interventions can extend healthspan, combining genes targeting different aging pathways may produce synergistic effects. However, multi-gene editing increases complexity and off-target risk.

Recommended Combinations

Metabolic Enhancement Stack (Ages 40-60):

  • SIRT1 (caloric restriction mimetic) + AMPK (energy sensor) + SIRT3 (mitochondrial function)
  • Rationale: Coordinate metabolic pathways for comprehensive improvement
  • Expected outcome: Enhanced insulin sensitivity, improved lipid profiles, increased energy

Cellular Repair Stack (Ages 60-75):

  • FOXO3 (stress response) + SIRT6 (DNA repair/inflammation) + KLOTHO (systemic protection)
  • Rationale: Multi-pronged approach to cellular maintenance and damage reversal
  • Expected outcome: Biological age reduction 5-10 years

Rejuvenation Stack (Ages 75+):

  • TERT (telomere restoration) + KLOTHO (cognitive/systemic) + GDF11 (tissue rejuvenation)
  • Rationale: Restore youthful function at cellular and systemic levels
  • Expected outcome: Extended independence, cognitive preservation, improved physical function

Multi-Gene Considerations:

  • Increased off-target risk (multiplicative, not additive)
  • Requires enhanced whole-genome sequencing monitoring
  • Consider sequential rather than simultaneous editing
  • Start with safest genes (FOXO3, SIRT6) before adding others

2.6 Patient-Specific Factors

Beyond age, multiple patient-specific factors influence gene selection:

  • Baseline Biological Age: Patients with accelerated aging (biological age >5 years above chronological) may benefit more from cellular repair genes
  • Disease Risk Profile: Family history of cardiovascular disease → prioritize APOE editing, SIRT1; Alzheimer's risk → KLOTHO, APOE
  • Current Health Status: Metabolic syndrome → SIRT1, AMPK; Inflammation → SIRT6; Cognitive decline → KLOTHO
  • Genetic Background: Existing beneficial variants (e.g., FOXO3 longevity alleles) may reduce benefit of upregulation
  • Lifestyle Factors: Exercise enthusiasts may derive less benefit from AMPK upregulation (already activated by exercise)

Key Takeaways

  • Longevity genes are classified into eight functional categories, each targeting different aging mechanisms (telomeres, metabolism, DNA repair, inflammation, etc.)
  • Evidence classification (Strong/Moderate/Emerging) guides clinical application priority, with Strong Evidence genes (FOXO3, SIRT1, SIRT6) recommended for initial treatments
  • FOXO3 has the strongest human genetic evidence and excellent safety profile, making it ideal for first-line interventions
  • TERT offers powerful telomere restoration but requires strict cancer surveillance due to increased malignancy risk
  • The Gene Selection Score algorithm integrates evidence strength, safety profile, age-appropriateness, and patient goals to optimize gene selection
  • Age-stratified recommendations prioritize prevention (ages 18-40), metabolic health (40-60), cellular repair (60-75), and rejuvenation (75+)
  • Multi-gene combinations may produce synergistic effects but increase complexity and require enhanced safety monitoring
