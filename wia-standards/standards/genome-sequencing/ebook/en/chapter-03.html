Quality Metrics and Standards

Understanding quality metrics, coverage requirements, and WIA-BIO-002 standards for clinical-grade genome sequencing.

3.1 Phred Quality Scores

Quality scores are fundamental to assessing the reliability of sequencing data. The Phred quality score, developed by Phil Green in the late 1990s during the Human Genome Project, provides a quantitative measure of base-calling accuracy. Understanding and properly interpreting quality scores is essential for any practitioner working with sequencing data.

3.1.1 Mathematical Foundation

The Phred quality score Q represents the probability P of an incorrect base call using a logarithmic scale:

Phred Quality Score Formula: Q = -10 × log₁₀(P) Where: Q = Phred quality score P = Probability of incorrect base call Rearranging to solve for P: P = 10^(-Q/10) Common Quality Scores and Their Meaning: Q10: P = 10^(-10/10) = 10^(-1) = 0.1 = 10% error (90% accuracy) Q20: P = 10^(-20/10) = 10^(-2) = 0.01 = 1% error (99% accuracy) Q30: P = 10^(-30/10) = 10^(-3) = 0.001 = 0.1% error (99.9% accuracy) Q40: P = 10^(-40/10) = 10^(-4) = 0.0001 = 0.01% error (99.99% accuracy) Practical Interpretation: Q10 = 1 error per 10 bases Q20 = 1 error per 100 bases Q30 = 1 error per 1,000 bases Q40 = 1 error per 10,000 bases

3.1.2 Quality Score Distribution

Quality scores vary across the length of sequencing reads due to several technical factors. Illumina sequencing typically shows declining quality toward the end of reads as phasing and prephasing errors accumulate. Understanding these patterns helps identify systematic problems and optimize downstream analysis.

In Illumina sequencing, Read 1 (forward read) generally exhibits higher quality than Read 2 (reverse read) because the chemistry is optimized for the forward direction. This asymmetry is normal and expected, but the magnitude of the difference can indicate technical issues with the sequencing run.

Quality Score (Q) Error Probability Accuracy Error Rate Clinical Use
Q10 1 in 10 90% 10% Unacceptable
Q20 1 in 100 99% 1% Marginal
Q30 1 in 1,000 99.9% 0.1% Clinical standard
Q40 1 in 10,000 99.99% 0.01% Excellent
Q50 1 in 100,000 99.999% 0.001% Exceptional

3.1.3 WIA-BIO-002 Quality Standards

The WIA-BIO-002 standard defines minimum quality thresholds based on the intended use of sequencing data. Clinical applications require significantly higher quality than research applications due to the direct impact on patient care decisions.

WIA-BIO-002 Minimum Quality Requirements: Clinical Sequencing (Diagnostic Use): ├─ Q30 Percentage: ≥90% of bases ├─ Mean Quality Score: ≥35 ├─ Modal Quality Score: ≥38 ├─ Bases Below Q20: <2% └─ Bases Below Q10: <0.1% Research Sequencing (Non-Diagnostic): ├─ Q30 Percentage: ≥85% of bases ├─ Mean Quality Score: ≥32 ├─ Modal Quality Score: ≥35 ├─ Bases Below Q20: <5% └─ Bases Below Q10: <0.5% Per-Read Quality: ├─ Read 1 Q30: ≥88% ├─ Read 2 Q30: ≥82% (typically 4-6% lower than R1) ├─ Index Read Q30: ≥90% (critical for demultiplexing) └─ Minimum Read Quality: Mean Q-score ≥25

3.2 Sequencing Coverage and Depth

Sequencing coverage, also called depth, represents the average number of times each nucleotide position in the genome is sequenced. Adequate coverage is crucial for accurate variant calling, as it provides statistical power to distinguish true variants from sequencing errors.

3.2.1 Coverage Calculation

Theoretical coverage is calculated using the total number of sequencing reads, read length, and genome size. However, actual coverage varies across the genome due to technical biases and biological factors.

Coverage Calculation: Coverage = (Total Reads × Read Length) / Genome Size Example 1: Human Whole Genome Sequencing (30×) Given: - Target Coverage: 30× - Genome Size: 3,000,000,000 bp (3 Gb) - Read Length: 150 bp paired-end (2 × 150 = 300 bp effective) Total bases needed: 30 × 3,000,000,000 = 90,000,000,000 bp = 90 Gb Read pairs required: 90,000,000,000 ÷ 300 = 300,000,000 pairs Single-end reads: 600,000,000 reads Example 2: Whole Exome Sequencing (100×) Given: - Target Coverage: 100× - Exome Size: 50,000,000 bp (50 Mb, ~1.5% of genome) - Read Length: 150 bp paired-end Total bases needed: 100 × 50,000,000 = 5,000,000,000 bp = 5 Gb Read pairs required: 5,000,000,000 ÷ 300 = 16,666,667 pairs Single-end reads: 33,333,333 reads Coverage Statistics: Mean Coverage: Average depth across all positions Median Coverage: Middle value of coverage distribution Modal Coverage: Most frequent coverage value Coverage at X%: Percentage of bases covered at ≥X depth

3.2.3 Coverage Uniformity

Perfect sequencing would provide exactly the same coverage at every genomic position. In reality, coverage varies due to GC content bias, PCR amplification bias, capture efficiency differences (for targeted sequencing), and mappability issues. The WIA-BIO-002 standard includes metrics to assess coverage uniformity.

Coverage Uniformity Metrics (WIA-BIO-002): Percentage of Bases at Coverage Thresholds: ├─ Bases at ≥10× coverage: ≥95% (WGS clinical) ├─ Bases at ≥20× coverage: ≥90% (WGS clinical) ├─ Bases at ≥30× coverage: ≥85% (for 40× mean target) └─ Bases at 0× coverage: <1% (coverage gaps) Uniformity Score: Formula: (Bases at ≥0.2×mean) / Total bases Clinical Standard: ≥90% Example: For 40× mean coverage, ≥90% of bases should have ≥8× coverage Coefficient of Variation (CV): Formula: σ / μ (standard deviation / mean) Target: CV <0.3 for clinical sequencing Lower CV = more uniform coverage Coverage Distribution: ├─ Mean / Median ratio: 0.9-1.1 (good uniformity) ├─ 10th percentile / Mean: >0.5 └─ 90th percentile / Mean: <1.5 Exome/Panel Specific: ├─ Target bases at ≥20×: ≥95% ├─ Target bases at ≥100×: ≥80% (for 100× target) └─ Fold-80 base penalty: <2.0 (uniformity metric)

3.3 Mapping Quality

Mapping quality (MAPQ) quantifies the confidence that a read is correctly aligned to its reported position in the reference genome. Reads that can map to multiple locations (due to repetitive sequences) receive low MAPQ scores, while uniquely mapping reads receive high scores.

3.3.1 MAPQ Score Calculation

Similar to Phred scores, MAPQ uses a logarithmic scale where higher values indicate greater confidence in the alignment:

Mapping Quality Score: MAPQ = -10 × log₁₀(P_error) Where P_error is the probability that the alignment position is wrong. Common MAPQ Values and Interpretation: MAPQ 0: Read maps to multiple locations with equal probability P_error = 1.0 (100% probability of error) Usage: Often excluded from variant calling MAPQ 10: P_error = 0.1 (10% probability wrong position) 90% confidence in alignment Usage: Marginal; may be excluded MAPQ 20: P_error = 0.01 (1% probability wrong position) 99% confidence in alignment Usage: Common threshold for variant calling MAPQ 30: P_error = 0.001 (0.1% probability wrong position) 99.9% confidence in alignment Usage: High-quality threshold MAPQ 40: P_error = 0.0001 (0.01% probability wrong position) 99.99% confidence in alignment Usage: Preferred for clinical applications MAPQ 60: Unique mapping position (BWA-MEM convention) Effectively perfect alignment confidence Usage: Highest quality alignments Factors Affecting MAPQ: 1. Sequence uniqueness in reference genome 2. Read length (longer reads → higher MAPQ) 3. Number and quality of mismatches 4. Aligner algorithm and parameters 5. Presence of SNPs/indels vs reference

3.3.2 WIA-BIO-002 Mapping Requirements

Mapping Quality Requirements (WIA-BIO-002): Overall Mapping Metrics: ├─ Total Reads Mapped: ≥95% of input reads ├─ Properly Paired: ≥90% (for paired-end sequencing) ├─ Duplicate Rate: <20% (clinical), <30% (research) └─ Secondary Alignments: <5% of total reads MAPQ Distribution: ├─ Reads with MAPQ ≥20: ≥85% ├─ Reads with MAPQ ≥30: ≥75% ├─ Reads with MAPQ ≥60: ≥60% (BWA-MEM unique mappers) └─ Reads with MAPQ 0: <10% Variant Calling Thresholds: ├─ Minimum MAPQ for variant calls: 20 (standard) ├─ Clinical variant calling: MAPQ ≥30 preferred ├─ Somatic variant calling: MAPQ ≥40 recommended └─ Structural variant calling: MAPQ ≥30 Per-Chromosome Metrics: ├─ Chromosome-level mapping rate variation: <10% ├─ Sex chromosome mapping: Consistent with sample sex └─ Mitochondrial mapping: <5% of total reads

3.4 Duplication Metrics

PCR duplicates are multiple sequencing reads derived from the same original DNA molecule through PCR amplification during library preparation. High duplication rates reduce the effective coverage and can bias variant allele frequency estimates.

3.4.1 Identifying Duplicates

Duplicate reads are identified by having identical alignment start and end positions for both reads in a pair. The Picard MarkDuplicates tool is the standard method for duplicate identification in genomics. Among duplicate reads, the one with the highest sum of base qualities is retained as the representative.

3.5 Error Rate Assessment

Sequencing error rates are typically assessed using control DNA with known sequence. The PhiX bacteriophage genome (5,386 bp) is commonly spiked into Illumina sequencing runs at 1-5% to provide internal quality control.

3.5.1 PhiX Control Standards

PhiX Control Quality Metrics (WIA-BIO-002): PhiX Alignment: ├─ % Reads Aligning to PhiX: 0.5-2.0% (for 1% spike-in) ├─ PhiX Error Rate: <0.5% (Q30+ equivalent) ├─ PhiX Q30 Score: ≥90% └─ Deviation from expected %: ±0.3% Error Types (from PhiX): ├─ Substitution Errors: <0.3% ├─ Insertion Errors: <0.05% ├─ Deletion Errors: <0.05% └─ Total Error Rate: <0.4% Per-Cycle Error Rate: ├─ Cycle 1-50: <0.2% error ├─ Cycle 51-100: <0.5% error ├─ Cycle 101-150: <1.0% error ├─ Cycle 151+: <1.5% error └─ Error Rate Increase: <0.01% per cycle Quality Flags: ├─ PhiX Error >1%: FAIL - sequencing chemistry issue ├─ PhiX % deviation >1%: WARNING - loading issue └─ Uneven PhiX distribution across lanes: WARNING - flow cell issue

Key Takeaways

  • Phred quality scores provide a logarithmic measure of base-calling accuracy, with Q30 (99.9% accuracy) serving as the standard threshold for clinical sequencing.
  • Coverage depth requirements vary by application: 30-60× for clinical WGS, 80-120× for tumor sequencing, and 100-150× for clinical exome sequencing.
  • Coverage uniformity is as important as mean coverage; ≥90% of bases should be within 20-80% of mean coverage for clinical applications.
  • Mapping quality (MAPQ) scores quantify alignment confidence, with MAPQ ≥20 (99% confidence) as the minimum for variant calling and MAPQ ≥30 preferred for clinical use.
  • Duplication rates indicate library quality and complexity; rates should be <20% for WGS and <40% for WES to ensure adequate effective coverage.
  • PhiX control sequences provide internal quality assessment, with error rates <0.5% indicating acceptable sequencing chemistry performance.
  • WIA-BIO-002 standards define rigorous quality thresholds ensuring clinical-grade sequencing meets diagnostic requirements for accuracy and reliability.
