Genome Annotation and Interpretation

Transforming raw variant calls into clinically actionable information through comprehensive functional annotation and evidence-based interpretation frameworks.

6.1 The Annotation Ecosystem

Variant annotation is the process of adding biological context to raw variant calls. A variant call from GATK tells us that position chr1:12345 differs from the reference, but annotation tells us this position lies within the BRCA1 gene, causes a frameshift mutation, predicts protein truncation, and has been classified as pathogenic in ClinVar. This transformation from coordinates to clinical meaning is what enables genomic medicine.

The WIA-BIO-002 standard requires comprehensive multi-layered annotation integrating dozens of databases and prediction algorithms. Modern annotation tools like Variant Effect Predictor (VEP), ANNOVAR, and SnpEff automatically query these resources, producing richly annotated VCF files that serve as the foundation for clinical interpretation.

Annotation Information Layers: VARIANT POSITION: chr17:43044295 C>T LAYER 1: Gene Context ├─ Gene: BRCA1 ├─ Transcript: ENST00000357654 ├─ Exon: 11/23 └─ Strand: - (reverse) LAYER 2: Molecular Consequence ├─ Type: missense_variant ├─ cDNA change: c.4837C>T ├─ Protein change: p.Arg1613Trp └─ Impact: MODERATE LAYER 3: Population Frequency ├─ gnomAD: 0.0001 (0.01%) ├─ 1000 Genomes: 0.0002 ├─ ExAC: 0.00015 └─ Interpretation: Rare variant LAYER 4: Clinical Significance ├─ ClinVar: Pathogenic ├─ COSMIC: Not in cancer database ├─ OMIM: Associated with breast cancer susceptibility └─ HGMD: Disease-causing mutation LAYER 5: Functional Prediction ├─ SIFT: Deleterious (0.01) ├─ PolyPhen-2: Probably damaging (0.95) ├─ CADD: 25.3 (highly deleterious) └─ REVEL: 0.88 (pathogenic) LAYER 6: Conservation ├─ PhyloP: 5.2 (highly conserved) ├─ GERP++: 4.8 └─ Interpretation: Evolutionarily constrained

6.2 Functional Annotation Tools

Three major annotation frameworks dominate the genomics landscape, each with distinct strengths. The WIA-BIO-002 standard recommends using at least one of these tools for compliant annotation workflows.

6.2.1 Variant Effect Predictor (VEP)

Developed by Ensembl, VEP provides comprehensive annotation using Ensembl gene builds. It predicts molecular consequences, retrieves regulatory features, and integrates dozens of data sources in a single analysis pass. VEP's ontology-based consequence terms provide standardized classification of variant effects.

Consequence Description Impact Example
stop_gained Nonsense variant introducing stop codon HIGH p.Arg123Ter
frameshift_variant Indel causing reading frame shift HIGH p.Lys100fs
splice_donor_variant Disrupts GT splice donor site HIGH c.456+1G>A
missense_variant Non-synonymous SNP changing amino acid MODERATE p.Val600Glu
inframe_deletion Deletion preserving reading frame MODERATE p.Phe508del
synonymous_variant SNP not changing amino acid LOW p.Leu123=
intron_variant Variant within intron MODIFIER c.123+50G>A

6.2.2 ANNOVAR

ANNOVAR (ANNOtate VARiation) uses a gene-based annotation approach and excels at integrating diverse annotation databases. Its modular design allows easy addition of custom annotation sources. ANNOVAR is particularly popular for integrating population databases and disease-specific annotations. 

ANNOVAR Annotation Workflow:

1. Gene-Based Annotation
   - RefSeq, UCSC, Ensembl gene models
   - Exonic, intronic, UTR, intergenic
   - Functional consequence prediction

2. Filter-Based Annotation
   - Population frequencies (gnomAD, 1000G, ExAC)
   - Variant databases (dbSNP, ClinVar, COSMIC)
   - Conservation scores (PhyloP, GERP, SiPhy)

3. Region-Based Annotation
   - ENCODE regulatory elements
   - DNase hypersensitivity sites
   - Transcription factor binding sites
   - ChromHMM chromatin states

4. Custom Annotations
   - Laboratory-specific databases
   - Gene panels for specific diseases
   - Local variant repositories

6.3 Population Frequency Databases

Population allele frequencies are among the most powerful filters for variant interpretation. Common variants (MAF >1%) are unlikely to cause rare Mendelian diseases, while ultra-rare variants deserve closer scrutiny. The WIA-BIO-002 standard requires annotation with at least gnomAD frequencies.

6.3.1 gnomAD: Genome Aggregation Database

gnomAD aggregates 125,748 exomes and 71,702 genomes from diverse global populations, representing the largest public allele frequency resource. Version 3.1 provides coverage-adjusted allele frequencies, accounting for differences in sequencing depth across the genome.

6.3.2 Filtering by Allele Frequency

WIA-BIO-002 Frequency Thresholds:

  • Dominant Rare Disease: Filter variants with gnomAD AF >0.0001 (0.01%)
  • Recessive Rare Disease: Filter variants with gnomAD AF >0.01 (1%)
  • Common Disease Risk: Consider variants 0.01-0.50 (1-50%)
  • Pharmacogenomics: Include common variants (AF >1%)
  • Population-Specific: Consider allele frequency in patient's ancestry
  • Absence from gnomAD: Not automatically pathogenic; may be ultra-rare benign or private mutation

6.4 Clinical Variant Databases

While population databases tell us what's common, clinical databases tell us what's pathogenic. Integration with curated clinical resources is mandatory for WIA-BIO-002 compliance, as these databases represent decades of clinical genetic experience.

6.4.1 ClinVar

ClinVar aggregates clinical interpretations of genetic variants from laboratories, researchers, and expert panels worldwide. It provides a five-tier classification system aligned with ACMG/AMP guidelines. However, ClinVar entries can conflict, requiring careful interpretation. 

ClinVar Classification Categories:

1. Pathogenic (P)
   - Sufficient evidence of disease causation
   - Reported in affected individuals
   - Functional studies support pathogenicity
   - Example: BRCA1 c.68_69delAG (breast cancer)

2. Likely Pathogenic (LP)
   - Strong but not definitive evidence
   - Computational prediction supports pathogenicity
   - Variant in critical functional domain
   - Example: Novel frameshift in known disease gene

3. Uncertain Significance (VUS)
   - Insufficient evidence for classification
   - Conflicting interpretations
   - Limited clinical or functional data
   - ~40% of ClinVar submissions are VUS

4. Likely Benign (LB)
   - Evidence suggests benign nature
   - High population frequency
   - Computational prediction benign
   - In trans with pathogenic variant in healthy individual

5. Benign (B)
   - Conclusive evidence of benign nature
   - Population frequency >1%
   - Functional studies show no impact
   - Example: Common synonymous variants

Review Status:

★★★  Expert panel reviewed
★★   Multiple submitters, no conflicts

     No assertion criteria provided

6.4.2 COSMIC: Catalogue of Somatic Mutations in Cancer

COSMIC curates somatic mutations identified in human cancers. It provides mutation frequencies across cancer types, drug resistance associations, and census genes known to drive cancer. Essential for somatic variant interpretation in oncology.

6.5 Pathogenicity Prediction Algorithms

When clinical databases lack information on a variant, computational prediction algorithms estimate pathogenicity based on evolutionary conservation, protein structure, and machine learning models trained on known pathogenic variants.

Algorithm Score Range Pathogenic Threshold Approach
SIFT 0-1 <0.05 (deleterious) Sequence conservation
PolyPhen-2 0-1 >0.85 (probably damaging) Structure + conservation
CADD 0-99 (Phred) >20 (top 1% deleterious) Machine learning ensemble
REVEL 0-1 >0.5 (pathogenic) Meta-predictor (13 tools)
MetaSVM 0-1 >0.5 (damaging) SVM ensemble
GERP++ -12.3 to 6.17 >2 (conserved) Evolutionary constraint

Important Caveat: Prediction Limitations

Computational predictions have ~75-85% accuracy and should NEVER be the sole evidence for pathogenicity classification. Per ACMG/AMP guidelines, predictions provide only "supporting" evidence. Clinical diagnosis requires multiple lines of evidence including segregation analysis, functional studies, and clinical correlation.

6.6 ACMG/AMP Classification Framework

The American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) and Association for Molecular Pathology (AMP) published standardized guidelines for variant interpretation in 2015, revolutionizing clinical genomics by providing an evidence-based framework that reduced inter-laboratory discordance.

6.6.1 Evidence Categories

Pathogenic Evidence Codes:

Very Strong (PVS1):

  • Null variant (nonsense, frameshift, splice site, initiation codon, single/multi-exon deletion) in gene where loss-of-function is known disease mechanism

Strong (PS1-PS4):

  • PS1: Same amino acid change as established pathogenic variant
  • PS2: De novo variant in patient with disease, confirmed paternity/maternity
  • PS3: Well-established functional studies show deleterious effect
  • PS4: Variant prevalence in affected significantly increased vs controls

Moderate (PM1-PM6):

  • PM1: Located in mutational hot spot or critical functional domain
  • PM2: Absent/extremely rare in population databases
  • PM3: Detected in trans with pathogenic variant (recessive disease)
  • PM5: Novel missense at same position as known pathogenic missense

Supporting (PP1-PP5):

  • PP3: Multiple computational predictors support deleterious effect
  • PP4: Patient phenotype highly specific for gene
  • PP5: Reputable source reports variant as pathogenic

6.6.2 Classification Rules

Pathogenic Variant Requires:
- 1 PVS1 + ≥1 PS, OR
- ≥2 PS, OR
- 1 PS + ≥3 PM, OR
- 1 PS + 2 PM + 2 PP, OR
- ≥5 PM, OR
- Other specific combinations detailed in guidelines

Likely Pathogenic Requires:
- 1 PVS1 + 1 PM, OR
- 1 PS + 1-2 PM, OR
- 1 PS + ≥2 PP, OR
- ≥3 PM, OR
- 2 PM + ≥2 PP, OR
- 1 PM + ≥4 PP

Benign/Likely Benign evidence (BA1, BS1-BS4, BP1-BP7)
weighted similarly with standalone/strong/supporting levels.

Key Takeaways

  • Annotation transforms genomic coordinates into biological meaning by integrating gene models, population frequencies, clinical databases, and prediction algorithms across multiple information layers.
  • VEP, ANNOVAR, and SnpEff are the three dominant annotation tools, each with distinct strengths; WIA-BIO-002 requires use of at least one for standardized consequence annotation.
  • gnomAD population frequencies serve as a primary filter for rare disease variant analysis, with AF <0.01% threshold for dominant disorders and <1% for recessive conditions.
  • ClinVar aggregates clinical interpretations from global sources but requires careful evaluation of review status and resolution of conflicting assertions.
  • Computational prediction algorithms provide supporting evidence but have limited accuracy (~75-85%); predictions alone are insufficient for pathogenicity classification.
  • ACMG/AMP guidelines establish evidence-based framework using 28 criteria weighted as very strong/strong/moderate/supporting to standardize variant classification.
  • Clinical interpretation requires integration of multiple evidence types: segregation, functional data, population data, and computational predictions to achieve confident classification.
