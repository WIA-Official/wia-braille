Next-Generation Sequencing Technologies

Exploring the major sequencing platforms, their chemistries, applications, and quality parameters defined in the WIA-BIO-002 standard.

2.1 Illumina Next-Generation Sequencing

Illumina's sequencing-by-synthesis (SBS) technology has become the dominant platform for genome sequencing worldwide, accounting for over 80% of all sequencing data generated. The technology's success stems from its exceptional accuracy, high throughput, and continuously decreasing costs. Understanding Illumina sequencing chemistry and quality parameters is essential for any practitioner working in genomics.

2.1.1 Technology Overview and Chemistry

Illumina sequencing employs a sophisticated multi-step process that enables massively parallel sequencing of millions of DNA fragments simultaneously. The process begins with library preparation, where genomic DNA is fragmented into smaller pieces (typically 300-500 bp) and adapter sequences are ligated to both ends of each fragment. These adapters serve two critical functions: they enable the DNA fragments to bind to the flow cell surface, and they provide primer binding sites for sequencing.

The sequencing process utilizes bridge amplification to create clonal clusters of DNA molecules. Each fragment binds to the flow cell surface via its adapter sequences, and through repeated cycles of isothermal bridge amplification, each single molecule is amplified to create a cluster containing approximately 1,000 identical copies. This amplification is essential because the fluorescent signal from a single molecule would be too weak to detect accurately.

2.1.2 Sequencing-by-Synthesis Process

The sequencing reaction employs reversible terminator chemistry, one of the most elegant molecular biology innovations of the 21st century. In each sequencing cycle, all four nucleotides (A, C, G, T) are added simultaneously to the flow cell, each labeled with a different fluorescent dye and carrying a removable chemical blocking group at the 3' position.

DNA polymerase incorporates the complementary nucleotide to the template strand, but the blocking group prevents incorporation of additional nucleotides. After incorporation, unbound nucleotides are washed away, and the flow cell is imaged using laser excitation and high-resolution cameras. Each cluster emits light at a wavelength corresponding to the incorporated nucleotide, allowing base identification.

After imaging, a chemical cleavage step removes both the fluorescent dye and the 3' blocking group, regenerating a free 3'-OH group and allowing the next cycle of nucleotide incorporation. This process repeats for typically 150 to 300 cycles, sequencing each cluster base-by-base.

2.1.5 Applications and Use Cases

Illumina platforms serve diverse applications across basic research and clinical diagnostics:

  • Whole Genome Sequencing (WGS): Comprehensive analysis of entire genomes at 30-60× coverage for clinical diagnostics or 10-20× for population studies
  • Whole Exome Sequencing (WES): Targeted sequencing of protein-coding regions (~1% of genome) at 100-150× coverage for Mendelian disease diagnosis
  • RNA Sequencing (RNA-seq): Transcriptome profiling to measure gene expression, identify splice variants, and discover fusion transcripts
  • Targeted Panel Sequencing: Deep sequencing (500-5000×) of specific gene panels for cancer diagnostics, carrier screening, or pharmacogenomics
  • Metagenomics: Sequencing of microbial communities from environmental or clinical samples
  • ChIP-seq and ATAC-seq: Mapping of protein-DNA interactions and chromatin accessibility
  • Methylation Analysis: Bisulfite or enzymatic conversion followed by sequencing to map DNA methylation patterns

2.2 PacBio HiFi Sequencing

Pacific Biosciences (PacBio) HiFi sequencing represents a paradigm shift in DNA sequencing technology, combining the long read lengths of third-generation sequencing with the high accuracy traditionally associated with short-read platforms. This technology has revolutionized applications requiring long-range genomic information.

2.2.1 Single-Molecule Real-Time (SMRT) Technology

PacBio sequencing utilizes zero-mode waveguides (ZMWs), which are nanophotonic structures that enable observation of single-molecule DNA synthesis in real-time. Each ZMW is a tiny well (100-150 nanometers in diameter) etched into a metal film on a glass substrate. The wells are so small that only the bottom ~30 nanometers are illuminated when light is directed from below.

A single DNA polymerase molecule is immobilized at the bottom of each ZMW, along with a template DNA molecule. When fluorescently-labeled nucleotides enter the illuminated volume and are incorporated by the polymerase, they emit a burst of fluorescence. The color and duration of this signal identify the incorporated base. After incorporation, the fluorophore is cleaved and diffuses away, and the cycle repeats.

2.2.2 HiFi Reads Through Circular Consensus Sequencing

The key innovation enabling PacBio HiFi sequencing is circular consensus sequencing (CCS). Library preparation creates SMRTbell templates - circular DNA molecules with hairpin adapters connecting the two strands of the insert. The highly processive PacBio polymerase can sequence around this circular template multiple times in a single sequencing reaction.

Each pass around the circle generates a "subread" with typical single-pass accuracy of 85-90%. However, by combining information from multiple passes (typically 10-15 for HiFi), a highly accurate consensus sequence can be generated. The resulting HiFi reads achieve >99.9% (Q30) accuracy while maintaining read lengths of 10-25 kilobases - far longer than any Illumina read.

2.2.4 Applications of PacBio HiFi

PacBio HiFi sequencing excels in applications where long-range information and high accuracy are both required:

  • De Novo Genome Assembly: Long reads enable assembly of complete chromosomes without gaps, resolving complex repetitive regions
  • Structural Variant Detection: Direct detection of deletions, insertions, inversions, and translocations from 50 bp to megabases
  • Full-Length Transcript Sequencing: Isoform discovery and quantification without assembly or computational inference
  • Haplotype Phasing: Long reads span multiple heterozygous sites, enabling direct phasing of variants onto maternal and paternal chromosomes
  • Repeat Expansion Disorders: Accurate sizing of tandem repeats associated with diseases like Huntington's and fragile X syndrome
  • Methylation Detection: Native detection of 5mC and 6mA modifications from kinetic signatures without bisulfite conversion

2.3 Oxford Nanopore Sequencing

Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT) has developed a fundamentally different approach to DNA sequencing based on measuring changes in electrical current as DNA molecules pass through protein nanopores. This technology offers unique advantages including ultra-long reads, real-time sequencing, and direct RNA sequencing capabilities.

2.3.1 Nanopore Sequencing Principle

Nanopore sequencing employs biological or engineered protein pores embedded in an electrically resistant membrane. A constant voltage is applied across the membrane, creating an ionic current through the nanopore. When a DNA molecule is threaded through the pore by a motor protein, different nucleotides cause characteristic disruptions to this current.

The current signal is measured at high frequency (thousands of measurements per second) and sophisticated algorithms decode these current changes into DNA sequence. Recent chemistry versions (R10.4) have improved base-calling accuracy to 99%+ for both DNA and RNA.

2.3.4 Unique Advantages of Nanopore Sequencing

ONT technology offers several capabilities unavailable with other platforms:

  • Ultra-Long Reads: Regular reads exceeding 100 kb, with documented reads over 4 megabases enabling complete chromosome assembly
  • Real-Time Analysis: Base calls available within seconds of DNA passing through pore, enabling adaptive sampling and rapid diagnostics
  • Direct RNA Sequencing: Native RNA sequencing without reverse transcription, preserving modifications and eliminating PCR bias
  • Native Modification Detection: Direct detection of DNA methylation, hydroxymethylation, and other epigenetic marks without chemical conversion
  • Portability: MinION device powered by laptop USB, enabling field sequencing for outbreak investigation and environmental monitoring
  • Adaptive Sampling: Real-time selection of molecules to sequence or reject based on reference comparison, enriching for regions of interest

2.4 Single-Cell Sequencing Technologies

Single-cell sequencing technologies have revolutionized our understanding of cellular heterogeneity by enabling genomic, transcriptomic, and epigenomic analysis of individual cells. The WIA-BIO-002 standard includes specifications for single-cell RNA-seq (scRNA-seq) and single-cell ATAC-seq (scATAC-seq).

2.4.1 Droplet-Based Single-Cell RNA-seq

The 10x Genomics Chromium platform represents the most widely adopted single-cell technology. Individual cells are encapsulated in nanoliter-scale droplets along with barcoded gel beads. Each gel bead contains millions of oligonucleotides with a unique cellular barcode, enabling all transcripts from a single cell to be labeled with the same identifier.

Key Takeaways

  • Illumina sequencing-by-synthesis dominates the market with exceptional accuracy (>99.9%) and throughput (up to 6 Tb per run), making it ideal for WGS, WES, and RNA-seq applications.
  • PacBio HiFi sequencing combines long reads (10-25 kb) with high accuracy (>99.9%) through circular consensus sequencing, excelling at de novo assembly, structural variant detection, and haplotype phasing.
  • Oxford Nanopore technology offers ultra-long reads (100 kb to >4 Mb), real-time sequencing, portable devices, and direct RNA sequencing capabilities unavailable with other platforms.
  • Each platform has distinct advantages: Illumina for high accuracy and throughput, PacBio for accurate long reads, and Nanopore for read length and real-time analysis.
  • WIA-BIO-002 quality standards define specific metrics for each platform including cluster density, read accuracy, coverage uniformity, and error rates to ensure clinical-grade results.
  • Single-cell technologies enable analysis of cellular heterogeneity at unprecedented resolution, revealing rare cell types and developmental trajectories.
  • Platform selection depends on application: Consider required read length, accuracy, throughput, turnaround time, and cost when choosing a sequencing technology.
