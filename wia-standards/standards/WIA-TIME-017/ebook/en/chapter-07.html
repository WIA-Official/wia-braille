Chapter 7: Calibration, Monitoring & Diagnostics

Ensuring safe, reliable chronosphere operation requires comprehensive calibration procedures before each displacement, continuous monitoring during operation, and sophisticated diagnostic systems detecting faults before they cascade into failures.

Pre-Flight Calibration

15-minute mandatory sequence includes: Power-On Self-Test (60s testing all systems), Temporal Field Alignment (120s achieving <0.1% uniformity), Sensor Cross-Calibration (180s with ±0.2°C, ±0.1 kPa, ±0.5% gas, ±0.01g precision), Life Support Verification (300s testing O₂ generation >83 g/hour, CO₂ scrubbing 1000→<400 ppm in 5 min), and Emergency Systems Test (120s simulating fire, cardiac arrest, field collapse).

200+ Sensor Network

Comprehensive monitoring via structural sensors (50 strain gauges, 12 accelerometers, 8 vibrometers, 16 impact detectors), temporal sensors (100 Hall effect field strength, 24 field geometry, 4 radiation monitors, 4 chronometers), environmental sensors (20 temperature, 8 pressure, 4 humidity, 12 gas composition, 8 airflow), power monitoring (4 voltage, 8 current, 2 battery, 4 temperature), passenger sensors (16 seat sensors, 4 vital signs monitors, 4 motion detectors, 8 interior cameras), and external sensors (8 cameras, 4 LIDAR, 2 radar, 4 thermal, GPS, star tracker).

Built-In Test Equipment (BITE)

Automated diagnostics test power (voltage, battery capacity, load distribution), temporal field (strength uniformity, geometry accuracy, leakage), life support (O₂/CO₂ levels, pressurization leak rate), environmental systems (temperature zones, humidity, air circulation), communications (intercom, radio, temporal messaging), navigation (sensors, position accuracy), medical equipment (defibrillator, monitoring accuracy), and emergency systems (fire detection/suppression, emergency power, beacon). Overall health calculation: (passed tests / total tests) × 100%, with mission readiness threshold >95%.

Fault Detection and Isolation

Statistical anomaly detection using 3-sigma rule identifies sensor readings outside 99.7% confidence interval. Cross-verification with nearby sensors isolates sensor failures versus real anomalies. If sensor disagrees with neighbors by >10%, flags as faulty and switches to backup. Real anomalies trigger graduated response: advisory (minor, <5 min action), caution (developing, <2 min), warning (significant, <1 min), urgent (critical, <30 sec), emergency (life-threatening, <10 sec).

What percentage of normal readings false-alarm under this threshold? Discuss trade-offs between sensitivity (detecting real anomalies) and specificity (avoiding false alarms).
  3. Evaluate pre-flight calibration completeness. Why must temporal field alignment precede sensor calibration rather than parallel execution? What failure cascades could occur if alignment uses uncalibrated sensors or sensors calibrate in misaligned field?

If three sensors read 22°C and two read 27°C, what conclusion follows? Develop algorithmic rules for majority voting versus median filtering.

Start with top event 'Insufficient O₂' and identify contributing failures: generator malfunction, power loss, sensor error, leak, increased consumption. Calculate overall failure probability if each component has 0.1% annual failure rate and systems have no redundancy versus 2× redundancy.
