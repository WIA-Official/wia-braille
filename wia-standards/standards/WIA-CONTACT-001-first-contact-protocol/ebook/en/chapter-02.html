Chapter 2: Signal Detection and Verification

2.1 Detection Methods

Modern SETI employs multiple detection strategies across the electromagnetic spectrum. Radio frequencies remain the primary focus, particularly the "Water Hole" (1420-1666 MHz), but optical SETI, infrared, and other methods complement radio searches.

2.2 Verification Requirements

A signal must pass rigorous verification before being considered authentic:

  • Minimum 3 independent observatory confirmations
  • Geographic diversity (observations from 3+ continents)
  • Temporal consistency (repeated detections)
  • Parameter agreement across all confirmations
  • Scientific consensus threshold: 95%+

2.3 False Positive Elimination

The protocol includes systematic checks to eliminate false positives: satellite transmissions, aircraft radar, terrestrial radio sources, equipment malfunctions, and natural astrophysical phenomena must all be ruled out before proceeding.

2.4 Multi-Site Coordination

The global observatory network operates 24/7, with automated alert systems triggering verification requests within minutes of detection. This distributed approach ensures robust confirmation and prevents single-point failures.
