Chapter 4: Health Monitoring & Veterinary Records

4.1 Digital Health Records

Complete medical history from birth to slaughter, linked to RFID tag for instant veterinarian access.

Record TypeData CapturedFrequency
VaccinationsVaccine name, batch, date, veterinarian, next due dateAnnual or per protocol
TreatmentsDiagnosis, medication, dosage, withdrawal periodAs needed
CheckupsWeight, temperature, body condition score, notesMonthly or quarterly
BreedingHeat detection, AI/natural breeding, pregnancy testsPer cycle
CalvingCalving ease, calf weight, colostrum intakePer birth

4.2 IoT Health Sensors

4.2.1 Temperature Monitoring

Early fever detection (38.5°C normal for cattle, >39.5°C indicates illness).

  • Ear Tag Thermometer: Continuous monitoring via RFID/Bluetooth
  • Bolus Thermometer: Rumen temperature (most accurate core temp)
  • Alert Threshold: Send SMS/app notification when fever detected

4.3 Disease Prevention

DiseaseDetection MethodPrevention
Foot & Mouth Disease (FMD)Activity drop, lameness detectionVaccination, quarantine of new animals
Bovine Respiratory Disease (BRD)Temperature spike, reduced movementMinimize stress, proper ventilation
Mastitis (dairy)Temperature increase, reduced milk yieldHygiene, teat dipping, regular screening
BloatGPS shows animal not moving, distressedAvoid sudden diet changes, monitor legume pasture

4.5 Growth Tracking & ADG

Average Daily Gain (ADG) is the key metric for beef cattle profitability:

  • Formula: ADG = (Current Weight - Previous Weight) / Days Elapsed
  • Target: 0.8-1.2 kg/day for beef cattle (varies by breed, feed)
  • Tracking: Automatic weigh scales with RFID readers (walkover scales)

Summary

Digital health records, IoT sensors, and data-driven disease prevention transform livestock management from reactive to proactive. Early detection of illness reduces mortality, improves welfare, and lowers antibiotic use.
