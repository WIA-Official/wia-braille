CHAPTER 05

Alternative Substances and Technologies

5.1 Natural Refrigerants: The Ultimate Solution

Natural refrigerants—substances found in nature that can be used for cooling without chemical synthesis—represent the most sustainable long-term solution for refrigeration and air conditioning. These include hydrocarbons (HCs), ammonia (NH₃), carbon dioxide (CO₂), water, and air.

5.1.1 Hydrocarbons (R-290, R-600a, R-1270)

Hydrocarbon refrigerants offer outstanding environmental credentials combined with excellent thermodynamic properties:

Refrigerant Chemical Name Formula ODP GWP Primary Applications
R-290 Propane C₃H₈ 0 3 Commercial refrigeration, small AC, heat pumps
R-600a Isobutane C₄H₁₀ (iso) 0 3 Domestic refrigerators, small freezers
R-1270 Propylene/Propene C₃H₆ 0 2 Industrial refrigeration, cascades
R-170 Ethane C₂H₆ 0 6 Cascade systems, ultra-low temp
Hydrocarbon Advantages and Safety Management

Environmental Benefits:

  • Zero ODP - no ozone depletion impact
  • Negligible GWP (3-6) - minimal climate impact
  • Natural substances - biodegradable, non-toxic
  • No regulatory phase-out risk - sustainable long-term

Performance Advantages:

  • Excellent thermodynamic efficiency - often 5-15% better than synthetic refrigerants
  • Good material compatibility with common metals, elastomers
  • Superior heat transfer properties
  • Lower system pressures in many applications
  • Compatible with mineral oils - simpler systems

Economic Advantages:

  • Low cost - typically 1/3 to 1/2 price of HFCs/HFOs
  • No patent restrictions - freely available
  • Reduced charge sizes - lower refrigerant inventory costs
  • Lower operating costs due to efficiency

Safety Considerations (Flammability Management):

  • Charge Minimization: Design systems with smallest possible refrigerant charge
  • Leak Prevention: High-quality brazed joints, avoid mechanical connections
  • Ventilation: Adequate air circulation in machinery rooms and around equipment
  • Ignition Source Control: Electrical components rated for flammable atmospheres
  • Detection Systems: Refrigerant leak detectors with automatic equipment shutdown
  • Separation: Compressors isolated from occupied spaces where possible
  • Safety Standards: IEC 60335-2-89, ISO 5149, EN 378 compliance
  • Charge Limits: Typically 150g for household appliances, varies by application
  • Training: Technicians certified in flammable refrigerant handling

Real-World Safety Record:

  • >1 billion hydrocarbon refrigerators in use globally (R-600a)
  • Safety incident rate comparable to non-flammable refrigerants
  • Proper design eliminates risk - decades of safe operation demonstrated
  • European standard since 1990s - extensive safety data available

5.1.2 Ammonia (R-717)

Ammonia has been used as a refrigerant since the 1870s and remains the workhorse of industrial refrigeration:

Property Value/Description
Chemical Formula NH₃
ODP 0 (zero ozone depletion)
GWP 0 (zero direct climate impact)
Efficiency Excellent - typically 10-20% better than synthetic refrigerants
Operating Pressure Moderate (2.9 bar @ -15°C evaporating)
Toxicity B2L - toxic but strong odor provides warning (detectable at 5 ppm)
Flammability Mildly flammable (narrow flammability range: 15-28% by volume)
Material Compatibility Excellent with steel; attacks copper, brass, bronze
Cost Very low - commodity chemical
Typical Applications Cold storage, food processing, ice rinks, industrial processes

        │  • Highest efficiency                                   │

        └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

        CONFIGURATION 2: Secondary Loop (Indirect)
        ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │  Primary: NH₃ system in machinery room                  │
        │  ↓ cools secondary fluid (glycol, brine, CO₂)          │
        │  Secondary: Circulated to cooling load                  │
        │                                                          │
        │  • Reduced NH₃ charge (50-90% less)                     │
        │  • NH₃ confined to controlled area                      │

        │  • Slightly lower efficiency (5-10%)                    │
        │  • Growing adoption for safety reasons                  │
        └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

        CONFIGURATION 3: NH₃/CO₂ Cascade
        ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │  High Stage: NH₃ (-10 to +30°C)                        │
        │  ↓ condenses CO₂                                        │
        │  Low Stage: CO₂ (-35 to -10°C)                         │
        │  ↓ to cooling load                                      │
        │                                                          │
        │  • Minimal NH₃ charge (machinery room only)            │
        │  • CO₂ in occupied spaces (non-toxic, non-flammable)   │
        │  • Excellent efficiency                                 │
        │  • Combines best properties of both refrigerants       │
        │  • Ideal for supermarkets, food retail                  │
        │  • Premium solution - higher capital cost               │
        └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

        SAFETY SYSTEMS FOR AMMONIA:

        Leak Detection:
        • Continuous NH₃ monitoring (5-25 ppm alarm levels)
        • Multiple sensors in machinery room and occupied zones
        • Automatic ventilation activation
        • Emergency shutdown systems

        Containment:
        • Machinery room isolation with negative pressure
        • Emergency exhaust fans (10-30 air changes/hour)
        • Sealed penetrations
        • Explosion-proof electrical equipment

        Training & Procedures:
        • IIAR (International Institute of Ammonia Refrigeration) standards
        • Operator certification programs
        • Emergency response plans
        • Regular safety drills

        Design Standards:
        • IIAR 2 (Equipment, Design, and Installation)
        • ASHRAE 15 (Safety Standard for Refrigeration Systems)
        • PSM (Process Safety Management) for large charges
        • Regular inspections and maintenance protocols

        TRACK RECORD:
        • 150+ years of safe industrial use
        • Dominant refrigerant in food industry globally
        • Excellent safety record with proper design/operations
        • >1 million tonnes used annually worldwide

5.1.3 Carbon Dioxide (R-744)

CO₂ refrigeration represents one of the fastest-growing natural refrigerant technologies, particularly in commercial refrigeration and heat pump applications:

CO₂ Refrigeration: Properties and Applications

Environmental Credentials:

  • ODP: 0 (zero ozone depletion)
  • GWP: 1 (baseline - negligible climate impact from refrigerant leaks)
  • Non-toxic, non-flammable (A1 safety classification)
  • Abundant, inexpensive ($0.50-2/kg vs. $10-50/kg for synthetic refrigerants)
  • No regulatory restrictions or phase-out risk

Technical Characteristics:

  • Critical Point: 31.1°C, 73.8 bar (transcritical operation required in warm climates)
  • Operating Pressures: High (30-130 bar) - requires robust equipment
  • Volumetric Capacity: Very high - allows compact equipment
  • Heat Transfer: Excellent - good efficiency potential
  • Temperature Glide: None (pure substance) - simplifies control

Applications:

  • Supermarket Refrigeration: >30,000 installations globally, especially dominant in Northern Europe
  • Cascade Systems: Low-temperature stage with NH₃ or HFC high stage
  • Heat Pumps: Excellent for hot water generation (residential, commercial, industrial)
  • Mobile AC: Under development for vehicles - Mercedes-Benz, other manufacturers
  • Vending Machines: Compact size advantage utilized
  • Ice Rinks: Secondary refrigerant in rink cooling systems

Advantages:

  • Safe operation in occupied spaces (non-toxic, non-flammable)
  • High efficiency in cold climates and heating mode
  • Simultaneous cooling and heating capability
  • Compact equipment due to high volumetric capacity
  • Minimal environmental impact from leaks
  • Low refrigerant cost and no supply concerns

Challenges and Solutions:

  • High Pressure: Requires specialized components, but mass production reducing costs
  • Warm Climate Efficiency: Transcritical operation less efficient; solved by optimized controls, ejectors, parallel compression
  • Initial Cost: 20-40% premium, but declining with scale and 10-20 year payback on energy savings
  • Technician Training: New skill set required; training programs now widely available

5.2 Hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs)

HFCs were developed as transitional replacements for CFCs and HCFCs. While they have zero ODP (no chlorine), they possess significant global warming potential, leading to the Kigali Amendment phase-down schedule.

5.3 Hydrofluoroolefins (HFOs)

HFOs represent the fourth generation of synthetic refrigerants, designed to combine zero ODP with ultra-low GWP through the use of carbon-carbon double bonds that accelerate atmospheric breakdown.

Refrigerant Chemical Name ODP GWP Safety Applications
R-1234yf 2,3,3,3-Tetrafluoropropene 0 4 A2L (mildly flammable) Automotive AC (standard in new vehicles globally)
R-1234ze(E) Trans-1,3,3,3-tetrafluoropropene 0 <1 A2L Chillers, heat pumps, foam blowing, aerosols
R-1233zd(E) Trans-1-chloro-3,3,3-trifluoropropene 0.00024 4.7 A1 (non-flammable) Centrifugal chillers (R-123 replacement)
R-1336mzz(Z) Cis-1,1,1,4,4,4-hexafluoro-2-butene 0 2 A1 Centrifugal chillers, ORC systems, foam
R-1224yd(Z) Cis-1-chloro-2,3,3,3-tetrafluoropropene 0.00029 <1 A1 Foam blowing agent
R-1132(E) 1,2-Difluoroethene 0 <1 A3 (flammable) Low-GWP blends, ultra-low GWP target

HFO Technology Assessment

Advantages:

  • Ultra-low GWP (typically <10 for pure HFOs, 100-700 for blends)
  • Zero ODP - no ozone depletion
  • Drop-in or near-drop-in for many existing systems (blends)
  • Generally non-flammable (A1) or mildly flammable (A2L) safety classification
  • Performance comparable to HFCs in many applications
  • Enable continued use of existing infrastructure with modifications

Challenges:

  • Cost: 3-10× more expensive than HFCs, 10-30× vs. natural refrigerants
  • Patent Restrictions: Limited number of manufacturers, IP constraints
  • Mild Flammability: A2L classification requires updated standards, safety measures
  • Decomposition Products: Trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) environmental persistence concerns under study
  • Long-term Availability: Transitional vs. permanent solution debated
  • Energy Efficiency: Often slightly lower than optimized natural refrigerant systems

Market Role:

HFOs and HFO blends serve as important transitional refrigerants, especially in applications where:

  • Retrofitting existing HFC systems is desired
  • Safety regulations limit natural refrigerant charge sizes
  • Drop-in replacement minimizes capital investment
  • Mild flammability (A2L) is acceptable but higher flammability (A3) is not

However, for new installations, many regions are increasingly favoring natural refrigerants (hydrocarbons, ammonia, CO₂) as the ultimate long-term solution due to superior environmental profile, lower cost, and no regulatory phase-out risk.

Selecting the optimal refrigerant requires balancing multiple factors including environmental impact, safety, performance, economics, and regulatory compliance.

The transition to ozone-safe and climate-friendly refrigerants is well underway. Natural refrigerants (hydrocarbons, ammonia, CO₂) represent the ultimate solution with zero ODP and negligible GWP, while HFOs and low-GWP HFCs serve as transitional options for applications with specific constraints. The WIA-ENE-068 standard supports informed refrigerant selection through comprehensive assessment tools and best practice guidelines.
